# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Surge XT's effects on any part** (`mix.surge_fx`): a chain of one to four of nineteen types (two reverbs and a spring, chorus, ensemble, flanger, phaser, rotary speaker, distortion, waveshaper, exciter, Bonsai, delay, and the granular Nimbus, frequency shifter, ring modulator, resonator, combulator and Tape) or of the 75 Airwindows algorithms inside Surge XT Effects (`{"type": "airwindows", "algorithm": "ToTape6", ...}`, by their Airwindows names, their knobs set by position and their defaults their sources' own), after the part's compressor and before its width and pan, each type's parameters named, with their units, limits and defaults, in [surge-fx.json](skills/music-production/references/surge-fx.json); a name or value outside them is refused before the render. Every parameter is set on each render, the project's value or the table's default, so nothing depends on the plugin's state before it; times are absolute. After the chain a 5 Hz high-pass removes any DC offset, and the part is brought back to the level it entered the chain at, so `level_dbfs` stays its level. The stem receipt gives each parameter as the plugin showed it and how far the chain had moved the level; the render's summary names a chain that moved it 12 dB or more. The headphone version runs a part's chain again, and where it did otherwise there, the receipt, the report and the summary say so. The effects run in `scripts/surgefx_host.py`, a GPL-3.0-or-later program of their own, as Surge XT's instruments do. The setup installs Surge XT Effects with Surge XT, from the same pinned plug-in-only archive into a folder of its own, its binary checked by its own hash (beside a Surge XT given with `--surge-vst3` or installed, a Surge XT Effects found there); `doctor` says whether it is installed. Without it, or with a changed file, a part plays without its chain, and its receipt, `report.json` and the render's summary say so, also when its stem comes from the cache. The host's report is checked against the chain asked (the type, the algorithm, every control and the value it shows): a plugin that reads otherwise is a failed run, never a receipt.
- **Effects that render otherwise each time are offered and marked**: Tape's degrade, the combulator's extra noise, the spring reverb's chaos and knock, the flanger's and phaser's noise waveforms (`varies_when` in the table); Tape otherwise agrees to within −120 dBFS after 4 s of silence, the other effects to the bit. Such a chain runs once for the part's audio into it and its output is kept in the part's cache entry, so the master, the headphone version and later renders play one take until the part changes; the receipt gives `repeat`, `varies` and `take_reused`, and the summary names the chains set to vary.
- **A processing catalog and `studio.py effects`**: 115 tools (the skill's own mix fields and effects, an auto-pan, the kit's gated reverb, the nineteen types and the 75 algorithms), each with its kinds, a description in this project's words, its roles, where it goes, what it needs, a fragment that validates, what to watch, a fallback of the skill's own, its measures at its defaults and its sources ([processing-catalog.json](skills/music-production/references/processing-catalog.json)). The command gives a short list for one part by role, style, kind and character, the style's documented tools first, the piece's seed choosing among the rest kind by kind, always closing with "nothing"; `--describe` gives one tool in full.
- **The style maps document treatments by role** (`treatments` in style-maps.json): 90 entries over the electronic, pop, rock, beats, jazz, orchestral and horror families, each a kind of treatment a published source documents for a role, how usual the source says it is (usual, often or documented), what it was applied to and its source, every one checked against its page.
- **A step for the treatment** (SKILL.md, step 6): each part's processing decided once, before the first round, with its reason in `decisions`; `review` describes each part's tools beside what its style's map documents for its role, and names a part with three tools or more that change its character. A question about a missing treatment is built but asked of no style yet: tried on 28 agent pieces, it asked about an electric piano's tremolo of an acoustic piano and strings.
- **`start --part` names a drum machine**: `drum_synth`, `drum_synth:808` or `drum_synth:909`, with no configuration, as the electronic guides use it (it could be set only by editing `compose.py`).
- **`compare` names an edit inside a list**, `surge_fx[1].params.output_mix 20 -> 40`, where two chains alike in their first characters were shown as the same shortened text, and names a part that played a varying chain anew.
- **FFmpeg's mixing tools as fields of a part's mix**: an `eq` band as an object, a peak, a low or high shelf with its gain and Q, or a notch, beside the `[Hz, dB]` pairs; the compressor's `knee_db`, `makeup_db`, `mix` (parallel compression below 1) and `detection` (RMS or peak); a `noise_gate`, first among a part's processors, FFmpeg's agate at a ratio of 10 (`{}` is the gate with its defaults); and the effects `tremolo` and `flanger`, each type with limits of its own. A project without them renders as before.
- **`saturate` takes a curve**: `{"drive", "curve"}` with `atan`, `alg`, `tanh` or `erf`, from the softest knee to the firmest, on the same path as the number, at four times the rate with the level kept; `{}` is drive 0.5 and tanh, and a drive of 0 is no saturation in either form. A number is tanh as before, to the bit.
- **`review` counts each accompaniment part's bar rhythms and chords** (`views.accompaniment`: how many sets of attack positions its bars have, its chord shapes and kinds, the commonest's share, by section; the summary prints the rhythm and harmony parts), and asks `unchanging:<part>` where a rhythm or harmony part plays a chorus as a verse or build of its own approach was (the same bar rhythm in 90% of the section's bars in each, a bar it rests in counting against it, chords of the same kind in any inversion, about the same register and note lengths), or keeps one rhythm and shape through every section of its stretch, of two kinds of section or more. It is asked only where the style governing the form says that its sections differ (the pop, J-pop and rock families, after Summach and Koozin), and not where `repetition` or `static_texture` already asks about the part; a decision answers it. Read on the 25 pieces of the 0.9.1 gallery and three more, it was asked of 5 of their 126 rhythm and harmony parts: a rhythm guitar in each of the four J-pop and J-rock band pieces, and a ballad's piano.
- **[processing.md](skills/music-production/references/processing.md)**, a guide to a part's gate, EQ, dynamics and effects: choosing a part's treatment, the order a part goes through, correcting by role, character by style, which tool for which effect, each Surge XT Effects type and the Airwindows algorithms and what to watch, effects that render otherwise each time, and what the render says, every practice with its source.
- The band guides: in [anime-opening.md](skills/music-production/references/anime-opening.md) and [rock-and-metal.md](skills/music-production/references/rock-and-metal.md) the guitars' tone goes with their voicing, the drums' patterns change with the form, the band is laid out by section as ways of playing rather than rules, and the instruments are options chosen by the piece's own idea; a sound or treatment the piece's words choose is recorded in `association.gives`, its reason in `decisions`.

### Changed

- **Every part is synthesized again once** after the upgrade. The code a take is made from moved: the clock, the notes as played and their controllers from `model.py` into `played.py`, a noise part's vinyl from `production.py` into `vinyl.py`, so that changing what a project may say about its mix no longer remakes every take. The takes FluidSynth, the built-in synths and the noise engine make are the same audio as 0.10.0's: rendered again, the 13 projects of our safety net gave every master, stem, bus and return the same samples as the reference run that 0.10.0 also matched (made with 0.9.3's code and FluidSynth 2.6.3), their Surge takes replayed from it. A Surge part comes out with other oscillator phases, as any new synthesis of it does.
- `review` counts a section named `sabi`, the chorus as Japanese songwriting names it, as an arrival, as it counts `chorus`: `arrivals_thinner` now asks of a J-pop chorus named so.
- The surf entry plays its wet spring reverb on Surge XT's (`spring_reverb`) where Surge XT Effects is installed; the plate stands in elsewhere.
- The setup guide says what `--system` downloads (the default bank, and unless `--no-surge`, Surge XT, its effects, pedalboard and the patches, as without it); the READMEs count thirty-one banks, six of them repacked from FreePats, as the release holds.

### Fixed

- Text a command prints from a project or a file escapes zero-width characters, the byte-order mark and the tag characters, which could hide text in a printed line; the zero-width joiner and non-joiner, which emoji and several scripts need, are kept, and no project is refused for them.

## [0.10.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added

- **What a held note does, for every Surge patch.** Three more playings of the catalog's probe note measure whether a patch dies away while the note is held, whether it darkens or brightens on the way, whether its pitch blips at the onset or steps by itself, whether its level, pitch or brightness swings at a rate it repeats, and how much of its power lies away from its harmonics; the words `decays`, `darkens`, `brightens`, `pitch blip`, `pitch steps`, `level swings`, `pitch swings`, `brightness swings` and `inharmonic` join each patch's character (593, 155, 92, 253, 40, 429, 214, 239 and 30 patches of the 2,997 measured; [sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md) gives how many each figure could be read for), and the figures its entry. Nothing the catalog held changes, and the catalog's older words find with `--character` what they found in 0.9.3. `studio.py sounds --without` leaves out patches with any word given, and those not known to lack it: its figure could not be read, or a swing reaches the line with no rate read (`--without "decays,level swings"` for a pad that holds still); `--character` asks for the new words; `sounds --id <id>` gives a patch's figures. Each figure is the median of the playings that read it, two or three (the inharmonic share only where they fall on the same side of its line), and a patch near a line still falls either side of it from one set of playings to the next. One note held 1.2 s cannot tell a swing slower than about one and a half times a second from a lasting change, so such a patch may read as `decays`, `darkens` or `brightens`, or as none ([sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md) says what each figure can and cannot tell).
- **`studio.py start` names any sound a part plays**, as `audition --sounds` writes it: `--part "pad harmony surge:<id>"`, `"lead melody synth:<preset>"`, `"strings harmony soundfont:<bank>:<n>:<program>"`, beside the words that find a SoundFont preset. A Surge or synth part needs no configuration. A name that is no engine's sound, or a SoundFont sound whose bank is not configured, is looked up as the words that find a configured preset, as 0.9.3 looked it up, so every part 0.9.3 took is taken; where neither finds it, it is refused before anything is written, with the nearest names. A sound written in one of the forms plays that engine's sound even where a configured preset bears that name.
- **`review` asks when a sound and its notes disagree:** `sound_attack` where a quarter or more of the spans a part's sound is started in end before it reaches half its level, and `sound_decay` where a part in a sustained role (harmony, texture, pad), for half or more of the time it sounds (each moment once, however many notes sound in it), sounds in spans held past 1.2 s and plays a sound that dies away within that. A span is a note, or a voice as the engine plays it: a mono synth's notes that meet, an arpeggio's chord, a Surge part's notes that overlap with `glide_ms`. A built-in preset is read from its envelope as the part sets it, a Surge patch from the catalog (with `swell`, which sets its attacks, or with macros or `velocity_to_cutoff`, which play it otherwise than at the saved settings the catalog measured, or with its mod wheel (CC1) moved on a patch whose wheel moves anything, as the catalog measured it with the wheel at rest, or on a patch whose envelopes a note's velocity, its key or another source that differs from note to note moves (the catalog's `per_note`, read from each patch's file: 657 of the 3,008 patches, 17 of the 50 curated ones), as it measured one note at velocity 100, neither question is asked; a Surge part's note let go while its sustain pedal is down is held on by Surge to an end review does not know, so it counts for neither), and a Surge part from its fallback where the configuration given does not let Surge XT play it. A decision answers each, as for every question. Read on the 195 Surge and synth parts of the 13 projects the safety net renders and 123 recent project files, `sound_attack` was asked of three, one bass part in three copies of one project, its patch reaching half its level in 0.43 s while most of its notes end sooner; `sound_decay` of none.

- **A later session finds the configuration.** `studio.py round`, `render`, `audition`, `instruments`, `video`, `doctor` and `start` take the file the environment variable `MUSIC_SKILL_CONFIG` names when `--config` is left out, and say so on stderr; the variable must hold an absolute path (a relative one would be read from each command's folder), a `--config` given wins, and `review`, `analyze` and `compare` still use a configuration only when it is named. `setup.md`'s commands write the configuration into `~/.music-skill/`, and after saving it the setup prints a line saying how to record it for later sessions. Step 0 of `SKILL.md` looks in those two places and nowhere else before asking, and after a setup offers to record the configuration in the user's agent instructions or in `MUSIC_SKILL_CONFIG`, with their agreement. `--config`, the variable and the setup's paths read a leading `~/` as the home folder, in PowerShell too. The selfcheck runs its commands without the variable, as it runs without a configuration unless `--config` names one.

### Changed

- **FluidSynth 2.6.3** is the build the setup pins on Windows (it was 2.6.1). It holds the fix for CVE-2026-111123 and for an overflow when playing a crafted MIDI file, and it changes some details of playback (sostenuto, voices ended after the polyphony is reduced, an LFO's direction), so renders differ slightly from 0.9.3's: of the 13 projects the safety net renders, 7 changed, their masters' difference lying 23 to 55 dB under the music (our measurement). A setup made before keeps its FluidSynth until the setup is run again. Run again, it changes the cache key of every part, since each names the FluidSynth build: the next render of a piece synthesizes every part again, and a Surge part comes out with other oscillator phases, though FluidSynth plays no note of it; run it between pieces, not during one whose Surge parts must stay as they are. The check that refuses a SoundFont FluidSynth is not safe with stays, for configurations that name an older FluidSynth, such as a system's. The READMEs name it, and the before-after example gives its figures with it.
- Pull requests to the documents and translations are open. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) says what is open for now and how a pull request goes, and a template asks what a pull request changes and how it was checked. A fix in the code starts with an issue.
- The repository's tests: each README must link every other one with a link a reader can follow (inline, reference or HTML, outside code); naming it in plain text passed before.

### Fixed

- `studio.py start` writes the piece's first `song.json` itself, from the skeleton it writes into `compose.py`, instead of running `compose.py` in the piece's folder, where Python imported a module file it found in that folder before the skill's own; the project is the same.
- **A composition script imports nothing from its own folder.** The `compose.py` that `studio.py start` writes takes its folder off Python's import path, as it is and where a link to it leads, before it imports anything, so a file there named as a library (`json.py`, `pathlib.py`) does not run in the library's place. SKILL.md has a script of the agent's own that reads a project from elsewhere kept and run outside that project's folder, the Python files beside a `compose.py` that came with a project listed before it is run, and such a piece rendered with `--cache` naming a new folder, as a cache that came with it can hold any audio.
- `studio.py words` tells a record it finds in the folder as its record has it ("given by the user, its record says"), as one that came with a piece from elsewhere is data, and prints at most 24 words of 60 characters, as `--own` takes them.
- A command whose configuration lies in the folder of the project, piece, render or round it works on (the two renders `compare` takes among them) says so in a WARNING line on stderr: a configuration names the programs that run. The examples in `studio.py`'s help name `~/.music-skill/local-config.json`, where setup.md writes it, not a `config.json` in the working folder.
- The setup runs pip in a new empty folder, not the caller's, and finds system programs (`--system`, and tar or 7-Zip for a FreePats bank) on PATH without the working folder, which Python on Windows searched first before 3.12, unless PATH names it (an empty entry names it, as `which` reads one); the 7-Zip fallback looks in Program Files only where the environment names that folder.
- `studio.py video` reads the render's master as WAV and from a file alone, as every other input it gives FFmpeg.
- The READMEs say that the setup fetches the pedalboard wheel from PyPI's file host (files.pythonhosted.org) itself and has pip install it offline, as SECURITY.md and PRIVACY.md say; they had it coming through pip's package index since 0.4.0. A repository test checks that each README names every host the setup's downloaders may reach.
- **The Surge catalog measures a patch built an octave from its key where a part plays it.** Renders play such a patch (1,175 with a `transpose` of +12, 149 with -12) with its notes moved by an octave, so that the written pitch sounds; the catalog had measured it at the written note, an octave away, so that its brightness, attack, tail, width and held-note words described a sound no part plays (the brightness word was wrong for about half of them). They are measured again where a part plays them: 1,054 of the 1,323 measured have other words, and `sounds` finds, orders and draws its shortlists by the new figures, so the same query can list other patches than in 0.9.3. The counts in [sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md) are the catalog's after the change. Where Surge XT is missing, a catalog bass part that names no fallback of its own plays the built-in `sub_bass` when the patch's centroid is under 120 Hz and `synthwave_bass` otherwise, so on 59 bass patches such a part now falls back to the other one (37 to `sub_bass`, 22 to `synthwave_bass`), and where Surge XT plays them it is synthesized again once, as its take's cache key names the fallback.
- The guides' searches for Surge's electric pianos also give `e-piano` and `electric`, which find the electric pianos those names miss (`glassy_e_piano`, `fm_e_piano_1_3_5`, `fm_e_piano_four_high`, `fm_electric_piano`).
- [electronic-sounds.md](skills/music-production/references/electronic-sounds.md) said a Surge patch in `audition --sounds` "takes the part's Surge settings after it", which read as if a candidate played with the part's glide and the like; it plays without them unless they are written after it, as it now says.
- `setup.md` said `doctor` re-hashes every configured file; it re-hashes FluidSynth, FFmpeg and every bank and checks that Surge XT and pedalboard are where the configuration says, as it now says.
- `setup.md` counts six FreePats banks and thirty-one in all, about 3.6 GB to download, and gives the `band` (1.6 GB) and `examples` (2.2 GB) groups their sizes in the lock; it said five, thirty, 3.5 GB, 1.3 GB and 2.5 GB.
- The Surge catalog holds 3,008 patches and measures 2,997 of them; the other 11 are silent on a plain note, audio-input templates and vocoders among them. `SKILL.md`, [commands.md](skills/music-production/references/commands.md) and [electronic-sounds.md](skills/music-production/references/electronic-sounds.md) had called all 3,008 measured.
- The `audition` command in [sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md) and in `review`'s questions about a built-in preset names the project, `--output` and `--config`; as written it stopped for the missing arguments.
- `audition` refuses a sound whose master still sounds at the end of the longest tail a project takes (15 s), and says what is left to do; the render's advice had asked for more seconds of tail than a project may have.
- `SKILL.md` names the commands whose summaries end with the file and keys of the full figures, and `sounds --help` the words `darkens` and `brightens` and the role `other`.

## [0.9.2] - 2026-11-23

Smaller, safer and easier to install. The same project renders to the same audio.

### Changed

- **`SKILL.md` is about 28% shorter (6,093 words to 4,386) and ordered by need.** What must hold comes first (listening, files from elsewhere, the configuration, rights), then what goes wrong without a word, the workflow and the commands of a normal piece. Rarer commands and options and what a render writes moved to the new [commands.md](skills/music-production/references/commands.md); each sentence of the old file has a place.
- **`studio.py sounds` prints one line a patch** with what tells them apart (25 patches were about 12 KB of JSON). `--json` prints everything.
- **`clean` also takes standalone `review` folders**, by the record they already keep. A folder of which nothing can go is no longer counted as removed: it is named with what stays in it. Its totals count what really went, a file another program holds open is left out of them, and folders come before cache entries in its list.
- **A part the plan names and the project does not have yet is one question** (`plan.unwritten:<parts>`), not one per section, so a score reviewed before its other parts are written shows its real differences. A decision on it covers those parts and no part named later.
- `analyze` gives a `--span` its own level, correlation and mono fold; where nothing is on the lead bus the melody stems stand in, and the melody group of a section or a span starts from the part the plan says leads it.

### Added

- **What a run could not see is said.** A command that reads a `song.json` ends with a note where the `compose.py` beside it was saved after it (a script that fails leaves the old project). A round lists the answered questions whose findings moved, since a decision goes on answering its question. `review` asks about notes that `performance.bend` keeps a quarter-tone or more off their pitch.
- The render's summary gives the MP3's true peak, a loop seam that may click, where the master's highest sample lies when the true peak holds a wide master's gain, and an end of three seconds or more under -60 dBFS.
- `voice_lead(..., thin=True)` voices a chord in fewer voices than its essential tones: the third, then the seventh. Without it such a chord is still refused.
- The chords `9#11`, `maj9#11`, `maj13#11`, `6/9#11`, `13sus` and `13sus4`. `song.part(..., engine='additive')` takes that engine's `instrument` settings.

### Fixed

- **`clean` keeps a take that a kept render plays from a cache inside a folder it removes**, an audition's or an earlier render's. The take went with that folder, and a Surge take cannot be made again, so the next render of that piece would have sounded different.
- **`clean` takes cache entries only from the kept piece's own cache, and none that a render that stays still plays.** Run on a folder holding two pieces that share a part, it deleted the other piece's takes while listing that piece as left alone. A cache it cannot show to be this piece's stays, and is named. A cache inside a folder that goes follows the same rules: no entry goes with the folder holding it.
- `clean` stops, removing nothing, where a kept render's `stem_receipts.json` does not name the cache key of every part of its project. A lost row, or a receipt saved again by another program, read as a render that does not use that take.

### Security

- **A SoundFont with a modulator destination past FluidSynth's 63 generators is refused before FluidSynth gets it** (CVE-2026-111123, in the pinned 2.6.1; the same destination bound as FluidSynth's own fix, released in 2.6.3), in its preset, instrument and default modulators alike. `add-soundfont`, `configure` and `doctor` name the bank, and a render or an audition stops before any program runs. None of the banks the setup installs has one. A bank that does not hold exactly what its RIFF header says is refused the same way, since the check and FluidSynth could otherwise read different tables.
- SKILL.md now says that a `compose.py` that came with someone else's project is a program: read it, and do not run it unasked.

### Install

- The READMEs give the Codex CLI's plugin route, and how to update on each of the three routes.
- `doctor` says whether Pillow is there for `still` and `video`, and whether it is new enough.

### Documentation

- Corrected against the code and the banks: the default bank's brush and marching kits and their keys, a slur's `bend_range`, crossfade with portamento, how dynamic layers are spaced, what `reverse` does on a sound that does not decay, the organ pedal's strongest partial, the steel drum's and vibraphone's off-pitch takes, the tail after a crash, what to ask of a wide game mix, how much `level_dbfs` moves when a part's loudest notes change, the keys `outputs.md` names, and which instruments the default bank holds (bandoneon, accordion, bagpipes, banjo, mandolin, shakuhachi, shamisen, timbales), which the style maps said it lacked.
- `outputs.md` gives the keys of `profile`, and says where a loop's folded tail is measured. The ordered commands show `clean`'s list before its removal.

## [0.9.0] - 2026-11-20

An electric guitar and bass played by string through an amplifier, parts that move across the stereo image, a headphone version of a mix, and a review that asks fewer questions it has no business asking.

### Added

- **A guitar amplifier for a part: `mix.amp`.** `{"voice": "crunch", "drive": 0.5}` plays a part through an amplifier and loudspeaker: the voices `clean`, `edge`, `crunch`, `lead` and `high_gain` for a guitar and `bass` for a bass guitar, `drive` from 0 to 1. It runs on the part's whole dry sound, so the notes of a chord pass one set of curves together, as strings into one amplifier do. It is made for a bank recorded from a pickup, and the setup's band group gains one: `fsbs_direct`, the FreePats FSBS guitar's direct recording (CC0, 67 MB). The levels of three bass banks into the amplifier are measured too. The voices' settings are this project's starting values ([schema.md](skills/music-production/references/schema.md), [rock-and-metal.md](skills/music-production/references/rock-and-metal.md)).
- **A part played by string.** `strings` on a part gives its open strings; every note then names its `string`, and a string sounds one note at a time while the others ring on, as on a guitar or a bass. Notes take `"mute": "palm"` and `"dead"`; a strum crosses the strings in order. The builder's `part.fret(...)` and `part.shape(...)` write notes by string and fret. `review` prints one line for such a part: the notes on each string, the frets used, the mutes, and the widest stretch between stopped notes that sound together.
- **A part that moves across the stereo image: `mix.move`.** A `path` of places by beat, or a `swing` to and fro around the part's pan with a period in beats. The stems, the buses and the master follow it; in a loop, a part that sounds across the loop point stands in the same place at both ends (within 0.05 of pan).
- **A headphone version of a mix: `mix.headphones`.** A bus-chain render also writes `headphones.wav` and `headphones.mp3`: the same piece with each part at a direction around the listener's head, made with measured responses of a dummy head (the SADIE II database, shipped with the skill under its licence). A part stands where its `pan` puts it and follows its `move`; `mix.around` places it anywhere around the head. The master and the stems are the files they are without it. Not for a looped piece.
- **`song.harmony`, `song.changes` and `song.chord_at` in the builder.** A composition script writes the chords once, by bar and beat counted from 1, where `review` reads the pitched parts' notes against them, and every part reads the same list back. `studio.py start`'s skeleton shows the three.
- **`analyze` names the parts that carry the top of the mix.** `top_end` in analysis.json and one line of the summary: each part's share of the stems' power from 6 to 16 kHz, with a low-pass under the band's top and the amplifier voice it plays through beside it, and the drum parts' share. A description with no target.
- **A guide to writing the electric guitar** ([rock-and-metal.md](skills/music-production/references/rock-and-metal.md), Writing the guitar part): riffs and how they repeat, power chords in standard tuning and in drop D, octaves, sixteenth-note strumming with dead strokes, chords left to ring, the lead, each from a source that was read. The rock and J-pop style maps rest on published studies where their rows rested on encyclopedia articles.

### Changed

- **`review` counts the minor second and the minor ninth between parts, as its guides say.** It counted the semitone at every octave; the same clash two octaves or more apart is now given apart, not counted and not asked about. A clash that begins on a downbeat or a half-bar and sounds a beat or more is asked by itself, `clash:<part>.<note>+<part>.<note>`, where a piece has five or fewer: a decision about the count no longer hides one that comes later.
- **`review` asks less where nothing is wrong.** Octaves inside a run of four attacks or more that two parts share one after another, all at the octave or unison, are a doubling, not parallels. A Surge XT patch that the catalog measured without a clear pitch (a noise sweep, a reverse crash) is left out of the pitch checks. The question about block chords no longer advises breaking the chord, which leaves its count as it is, and says so.
- **`review` asks about a drum part levelled by its peak.** A kit, hats or a shaker with `level_mode: "peak"` can come out far too low, one hard hit taking the level (15 to 30 dB in the pieces measured): a drum part written so that plays more than once a bar is asked about once, answered by a decision. The guide says what `peak` suits.
- **A draft render passes with a tail that still sounds.** `tail_quiet` failed a draft, and a round with it, where a cymbal rang past the end. A draft now passes and says what delivery needs (`delivery_needs`), with how loud the end is, how fast it falls and, where it falls fast enough, an estimate of the tail it needs; a full render fails as before, with the same figures.
- **The paths below a piece's folder are shorter.** The cache and the files of a render, a round and an audition keep short names, so that a piece in a deep folder (a session's temporary workspace on Windows) renders and makes its video ([setup.md](skills/music-production/references/setup.md)).
- **`studio.py video` keeps to the size asked by itself.** An encode that comes out over the size is made again at a lower rate, twice at most, before the command refuses.
- **`studio.py instruments` finds more.** A word of the form `0:41` is a bank number and program; where no preset has every word and each word is some preset's, those that have the most are listed with the words each has; a refusal points to the synthesizers' own lists. The builder takes only a preset that has every word.
- **A tempo point inside a ritardando keeps the ritardando.** A written tempo change that fell between two steps of a `clock.rubato` curve put the written tempo back for a fraction of a beat; it now changes the tempo that is slowed. A ritardando shorter than an eighth of a beat no longer slows past its own ratio. A passage cut by `excerpt.py` keeps a tempo point on its last beat.
- **A draft taken up over a full render removes that render's delivery files** (`master.flac`, a loop's Ogg copy and layers, as that render's report lists them), which would lie beside a newer master, and says which; it stops first where one cannot be removed.
- **`compare` says where a changed part came from.** A part whose audio differs and that the later render took from the cache is listed as `changed_from_cache`, not as synthesized again; `reused` lists the parts taken from the cache unchanged.
- **Thirty-one banks.** The setup's count of banks and its download size include the direct guitar.

### Fixed

- **A pass that fails leaves no scratch file.** A chorus's intermediate stem, a loud delivery's clipped and limited mixes, a loop's measuring copy and a loop layer's premix stayed in the render's folder when the FFmpeg pass after them failed.
- **A loop's report counts the notes that sound at its end.** A stroke whose timing puts it after the loop's end was counted, and two hits that a part's `key_map` moves onto one key were counted as two. A loop whose only tempo change lies on its last beat has a tempo in `loop.json`.
- **The tempo map is laid at exact beats.** A tempo change on a triplet's beat at the end of a ritardando kept the tempo before it, or the ritardando's last, for the rest of the piece; a ritardando span that had ended by the first beat slowed the whole piece.
- **A mix keeps its parts to its last sample on FFmpeg 6.** With FFmpeg 6 (the system FFmpeg of Ubuntu 24.04) the last few hundredths of a second of a mix could hold its reverb's return alone: the parts ended a block before the return did, and the mixer let go of their last block. Every input of the last mix is padded before it is added, and the sum cut to the piece's length.
- **A passage keeps the chord in force at its cut.** `excerpt.py` kept only the chords that begin in the passage, so its first notes were read against no chord.
- **`review` names the chord that sounds.** A chord written with `transpose_semitones` was judged as transposed and named by its written symbol.
- **A duck's `keys` is checked.** One number in place of a list passed `validate` and stopped the render with a traceback.
- **A loop's report measures every lap of its tail.** `folded_tail_lufs` read the tail's first second alone: in a short loop, an echo that comes round a lap later was reported as silence.
- **A part played backwards is read by its written spans** in the seam's count and notes.csv, as it is rendered: a reversed note at a short gate was ended early in both.
- **A summary's last line names what was written.** A review of a score without a note pointed to a `notes.csv` it had not written (the file is now written with its header alone); a render through the room chain pointed to figures that only the bus chain's report holds.
- **A composition script prints a name its console cannot encode.** A script that writes a song with the builder prints such a character as an escape (a chord quality's sign on a Traditional Chinese Windows console stopped it).
- **A stem too faint to measure no longer stops the level meter.** A part whose only audible moment was shorter than half a second ended in a traceback; the render now names the part as silent.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-11

A true-peak limiter in the master, a lead that stays near where it is written, and a timing line after how players keep to a beat.

### Added

- **The mix's rules of version 2** (`mix.rules: 2` on the bus chain; [performance-and-mix.md](skills/music-production/references/performance-and-mix.md#the-rules-of-version-2-mixrules-2)). A project without the key stays on version 1 and is mixed as it was. None of what version 2 changes is set from a measurement of the piece's audio: each takes what it needs from the project file. What version 1 measures, version 2 still does (each part to its written level, the mix to its loudness target, a wide master's `range_lu`).
  - The lead bus's compressor takes its threshold from the lead's written level: 6 dB over the loudest lead part, with 0.4 dB of make-up. Under version 1 one threshold served every piece, so the compressor took between 1.2 and 8.9 dB from the lead bus's loudness across 27 test projects and the lead bus came out between 4.8 dB under and 2.8 dB over the sum of its parts. The same projects rendered under version 2: of the 21 whose lead bus compresses, the bus ends within 0.4 dB of the sum of its parts on 20, and 1.1 dB under on one, a bell line whose peaks stand far over its level. These are this skill's test projects, not a bound. The lead is compressed less than under version 1, which is a difference in sound and is to be heard.
  - One limiter on true peaks in the master (`musiclib/limiter.py`, after Hämäläinen 2002), with no delay. Pushed 6 dB into limiting it leaves a 50 Hz tone's harmonics 115 dB down where FFmpeg's limiter as version 1 sets it leaves them 37 dB down, and it reaches the true-peak ceiling in one pass.
  - The drum, bass and rhythm buses saturate at four times the sample rate, so far less of the curve's harmonics folds back into the band: under three drum buses the folded part went from 53–80 dB down to 100 dB down or lower.
- **How near an orchestra is heard** (`mix.c80_db`, with `mix.rules: 2`; [orchestration.md](skills/music-production/references/orchestration.md#how-near-the-orchestra-is-heard)). The skill's orchestral pieces were mixed far clearer than a hall is: the response such a mix gives its parts has a clarity (C80) of about +16 dB, where −2 to +2 dB is recommended for concert halls (Barron 2013). A piece now asks for a clarity and the hall's return moves to give it; the guide says which way to lean by the piece and where it is heard, since listeners themselves divide between an intimate sound and an enveloping one (Lokki et al. 2012). The clarity is worked out from the project file (each part's written level, the time its notes sound and its send) and from the spaces' impulse responses in the octaves of 500, 1000 and 2000 Hz, as the index is defined, so it is the same before a note is rendered and changes only with what is written; it compares the balance of early and late sound with a hall's and says nothing else of a hall. `analyze` reports the mix's C80 beside the range for halls.
- **A sound chosen by number.** `studio.py sounds --centroid 600-900 --attack 0.1-0.3` takes ranges of a patch's measured brightness and attack. The Surge XT catalog now measures every patch three times and keeps the medians, and calls a patch steady or moving by how far its brightness differed between the playings (1,144 of 2,997 moved by more than 10%). Each patch keeps the octave it is played at.
- **`analyze` reports the reverb**: `reverb` holds the spaces' early decay time and the mix's clarity, worked out from the project and the spaces' impulse responses ([measuring.md](skills/music-production/references/measuring.md)).

### Changed

- **The timing line follows how players keep to a beat** ([performance-and-mix.md](skills/music-production/references/performance-and-mix.md#player-like-deviation-musiclibexpression)). `expression.apply` now moves each part the way the research on keeping time describes it: by the next beat a player takes back a share of the distance from the beat, and a new small error comes on top (linear phase correction: Repp 2005). The share is 0.4, inside the range in which timing varies least (van der Steen and Keller 2013); a part's profile sets another with `correction`. Each onset also has a small delay of its own (Wing and Kristofferson 1973), and each part keeps to the score's beat by itself.
  - The size of the deviation is what it was, 2.5 ms on drums to 6 ms on a melody, and the loudness line is drawn as it was. On a steady sixteenth-note hi-hat the timing now has a standard deviation of 2.9 ms and a correlation of +0.64 between neighbouring notes, +0.45 a beat apart and +0.27 two beats apart.
  - A project file already written is not touched: its deviation is in its notes. A script run again writes another performance of the same size: other timing offsets and note lengths, and other values of the loudness line, which is drawn after them. On a sampled instrument a note near the border of two recorded dynamics can cross it, so an exposed line can sound different.
- **A drum hit takes one of its recorded takes by a draw of its own.** A drum part on a bank with several takes of a hit used to step through them in order; each hit now takes one by a draw made from the hit itself, the same on every render (`musiclib/variation.py`). A hit has the take of the hit before it on that drum and layer about one time in N, where the layer has N takes. This is the one place where a project made before 0.6 does not render sample for sample as it did: its drum parts play other takes of the same hits in the same dynamic layer, unless `performance.round_robin` is false. The velocities written in the project and in the exported MIDI are unchanged; the draw is made from the hit's id, its key and where it falls, so a hit that is moved draws again.
- **The four examples are made again** from their scripts with the new timing line: the same notes, harmony, sounds and mix in another performance. Among the notes of the parts that `expression.apply` shapes, the timing moved by a median of about 4 ms in absolute value (3.5 to 4.8 by piece) and the velocity by 2 to 3; parts written without it, such as the fughetta's harpsichord, did not change. They do not sound note for note as the recordings of examples-2 did. Their recordings are the release examples-3.
- **The horror style map is arranged by what each device does to a listener** and rests on more sources: instability and expectation kept waiting (Lehne and Koelsch 2015; Huron 2006), the signals of alarm (Arnal et al. 2015; Blumstein et al. 2010; Trevor et al. 2020) and the startle, beside the film-music writing it had.
- `tonal-references.json` gives its seven pieces' measures without links to recordings; the measures and `analyze --reference` are unchanged.
- **Three lists are shorter.** The J-pop map gives three chord progressions as examples and names the study that counts them (Uehara et al. 2016); the guide to character words gives three words and names the study that compares the collections (Moffat et al. 2022), and a few labels of each of the five clusters of mood labels where it listed all 29 (Hu and Downie 2007).
- **The style maps and genre guides are reworded** in about 180 rows and sentences; no trait, figure or citation changed. [sources-and-rights.md](skills/music-production/references/sources-and-rights.md) says what is taken into the skill and what is only cited, and [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md) names Wikipedia and the two open textbooks among the works cited.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-10

Styles described by their traits, and work that survives a stop. 0.5.0 maps twelve families of styles in 88 entries, each trait with its source, and `review` takes its limits from the entry that governs each question; building blocks write drums from professional drummers' statistics and bass and accompaniment from named figures; `analyze` sets a master's spectral slope beside published genre means; Surge XT parts slide, take velocity on the filter and swell as the score asks; and every write is journaled, so a killed render is taken up by `--resume` and `clean` removes only what a record or the journal proves.

### Added

- **Building blocks** (`musiclib.blocks`): a drum groove, a bass line or an accompaniment written as notes to start from, from the piece's own settings ([building-blocks.md](skills/music-production/references/building-blocks.md)).
  - Drums follow Open Music Theory's three layers: the backbeat, the kick and the cymbals' pulse. What fills the layers in comes from professional drummers, feel by feel: twelve feels, from rock, soul, funk and hip-hop to Latin and the rock and jazz shuffles, measured from the [Groove MIDI Dataset](https://magenta.tensorflow.org/datasets/groove) (Gillick et al. 2019; Google LLC, CC BY 4.0). They give where each drum falls in the bar, how loud, how many notes a bar holds, how much the bars vary, and the fills with the crash after them. Complexity, intensity, fills and variation are quantiles of those drummers' distributions, and can differ per section, as Logic Pro's Drummer sets them per region.
  - Bass and accompaniment take the guides' figures (the two-feel, funk, bossa nova and walking lines, broken chords, comping, strummed chords) or a rhythm of the piece's own, written as Logic Pro's Session Player patterns mark main and probable hits. Pushes, an eighth early over a change; fills at phrase ends; a walking line through the scale tones of Open Music Theory's chord-scale table. Velocities follow MuseScore's table of dynamics. The comping answers the melody and the bass, leaving the eighths where they strike (Israels).
  - Writers given one tool's idea wrote stories more alike, and still did with five ideas to choose from (Doshi and Hauser 2024). To keep pieces from starting alike, no setting has a default, the seed comes from the piece's title, and the comping follows the piece's own melody. `blocks.candidates` sets several parts of one setting side by side, and the record names a choice that is its style's most common form. Measured at one setting across fifty pieces' seeds: fifty drum grooves; fifty rhythms from each comping figure but the steady broken chord; eight from the bass figures of fixed rhythm (root, two-feel, bossa nova, walking) through their fills and pushes over sixteen bars.
  - No number in the blocks stands in for what players do: each is the drummers', a source's or a tool's, a setting's definition, or a uniform choice by the seed ([building-blocks.md](skills/music-production/references/building-blocks.md#where-the-numbers-come-from)).
- **Style maps** ([style-maps.md](skills/music-production/references/style-maps.md)): what a style sounds like, described on seven axes (tempo, harmony, melody, rhythm, timbre, form, mix), family by family. Style names do not agree between catalogues (Pachet and Cazaly 2000: of three catalogues' genres, 70 words were common to all three), so an entry is described by its traits. A genre is a set of musical facts governed by norms, a sub-genre a subset, and a piece may lie in the intersection of genres (Fabbri 1981).
  - A project's `style` names an entry, or a primary entry with entries borrowed for some axes; each axis follows one entry, and each borrowing records its reason, as a creative combination is valued for an intelligible link (Boden 2004). `validate` refuses an entry the maps do not have.
  - `review` takes each check's limits from the entry governing the check's axis. Its record lists every limit with its origin: a source, or `ours` where the project set the number itself on its early pieces (11 of its 18 checks; the two added for brightness and tempo, below, take theirs from sources with margins of the project's own). The guides say which of the numbers they quote are the project's own. On projects without a `style`, review asks and records exactly what it did before (20 projects compared).
  - Twelve families, 88 entries: orchestral and classical (8), horror and suspense (4), electronic (16), J-pop and anime songs (5, fast electro J-pop among them), pop and ballads (5), rock and metal (14), beats (7), jazz, blues, funk and soul (9), Latin American, Caribbean and Iberian (9), Chinese (3), folk and roots (4), and the places of a role-playing game (4), whose town, overworld, dungeon and battle music differ in form and harmony (Elkins 2020) and take their sound from the game's own style. Each trait names its source, among them Music Theory Online's studies of dance music, post-rock and anime songs, the Weimar Jazz Database's measurements and corpus studies of J-pop; the 166 of 616 cells that no source read describes say so and give no advice. Each entry says what the skill plays it with and what the banks lack.
  - Tempos and section lengths measured on the [Harmonix Set](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset) (Nieto et al. 2019; annotations under the MIT licence, credited in THIRD_PARTY.md), 912 full tracks annotated by professional musicians: medians and shares by its genre labels for pop, hip-hop, rock and metal and the dance styles, and the tempo and form of disco, alternative and indie, country and reggaeton, which no source read described. In 422 pop songs, verses and choruses ran 8 bars in about half, 16 in about a fifth.
  - The tempo ranges `review` asks about say what they rest on, traced through Wikipedia's citations: Ableton's Learning Music lesson for house, techno (now 120–140), dubstep (now 135–145) and drum and bass; a production manual for trance; for the others a sentence citing a blog, a fan site or nothing.
  - Requests without an entry of their own are combined from entries: a J-pop ballad from the pop ballad with the anime opening's harmony. The anime underscore has no entry, as the sources read describe anime songs only; it is written as music for a scene.
  - Families and entries set limits only where their sources speak: the orchestral, electronic and beats families do not ask about pushes, rock does not ask about parallel fifths (power chords), the dance styles, the beats, funk, doom and a game's dungeon do not ask about a part's loop, and ambient and post-rock not about a static texture.
  - In the dance styles and the beats, `review` asks about every part repeating one block note for note only where no filter sweep, gain automation or controller curve moves the sound in that span (`processes_vary`): loops in dance music change by layers and continuous processes (Smith 2021; Garcia 2005).
- **Moods across styles** ([moods.md](skills/music-production/references/moods.md)): which cues carry happy, sad, peaceful and frightening music, from a study that varied mode, tempo, register, dynamics, articulation and timbre independently (Eerola, Friberg and Bresin 2013), with unease, elation at a peak and nostalgia from the maps. A mood moves the cues inside a style.
- **Brightness and air** ([performance-and-mix.md](skills/music-production/references/performance-and-mix.md#brightness-and-air)): what brightens a mix, by its sources: the genre's published top end, percussion (Elowsson and Friberg 2017), register and the louder recorded dynamics, the sound chosen, low-passes, the bands mixing books name (air at 10–20 or 12–20 kHz in most, as Moffat, De Man and Reiss 2022 list them), reverb, and what the chain itself does.
  - `analyze` measures Pestana et al. (2013)'s spectral slope, log magnitude on log frequency over 100 Hz–10 kHz, and sets it beside the mean of the US and UK number-one singles of the genre governing the piece's mix axis (`published_genre`): electronic −0.75, disco −0.80, hip-hop −0.81, pop −0.94, rock −0.98, soul −1.03, folk −1.18, jazz −1.29. It asks about a master more than 0.3 steeper, a margin this project chose. J-pop borrows pop's and says so; lo-fi, ambient and a game's places have none, with the reason.
  - `review` asks when most parts other than the bass are low-passed at or under 12 kHz: widespread low-pass filtering seldom helps and easily dulls a mix (Senior 2012). Lo-fi, unpolished on purpose, is not asked. The 0.4.0 agent pieces had low-passed 45 of their 163 parts there, eight pieces every part.
  - `review` asks when a piece's main tempo lies outside the range its style entry's source gives (twelve entries, from house's 115–130 BPM to smooth jazz's 90–105). In a closed range a half- or double-time reading counts as inside.
- **Character words** ([character-words.md](skills/music-production/references/character-words.md)): a quality a brief names (bright, warm, thick, spacious, bouncy, rousing, epic), grouped as research groups such words.
  - Sound: the three shared dimensions of timbre listeners used in two languages, luminance, texture and mass, with the adjectives loading on each and what was measured with them (Zacharakis, Pastiadis and Reiss 2014); the words people give to effects, the equalization that goes with warm and with thin, and the bands mixing books name, from air to fat (Moffat, De Man and Reiss 2022); the timbral attributes with models validated on listeners' ratings (Audio Commons, Pearce et al. 2018).
  - Motion: the cues measured to move listeners' judgements (articulation, tempo, syncopation, timing, register and dynamics). Feeling: perceived against felt emotion, AllMusic's mood labels in five clusters (Hu and Downie 2007), and the nine dimensions of music-evoked emotion of the Geneva Emotional Music Scale (Zentner et al. 2008, through Strauss et al. 2024).
  - No word is broken into cues unless a source does it: bouncy, epic or heroic have none in the sources read, and the guide says so.
- **Surge XT plays the 808 and the acid line as the score asks.** A Surge part takes `glide_ms`, one voice gliding only between overlapping notes over that many milliseconds without restarting its envelopes (Surge's mono single-trigger fingered portamento), and `velocity_to_cutoff`, velocity moving filter 1's cutoff in semitones; the patch is edited before it loads, read from Surge XT 1.3.4's sources, and a part without them sends it byte for byte. Where Surge is missing, the fallback keeps the slide.
  - The candidates were measured as played, with no listening ([surge-roles.json](skills/music-production/references/surge-roles.json)): two 808 patches slide in 82 ms at a setting of 80 with their notes within 8 cents; of thirteen acid patches, four take accents and slides, five accents only, four neither. A note that slides in keeps the velocity of the note the slide starts from (Surge XT's legato), so it takes no accent, and `review` names such notes. `review --config` names those patches and their settings for the built-in `bass_808` and `acid_bass`, which stay the fallback, and `audition` plays them with those settings (`surge:<patch>+glide_ms=80`).
  - The guides follow the sources on what the parts do (the TR-808's bass drum and its slides in trap and drill; acid house's resonance, accent and slide; Roland's TB-303 manual) and say that reading an accent as a velocity opening the filter is this skill's.
  - The electric pianos measured at four velocities: six grow brighter and louder, three brighter, three only louder ([sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md)).
- **Risers, reverse cymbals and a recorded crash played backwards.** `surge.swell` sets the attacks of a Surge patch's amplitude and filter envelopes to the length of the part's notes; measured with it, a reverse crash and a dark noise riser peak at the note's end and rise by 25 to 62 dB, and four other riser patches peaked early or grew darker, so the built-in `noise_riser` stays the riser's default. A sampled part with `reverse: true` plays each note's recording backwards into the span the note sits in, so a kit's crash swells and stops on the downbeat, as tape reversal made it.
- **More building blocks** ([building-blocks.md](skills/music-production/references/building-blocks.md)): the traditional, hammer and bomb blast beats and the double kick (Wikipedia, Blast beat); a drum groove written on a step grid, voice by voice; the skank and the double skank (Wikipedia, Reggae), la pompe (Persinger 2014, *Premier Guitar*: the quick upstroke before beats 1 and 3) and the bossa nova batida (Wikipedia, Bossa nova) as accompaniment figures; `slides`, notes held into the next for a patch that glides; and `articulate`, each note's length a ratio of the time to the next onset at Eerola, Friberg and Bresin's levels.
- The selfcheck runs 148 checks (119 without a configuration); the new ones cover the building blocks, the style maps' limits, the published spectra, the low-passes, the tempo ranges, the Surge controls, the reversed notes, the journal killed after each of its lines and renames, a render killed and resumed, and cache entries cleaned by their own record.
- **`expression.apply(..., drift='time')`**: the player's slow timing and dynamics line wanders in time, on the sixteenth-note grid. A note added or removed changes only itself and the onset before it, so `compare` shows a revision where it is. Before, every note of a part moved when it gained one. The default, `drift='notes'`, keeps 0.4.0's line, so earlier scripts, the examples' included, write the projects they wrote. On a steady sixteenth-note part the two give the same statistics.

### Fixed

- **A killed render is taken up by `--resume`.** While a render works, its folder and each cache entry it writes keep a journal, `files.journal`, each line written whole: each new file's name before the file is made and again, with its file number, once it is made, and each file's size, time and number before it takes its name, as a database logs a change before it makes it (PostgreSQL, Write-Ahead Logging). A program's log is written the same way, under a new name, then given its own. A killed render so leaves only files its record or its journal proves: `--resume` removes the half-written and takes up the rest, and `clean` removes them all. A name alone proves nothing: the line before a file is made may be followed by a failure on a file someone else has there, and a file put later under a name noted made is another file, by its number. So a render killed in the instant between making a new file and noting it made leaves that file unproven, and so does a program the killed render had started when it makes its output anew after the render's own was removed: `--resume` names such a file and stops, and `clean` leaves it. Before, a render killed outright had a record of the stages it had finished only: `--resume` refused the files its last stage had begun, and `clean` left them.   - Left for 0.6: operations bound to a directory handle, for folders another account can write; work budgets for a spec's regular expressions and an audition's versions; one behaviour for a cached download that does not match; the CI's actions pinned by hash.

No bank changes.
- `audition` takes `--jobs`, the most parts rendered at once, as `render` and `round` do; it always took render's default, one part per processor up to eight. Each of six agents making pieces on one machine asked for it.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-09

Faster revisions with the same music. In the nine 0.3.0 agent sessions most of the time was the model's, and the agents opened the commands' JSON with code of their own 26 times a session, because the printed output did not answer what they had to decide. 0.4.0 changes what reaches the agent and what a render does, not how the music is made: the review's questions, the guidance and the audio are those of 0.3.0.

### Added

- **`studio.py round`**: one revision round in one call. It validates the project, reviews the score, renders a draft, analyzes it and compares it with the previous round (`--previous`), printing each step's summary as it finishes and writing `round.json` from the start, which names the step that stopped a round that failed; `--clip` adds the change old then new at one loudness, and `--json` prints round.json instead of the summaries. SKILL.md makes it the way to revise; the single commands remain for what a round does not do. Anthropic's guidance for agent tools: consolidate steps that are always chained.
- **Draft renders** (`render --draft`, and every round): the same audio, stems, buses and returns as a full render, without master.flac, the check that the MP3 decodes to the master's loudness, and a game loop's Ogg copies and layers, as REAPER's dry-run render processes everything without writing the output files. A draft's status is `DRAFT_PASS`; deliver a full render.
- **Frozen parts**: a part's cache entry keeps its processed stem, and the next render copies it while nothing it depends on has changed: its raw synthesis, its mix (not the sends and bus, which only the mixer reads), the song's length, the part a duck follows, the renderer's code, FFmpeg and NumPy. The copy is checked against its hash. A DAW plays a frozen track the same way. In our tests, moving one kick note in Sprinkle Satellite processed the kick and the six parts it ducks, and nothing else.
- **`studio.py clean`** lists the piece's render and round folders other than those kept (`--keep` the final render and the previous round), its auditions' caches and the cache entries the kept renders do not use, as Ableton Live's Cleanup does, and removes them only with `--remove`, as git clean deletes only with `-f`. It removes files, not folders: every folder a command writes records the files it wrote in `files.json` (path, size, modification time and file number), and clean removes a file only while that record lists it unchanged, as Ninja's clean tool removes only the outputs its build log names. A render, round or audition holds its folder while it runs (an operating-system lock, released however the run ends): clean leaves a folder at work alone, and a second run into it is refused at once, as Bazel serves one command at a time per output folder. A file put in such a folder, a file changed since or put back as a copy, another piece's render inside it, a folder copied elsewhere rather than moved and a folder from before 0.4.0 (no record) stay, and the report names them; a folder goes whole only when nothing else is left in it, its records last, so a clean stopped part-way is found again by the next. A cache entry goes only when it holds nothing but what renders write there, under the entry's lock. Links and junctions are never followed, on every Python the skill runs on. On the 27 GB folder of one 0.3.0 agent piece it would free 20 GB.
- **Short summaries**: `round`, `review`, `render`, `analyze`, `compare`, `audition` and `clean` print what an agent decides from, and end with the file and the keys holding the full figures; `--json` prints the whole report. The summaries hold every review question with the first places it occurs, the failed checks, the master against its target, each section's level, stereo and lead, the tonal balance against published measurements and a reference, and what changed. [outputs.md](skills/music-production/references/outputs.md) lists every key. On the nine agents' own outputs, the median summary is 1.9 k characters for a review (0.3.0 printed 1.1 k), 2.6 k for an analysis (5.5 k) and 3.2 k for a comparison (3.3 k), against 12 to 19 k for the full JSON.
- `report.json` records the seconds per stage and in FFmpeg by kind; each stem receipt, the part's synthesis and processing seconds and whether its stem was reused.

### Changed

- **Swing pairs are counted from each bar line**, as a drum machine delays the even-numbered steps of the beat (Linn 2013): every bar swings alike, and a bar holding an odd number of units (eighths in 5/8 or 7/8, quarters in 3/4) plays its last one straight. Before, pairs ran on from beat 0 across the bar lines, so in 7/8 every second downbeat played 80 ms late. Meters whose bars hold whole pairs play as before.
- `review` says what it counts as a push: an attack at most an eighth before a downbeat or half-bar that its part holds over or rests through (Longuet-Higgins and Lee 1984), in [development-and-groove.md](skills/music-production/references/development-and-groove.md).
- **`validate` finds a key struck twice at once**, or one tick apart, as written and as played, before anything renders. The error names both notes, the bar and beat and what to change; before, it came only at render time.
- **Renders are faster with the same audio.** The production delay's band, saturation, the mono low end and `compare`'s bands take their FFTs at a fast length. A stem's sample count can have a large prime factor, which made the delay the slowest step of a part. The K-weighting response is computed once per length and the master is measured once. Banks are hashed when a part first uses them, and again if the file changes. The mix's checks run while the master is made where the free memory holds both (measured: 15 float64 copies of the song together), and the MP3, FLAC and a game loop are made at the same time. On our test laptop, Sodium Lights (4:28, 14 parts) took 140 s from scratch with 0.3.0; with 0.4.0 a draft from an empty cache takes 80 s, and a full render that keeps every stem takes 52 s. Every stem not played by Surge XT matches 0.3.0's bit for bit, or within −277 dBFS for one delayed guitar, and the fughetta's master is bit for bit the same. On test signals the delay's band moved by at most −225 dBFS, saturation and the mono low end by at most −83 and −76 dBFS.
- **Guides in parts.**
  - `soundbanks.md` lists the banks; their presets, what probes measured on each bank and building a bank are in [soundbank-presets.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-presets.md), [soundbank-measurements.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-measurements.md) and [soundbank-building.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-building.md).
  - The analysis sections of `performance-and-mix.md` are [measuring.md](skills/music-production/references/measuring.md).
  - The sound design and the curated Surge XT patches of `electronic.md` are [electronic-sounds.md](skills/music-production/references/electronic-sounds.md).
  - Every reference over 100 lines starts with a table of contents (Anthropic, Skill authoring best practices). `schema.md` stays whole, with headings in its opening block, because every agent that read it used every section. No guidance was removed.
- Stems and returns stay 32-bit float WAV. FLAC holds integer samples only (Xiph), and in eight of the nine 0.3.0 pieces a stem, bus or premix passes full scale, which 24-bit FLAC would clip.
- A render no longer prints a line per part. A failed master check names the true peak, the clipped samples and the length, and a failed render names its failed checks.
- The selfcheck runs 136 checks (109 without a renderer configuration). It adds the review summary and revision rounds followed by `clean`, and checks that a draft equals the full render, that frozen stems are reused bit for bit, that a damaged or swapped one is processed again, that a send edit keeps every stem and a moved kick processes the parts ducked to it, that a cache entry is held across processes, that `clean` keeps what the tools did not make (files named like records, links and junctions, another piece's folders), that a named bank is hashed at first use, and that the mix's checks give the same render beside the master and after it; and that no write goes through a link or overwrites what is there, that the installers and `--delete-sources` keep what is not theirs, that a FLAC source, a SoundFont's tables and pedalboard are what they claim, that delivered audio carries no tags from its inputs and shared records no local paths, that 200 automation points render, that swing pairs and an excerpt's edges follow the piece, that parts wait for memory, and that every script prints a character its output cannot encode as an escape. A repository test checks that every link to a section names a heading that exists, as GitHub renders the heading.

The README no longer says how the four were picked.

### Fixed

Before release, 0.4.0 went through repeated independent code reviews and stress tests (random projects, excerpts and renders by the tens of thousands, hostile files, renders, rounds and `clean` at once on one cache) on Windows, macOS and Linux with Python 3.10 to 3.14. Each finding was reproduced, fixed and covered by a check. Listed here are the problems 0.3.0 had; those found in this release's own new features before release are not.

**Files and installs**

- **No command overwrites or writes through what is already there.** An output never takes an occupied path, a dangling link included, and is published only if nothing has taken the name meanwhile. Every file is written under a name of its own and swapped in whole, FFmpeg's and FluidSynth's outputs included, so a failed or stopped command leaves no half-written file, a hard link at the path keeps its bytes, and two runs never share a temporary name; on Windows the swap is tried again for up to a second when a virus scanner holds the new file. `soundbank.py cut` and `split` publish their notes and manifest together or not at all, `soundbank.py inspect` no longer overwrites its own input, and `build` refuses a receipt that names the bank, through a link or on a case-insensitive volume.
- **The installers keep what they did not put there.** `bootstrap.py` and `fetch_soundbanks.py` extract into a private folder and install file by file: a file at the target that the installer did not record there, a dependency receipt included, is set aside as `<name>.replaced-<time>`, never deleted, and so is a cached download that no longer matches the lock. A failed download leaves no partial file, and a configuration is written only where nothing has taken its path. `--delete-sources` deletes only files that are still the installer's own (an archive by its locked size and SHA-256, a sample by its git blob hash), never through a link or a junction, and names what it kept.
- **What an install fetches is pinned.** A bank built from a sample repository takes only the files the lock pins (each path with its git blob hash and size, `samples_sha256`): a listing that names other files, or a path that leaves the sample folder, is refused before anything is fetched, and each file is fetched no larger than its pinned size. requirements.txt names every file PyPI publishes for the pinned NumPy (2.1.2, and 2.5.4 on Python 3.14 and newer) by SHA-256, which puts pip in its hash-checking mode; install other packages with a pip command of their own. The pedalboard wheel comes from its locked address, no larger than its locked size and hashed as it arrives, and Surge XT plays only when the plugin that loads is the binary checked and pedalboard's files are those pip installed (each file's hash in the package's RECORD).
- `bootstrap.py` tries a download again after a dropped connection, a timeout, a server error (5xx or 429) or a download cut short, after 3, 6 and 12 s, as `fetch_soundbanks.py` does, and names the one that failed: one HTTP 504 from GitHub's servers stopped a whole install. A redirect that the host check refuses closes its connection.
- `selfcheck.py --work` takes only a new or empty folder.

**Files from elsewhere**

- Projects, reports, receipts, bank specs and manifests, and configurations are read through one reader that refuses a link, a file larger than its limit, JSON nested more than 64 levels (Python 3.14 parses far deeper than earlier versions) and a document of the wrong shape, naming the field. They were read through links, or stopped the command with a traceback.
- `validate` refuses a project that is not a JSON object and any field of the wrong type, naming it: a clock, a track or a `revision_history` of another type stopped the command, and a huge integer raised OverflowError. Before anything renders, it also refuses a synth patch's malformed `pwm` or `index_env`, an LFO given in both Hz and beats, notes that overlap as written in a part with slur legato (its one pitch wheel bent them together; crossfade legato plays them), a phaser rate outside FFmpeg's 0.1 to 2 Hz, and a part whose bank lacks its preset (FluidSynth put another preset in its place).
- A SoundFont whose preset or instrument tables do not hold together, as the SoundFont 2.04 specification and FluidSynth refuse them, or that is cut short inside a chunk, a game loop's chunks shorter than they count, a FLAC source without FLAC's `fLaC` marker (RFC 9639) and a WAV longer than a render can be (31 minutes) are refused: indices running back and forth made a 4 KB file expand into 8,192 zones. FFmpeg reads a FLAC source with its FLAC demuxer from local files only.
- `analyze` and `compare` validate a render folder's project and refuse one holding links, so no stem is read from wherever a link points; a render refuses an output given as a link.

**Rendering**

- **A Surge XT part plays on into the piece's tail**, as `tail_seconds` asks: its host stopped four seconds after the last note and the rest was silence, so a long release ended abruptly while the quiet-tail check passed. A release longer than the tail now fails that check, which says to lengthen the tail.
- **A render that fails or is stopped stops the programs it started**, by Ctrl+C or because a part failed: the parts still waiting do not start, those at work stop, and the render ends on the first failure and writes `report.json` with `status` FAIL, the stage it had finished, the part and the reason; `--previous`, `compare` and `analyze --reference` refuse such a render. A part whose synthesis is silent is named, with what to check.
- `--resume` refuses a folder holding links, a file the earlier render did not write, or one changed since (the user's own MIDI under a render's name).
- Delivered audio carries no tags from the files it was made from: every FFmpeg output is written with `-map_metadata -1 -map_chapters -1` (a tag added to a cached WAV reached master.wav, the MP3 and the FLAC).
- The additive synth's pitch bend rests at zero until its first point, and its bend and controllers follow the curve the MIDI holds, in beats, so the audio and the exported MIDI agree under a tempo change. A synth LFO, pulse width or arpeggio in beats follows the beats the voice plays, so a tempo change or rubato under a held note moves it as it moves the beat.
- A part's automation is applied in NumPy, sample by sample: an FFmpeg expression failed past 95 points and stepped the level every 4,096 samples (85 ms). Before its first point the part keeps the first point's level.
- A part's raw synthesis is found in the cache again only when no code it runs has changed (a vinyl noise part kept its old noise after `production.py` changed), and two renders of one piece at once no longer write one cache entry together: a part holds its entry across processes while it is made.
- Parts made at once share the memory free at the start, each holding what it was measured to need, instead of 2 GB each whatever the song; the built-in synth's filter takes a long note a chunk at a time (a two-minute pad: 3.7 GB, from 8.3 GB, the same samples), and a part that would need more memory than the machine has is refused before anything is synthesized.
- The mix reads its filtergraph from a file: on one command line, 64 parts in a deep folder passed the 32,767 characters a Windows command line holds.
- The scripts print a character their output cannot encode as an escape (`\u5586`) instead of stopping on it: on Windows, Python writes the output an agent reads through a pipe in the ANSI code page, which lacks characters some names hold. Folder and piece names may hold any characters; on Windows, setup.md asks for runtime and piece folders under 120 characters, as FluidSynth 2.6.1 does not opt in to paths over 260 characters.

**Game loops**

- Loop layers go through the mix's `mono_below_hz` and `wow_flutter`, as the full mix does: they kept the stereo lows the mix takes out, and their sum differed from the mix by 3 dB.
- `validate` refuses loop layers that split a bus which compresses or saturates its parts together (every bus but `fx`, unless `mix.dynamics` is `"wide"`), and says to keep those parts in one layer, give some another bus, or make the dynamics wide: each layer's bus acted on its own parts only, and the layers no longer added up to the mix.

**Excerpts and auditions**

- **An excerpt, and so an audition, plays a passage as the piece does.** Before, an excerpt could play a note the piece had already stopped, cut a note short, leave one out, or strike a key twice at once and fail to render.
  - A note is re-attacked at the cut only if it still sounds there, and not where its key is struck again within 100 ms as played (counting both notes' timing, the part's offset and swing); a legato overlap that only its gate carries past the cut is not re-attacked, and a note held over the cut that its gate or timing stopped is left out.
  - A note the piece plays across a cut plays the part of it the passage holds, at the timing and gate that put it on those ticks. Notes that a part's offset or timing plays before the cut start the excerpt; a note written in the next bar that they play early belongs to the next passage. Which notes the passage holds is decided on the tick grid the piece plays them on, after a change of tempo too.
  - A part held to one line (the additive lead, slur legato) stays one line in the piece's written order: the passage's own notes keep their places and lengths, and a note from before the cut ends sooner where the line leaves it no room. In other parts, a pickup that the passage strikes again on the cut is written a few ticks later, its timing keeping where it plays.
  - A linear CC ramp keeps its value at the cut and the end, the piece's ritardandi that reach into the passage are kept (`clock.rubato.spans`), a rhythmic gate keeps the piece's phase (`mix.gate.phase_beats`), and an older free-form harmony object is kept as it is.
  - Left as it is: a part offset beyond timing's ±100 ms starts a note from before the cut late, or leaves it out.
- An audition that renders a version again with a longer tail removes only its render's own files, and stops if anything else is there: it removed the whole folder. `audition --solo` plays a part ducked to another part, leaving the duck out and saying so, instead of failing every version, and an audition records what stopped it.

**Review, analysis and compare**

- `review` of a dense score no longer takes quadratic time: minor seconds and ninths and low intervals are found through an index of the notes by pitch (2,000 dense notes: 0.05 s, from 0.27 s), with the same findings on random projects. It checks the text it reads (a brief, decisions, intents, roles, note labels), and a spreadsheet formula in a note's phrase or articulation is written to notes.csv with a leading apostrophe (OWASP, CSV Injection).
- `analyze` gives no lead figures for a section where the lead rests, rather than NaN, which stopped it. It refuses two regions of one name (a `--span` label given twice, or one named as a section is): their figures were mixed, and a section's came out 19.6 dB wrong with a cut suggested on them. `round` asks before it renders.
- `compare` gives no overall level for a file that changed throughout (a rewritten harp showed −332 dB): where one gain cannot match the two files, `level_db` is null and the summary says so.
- The records meant to be shared (the comparisons, audition.json) name files relative to their folders, and a Surge fallback's reason names files, not paths; the logs keep the full paths ([outputs.md](skills/music-production/references/outputs.md) says what stays local).

**Sound banks**

- A bank build loads a take once for each way its layers cut and measure it, and its take cache keeps the measured pitch, so two layers that measure one take differently each keep their own partials. `soundbank.py cut` keeps no note of a run in which a note fails its hash, and says why. `configure` checks its SoundFont's tables, as `add-soundfont` does. The crossfade legato planner finds each next note by bisection, in N log N rather than N squared.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-07

Music that keeps changing and moves. Seven pieces that agents made with 0.2.0 were heard as not wrong, but at times dull or stiff; measured on their scores, the causes were loops held for 24 to 48 bars, choruses thinner than their pre-choruses and chord parts struck as blocks, not missing micro-timing.

Guidance from professional sources only. Every guide was checked line by line: each statement now names a source, or is marked as this skill's own measurement, an example plan or a starting value, and what had neither was removed. New guidance comes from read sources: harmony from Open Music Theory, the voice leading `review` now follows, instruments for a scene's character from studies of timbre and emotion, and the full Surge XT patch set, measured, for choosing a synth sound.

### Added

- **[development-and-groove.md](skills/music-production/references/development-and-groove.md)**, from the measurements and the literature:
  - an energy plan per section, and how much the music should change;
  - variation that keeps identity: texture, register, rhythm, harmony, melody and transitions;
  - loops that move at 8- and 16-bar phrase edges;
  - syncopation in moderation, and pushes into strong beats;
  - accompaniment that moves, and chord voicings.
- **`musiclib.voicing.voice_lead`** voices a chord progression for a keyboard, pad, string or brass part, chosen across the whole progression so the voices move as little as possible (common tones held).
  - Spacing: adjacent voices within an octave, no close interval below its low interval limit, no voice crossing, and no minor second or minor ninth between two voices.
  - `against`: the pitches other parts sound with each chord (the melody, the bass, the left hand). The voicing keeps minor seconds and ninths away from them and, where it can, leaves their pitches to them.
  - `ceilings`: keeps the voicing under the melody, chord by chord.
  - Optionally no consecutive fifths or octaves, wherever the range leaves another voicing; where it leaves none, the fewest remain. `review` lists parallels between parts that each play one line, not inside one chord part.
  - Tones left out or doubled in the order arrangers use; under a major seventh the root goes first. A chord the range cannot hold doubles another tone, then leaves one more out. A tone that would rub against the other parts wherever the range puts it is left out: under a melody on the root of a major-seventh chord, the accompaniment plays the triad. `keep_root` keeps the root where no bass part plays it (a rootless A♭maj7 in three voices is heard as C minor).

  A ii-V-I moves three semitones per change; an I-V-vi-IV loop moves five to seven, against seven to 28 as root-position blocks.
- **A bright pop piano** in [performance-and-mix.md](skills/music-production/references/performance-and-mix.md): the bundled pianos measured for brightness. The recording and the touch changed it more than EQ or compression. The two uprights and the Kawai grand measured brightest of the acoustic pianos. On the Kawai a presence EQ raised the spectral centroid 77 Hz (about 9%), a firmer touch (velocity 80 to 110) 51 Hz, a 12 kHz boost 5 Hz. Linked from the pop, ballad and anime-opening guides.
- **Writing a melody that sounds composed** in [climax-and-melody.md](skills/music-production/references/climax-and-melody.md), a method from the songwriting, music-theory and music-psychology literature:
  - the hook written first, as the motif: what gives a melody character (Perricone 2000), and the common contour with uncommon intervals or repeated notes of earworms (Jakubowski et al. 2017);
  - phrases that breathe, mostly by step, with leaps for character (Perricone 2000);
  - each section built from restatement (srdc, after Summach 2011; the sentence, Caplin 1998 and Open Music Theory);
  - contrast between sections (Summach 2011), embellishing tones placed in the beat, question-and-answer phrases (the period), and the highest note saved for the high point.

  Linked from SKILL.md and the anime-opening guide.
- **`profile` describes the melody per section**: its phrases, the longest run without a breath, leaps, the bars that restate an earlier bar's rhythm and shape, and the peak. **`review` asks** about a section of eight bars or more in which no melody bar restates another. On the release demos, sections of eight bars or more restate between one in eight and four in five of their melody bars, and none is asked about. On the 0.2.0 agent pieces it asks about the rock piece's first chorus and solo and the horror cue's music-box passage, which restate none of their eight or nine bars.
- **Surge XT is the default for synth parts.** Where Surge XT is installed, SKILL.md and the sound design of [electronic.md](skills/music-production/references/electronic.md) start a synth part from a Surge patch: one chosen from the full catalog for the part's job, or one of the curated patches. The built-in presets are the fallback, which plays by itself where Surge is missing, and the instrument for what the list lacks (chip waves, the 808, the 303, risers). With Surge installed, `review --config` asks about a built-in preset that a curated patch covers and names the patches to audition. The beats, pop and ballad, jazz and funk and anime-opening guides name Surge patches beside the built-in presets. An eighth-note melody from G4 to G5, played alone, put 65% of its power above 2 kHz on the built-in `fm_bell` and 2% on Surge's `fantasy_bell`.
- **What each Surge patch's controls move.** [surge-controls.json](skills/music-production/references/surge-controls.json), read from the patch files, lists what each curated patch's mod wheel and macros move, with the depth and the wheel position it was saved with. In many leads and pads the wheel opens the filter: `smooth_lead` by 17 semitones, `juno_60_strings` by 42. In a few (`sharpish`, `slow_poly`) it closes the filter. A CC1 value or curve on the part sets or sweeps the brightness. Measured on a passage: `smooth_lead` at full wheel doubled its spectral centroid, and `bright_pad`'s Cutoff macro raised it from 668 to 1150 Hz.
- **The full Surge XT patch set, measured.** [surge-catalog.json](skills/music-production/references/surge-catalog.json) describes all 3,008 patches of Surge XT 1.3.4, 637 from its team and 2,371 from 32 sound designers. Each was measured on one probe note: its brightness, the share of its power above 2 and 5 kHz, its attack, tail and width, whether it sounds at the written pitch, and what its mod wheel and macros move. Patches that stayed silent on a plain note, need an MPE controller, are microtonal or sound off their pitch are flagged.
  - **`studio.py sounds`** searches the catalog by role, measured character, name and designer, dark to bright, leaving flagged patches out; `--id` prints one patch in full.
  - A part plays any catalog patch by its id (`surge: {"preset": "factory/pads/bright"}`), falling back by its role where Surge or the patch is missing. Whether the patch can play is part of the part's cache key, so a fallback is rendered again once Surge or the full set is installed.

  - what puts a sound in front or behind: the timbre dimensions of McAdams et al. (1995), and depth by brightness, reverb and chorus (White 2009, *Sound On Sound*);

- **Melody on top, rhythm in the bass**, in [sound-selection.md](skills/music-production/references/sound-selection.md) and the arrangement guide: the ear follows the pitch of the highest voice best, from three months of age (Marie and Trainor 2014), and the timing of the lowest (Hove, Marie, Bruce and Trainor 2014), and picks out the top and bottom bands of a mixture of pure-tone melodies more easily than its middle (Bürgel, Mares and Siedenburg 2024).   - where the parts move as blocks: chord parts struck and released together when they are held or keep one length (syncopated stabs of varied lengths are a rhythm), and no melody or accompaniment attack pushing a strong beat (in 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 the beats are dotted).

A game-music guide covers loop seams, repetition, mood cues and scenes, layers, stingers and endings, and delivery to Godot, Unity and RPG Maker.
- **Anime openings**: a guide to TV-size band cues: the form and the minor-third modulations measured on 100 popular openings (Li 2026), the build to the chorus (Summach 2011), harmony and power chords under distortion, an instrumental lead, drums and groove (Witek et al. 2014), arrangement by section, and where a band mix gets its brightness. `musiclib.expression` strums guitar chords down and up.
- **Scene music**: a guide to music under the text and voices of visual novels and RPG scenes: tempo and level for reading (Thompson et al. 2012; Kämpfe et al. 2011), the level under voiced lines (Torcoli et al. 2019; WCAG 1.4.7) and Ren'Py's ducking, the 1–4 kHz octaves that carry speech (ANSI S3.5), a set of cues built on leitmotifs (Summers 2014), scene starting points and delivery to Ren'Py.
- **Orchestra**: a guide to writing for the symphony orchestra on the banks: instruments and ranges, balance by Rimsky-Korsakov's equivalents of strength, chord spacing and doubling, soft solos on tracks of their own, seating, the hall (Beranek 2004) and the wide master.
- **Expression on sampled instruments**: `performance.dynamics` plays each note of a sustained part on the recorded dynamic nearest a curve, the curve moving its level and its tone through a filter learned from the bank's own layers (Fabiani 2009), or, with `dynamics_mode: "crossfade"`, crossfades renders of every recorded dynamic along it, with equal-power curves corrected for the renders' correlation (Fink, Holters and Zölzer 2016); one take per note is the default, since neighbouring dynamics of a note agree in phase only by chance and comb-filter when held together; crossfade legato joins a single line from presets that enter each take past its attack, as SFZ legato regions do; `mix.depth_m` stands a part on a modelled stage, with early reflections from image sources (Allen and Berkley 1979) and more hall with depth; `mix.range_lu` brings a wide master's loudness range down by lifting its quieter passages. The `vsco`, `vsco_extra` and `cello_bigcat` banks carry the dynamic-layer and legato presets.
- **Organ**: a guide to the pipe organ: registrations, manuals and pedal, phrasing through timing rather than dynamics (Gingras et al. 2010, 2013), and the church it sounds in (Mapp 2019; Girón et al. 2017, 2020).
- **Building banks**: `soundbank.py build` turns WAV, FLAC or AIFF sample sets into SF2 instruments from a JSON spec: pitch checked against file names, levels matched, round robins spread over neighbouring velocities. Kits can mix several microphones per hit, keep the recorded balance between pieces, choke an open hi-hat and replace the synthesizer's velocity curves. A pitch reading an octave off, from a tracker that locked onto a harmonic, can count as the named note. Sustained instruments keep each recording's own loop, moved back and crossfaded where a take sags at its end; takes recorded without one get a loop cut from their steady part, clear of noises. Uneven takes can be evened against their nearest neighbours, keeping the register's slope. A straight velocity line and a delayed vibrato are available for takes recorded without one. `soundbank.py split` cuts recordings of notes played one at a time (a chromatic run per file and dynamic) into one file per note, matching what it finds to the run the file is named for, keeping the better-tuned take of a note played twice, and records every cut with its hash so `soundbank.py cut` reproduces the notes exactly on install; a faint ring or hum far under the file's notes is not taken for a note, and a plucked note starts at its attack. A build can reduce the steady hiss and rumble of each take by a set amount, against the noise its own recording carries between notes (measured without the publisher's gated silence, breaths or last rings), leaving the notes' level and tone; told each note's pitch, it leaves the note's own low partials as recorded, where a soft low note is no louder than the room's rumble.
- **Measuring**: `studio.py analyze` for section levels, band shares, stereo correlation, stem levels and lead presence, in NumPy only, and for the whole master's tonal balance, width per band and peak-to-loudness ratio against published measurements of commercial recordings (Pestana et al. 2013; Elowsson and Friberg 2017) and, with `--reference`, against a measured demo of the genre, another render or a WAV file; `studio.py profile` describes the written score per part and section (density, register, intervals, rhythm, repetition) after Yang and Lerch (2020).

### Known issues

- Nobody but a person can say whether a piece works. The agent cannot listen; `review` and `analyze` only locate problems.
- Pitch bends move the whole sample, so a sampled guitar bend lacks a real string's change of tone, and fast runs cannot be slurred.
- The free banks have limits:
  - the YR basses have one velocity layer and sample only up to B♭2;
  - the VCSL piano has three velocity layers and no pedal-down samples (the Osiris piano has both pedal states, in two layers);
  - the cello's takes carry no vibrato of their own;
  - the double bass's vibrato takes repeat the player's bow changes about every 2.3 s, and the piccolo is sampled on five notes;
  - the section strings, the clarinet and the tremolo speak slowly;
  - marimba and xylophone are sampled sparsely;
  - the trumpet's vibrato samples last about 4.7 s.
- The erhu's sustained takes last 2–3 s, so a longer held note repeats its last second; the VCSL tenor saxophone's two channels are partly out of phase and want `mix.width` near 0.2; the steel drum's top notes keep their recorded tuning.
- The synthesizers run in NumPy, a few times slower than real time for dense parts.
- The timpani's tuning was measured from the recordings, not by ear; an independent by-ear mapping of some of the same takes sits about half a semitone lower.
- Layer loops carry no compressor or limiter, so a game playing them together needs a limiter on its music bus.
- Linux and macOS are tested in CI with the system tools only; samples can differ slightly from the pinned Windows builds.
- `declick` is for takes recorded one note per file: it reads clicks on the average of the channels, so a click recorded in opposite polarity on the two can be missed, and it cannot tell a second strike's hammer noise from a knock, which on a take holding more than one note would lower the new note's first frames.
- `match_notes: "across"` measures each dynamic against its own median, so a dynamic recorded over less of the range than the others can step slightly at the edge of its range (on the Kawai, 0.5 dB at most).
- Installing banks assumes a runtime folder that no other program changes during the install: archive members are checked against links in the destination before they are written, not while.
