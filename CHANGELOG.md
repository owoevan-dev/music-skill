# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

Faster revisions with the same music. In the nine 0.3.0 agent sessions most of the time was the model's, and the agents opened the commands' JSON with code of their own 26 times a session, because the printed output did not answer what they had to decide. 0.4.0 changes what reaches the agent and what a render does, not how the music is made: the review's questions, the guidance and the audio are those of 0.3.0.

### Added

- **`studio.py round`**: one revision round in one call. It validates the project, reviews the score, renders a draft, analyzes it and compares it with the previous round (`--previous`), printing each step's summary as it finishes and writing `round.json` from the start, which names the step that stopped a round that failed; `--clip` adds the change old then new at one loudness, and `--json` prints round.json instead of the summaries. SKILL.md makes it the way to revise; the single commands remain for what a round does not do. Anthropic's guidance for agent tools: consolidate steps that are always chained.
- **Draft renders** (`render --draft`, and every round): the same audio, stems, buses and returns as a full render, without master.flac, the check that the MP3 decodes to the master's loudness, and a game loop's Ogg copies and layers, as REAPER's dry-run render processes everything without writing the output files. A draft's status is `DRAFT_PASS`; deliver a full render.
- **Frozen parts**: a part's cache entry keeps its processed stem, and the next render copies it while nothing it depends on has changed: its raw synthesis, its mix (not the sends and bus, which only the mixer reads), the song's length, the part a duck follows, the renderer's code, FFmpeg and NumPy. The copy is checked against its hash. A DAW plays a frozen track the same way. In our tests, moving one kick note in Sprinkle Satellite processed the kick and the six parts it ducks, and nothing else.
- **`studio.py clean`** lists the piece's render and round folders other than those kept (`--keep` the final render and the previous round), its auditions' caches and the cache entries the kept renders do not use, as Ableton Live's Cleanup does, and removes them only with `--remove`, as git clean deletes only with `-f`. It removes files, not folders: every folder a command writes records the files it wrote in `files.json` (path, size and modification time), and clean removes a file only while that record lists it unchanged, as Ninja's clean tool removes only the outputs its build log names. A file put in such a folder, a file changed since, another piece's render inside it and a folder from before 0.4.0 (no record) stay, and the report names them; a folder goes whole only when nothing else is left in it, its records last, so a clean stopped part-way is found again by the next. A cache entry goes only when it holds nothing but what renders write there, under the entry's lock. Links and junctions are never followed, on every Python the skill runs on. On the 27 GB folder of one 0.3.0 agent piece it would free 20 GB.
- **Short summaries**: `round`, `review`, `render`, `analyze`, `compare`, `audition` and `clean` print what an agent decides from, and end with the file and the keys holding the full figures; `--json` prints the whole report. The summaries hold every review question with the first places it occurs, the failed checks, the master against its target, each section's level, stereo and lead, the tonal balance against published measurements and a reference, and what changed. [outputs.md](skills/music-production/references/outputs.md) lists every key. On the nine agents' own outputs, the median summary is 1.9 k characters for a review (0.3.0 printed 1.1 k), 2.6 k for an analysis (5.5 k) and 3.2 k for a comparison (3.3 k), against 12 to 19 k for the full JSON.
- `report.json` records the seconds per stage and in FFmpeg by kind; each stem receipt, the part's synthesis and processing seconds and whether its stem was reused.

### Changed

- **Swing pairs are counted from each bar line**, as a drum machine delays the even-numbered steps of the beat (Linn 2013): every bar swings alike, and a bar holding an odd number of units (eighths in 5/8 or 7/8, quarters in 3/4) plays its last one straight. Before, pairs ran on from beat 0 across the bar lines, so in 7/8 every second downbeat played 80 ms late. Meters whose bars hold whole pairs play as before.
- `review` says what it counts as a push: an attack at most an eighth before a downbeat or half-bar that its part holds over or rests through (Longuet-Higgins and Lee 1984), in [development-and-groove.md](skills/music-production/references/development-and-groove.md).
- **`validate` finds a key struck twice at once**, or one tick apart, as written and as played, before anything renders. The error names both notes, the bar and beat and what to change; before, it came only at render time.
- **Renders are faster with the same audio.** The production delay's band, saturation, the mono low end and `compare`'s bands take their FFTs at a fast length. A stem's sample count can have a large prime factor, which made the delay the slowest step of a part. The K-weighting response is computed once per length and the master is measured once. Banks are hashed when a part first uses them, and again if the file changes. The mix's checks run while the master is made where the free memory holds both (measured: 15 float64 copies of the song together), and the MP3, FLAC and a game loop are made at the same time. On our test laptop, Sodium Lights (4:28, 14 parts) took 140 s from scratch with 0.3.0; with 0.4.0 a draft from an empty cache takes 80 s, and a full render that keeps every stem takes 52 s. Every stem not played by Surge XT matches 0.3.0's bit for bit, or within −277 dBFS for one delayed guitar, and the fughetta's master is bit for bit the same. On test signals the delay's band moved by at most −225 dBFS, saturation and the mono low end by at most −83 and −76 dBFS.
- **Guides in parts.**
  - `soundbanks.md` lists the banks; their presets, what probes measured on each bank and building a bank are in [soundbank-presets.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-presets.md), [soundbank-measurements.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-measurements.md) and [soundbank-building.md](skills/music-production/references/soundbank-building.md).
  - The analysis sections of `performance-and-mix.md` are [measuring.md](skills/music-production/references/measuring.md).
  - The sound design and the curated Surge XT patches of `electronic.md` are [electronic-sounds.md](skills/music-production/references/electronic-sounds.md).
  - Every reference over 100 lines starts with a table of contents (Anthropic, Skill authoring best practices). `schema.md` stays whole, with headings in its opening block, because every agent that read it used every section. No guidance was removed.
- Stems and returns stay 32-bit float WAV. FLAC holds integer samples only (Xiph), and in eight of the nine 0.3.0 pieces a stem, bus or premix passes full scale, which 24-bit FLAC would clip.
- A render no longer prints a line per part. A failed master check names the true peak, the clipped samples and the length, and a failed render names its failed checks.
- The selfcheck runs 136 checks (109 without a renderer configuration). It adds the review summary and revision rounds followed by `clean`, and checks that a draft equals the full render, that frozen stems are reused bit for bit, that a damaged or swapped one is processed again, that a send edit keeps every stem and a moved kick processes the parts ducked to it, that a cache entry is held across processes, that `clean` keeps what the tools did not make (files named like records, links and junctions, another piece's folders), that a named bank is hashed at first use, and that the mix's checks give the same render beside the master and after it; and, from the later reviews, that no write goes through a link or overwrites what is there, that the installers and `--delete-sources` keep what is not theirs, that a FLAC source, a SoundFont's tables and pedalboard are what they claim, that delivered audio carries no tags from its inputs and shared records no local paths, that 200 automation points render, that swing pairs and an excerpt's edges follow the piece, that parts wait for memory, and that every script prints a character its output cannot encode as an escape. A repository test checks that every link to a section names a heading that exists, as GitHub renders the heading.
- The examples no longer carry the agents' notes. Each was a report for the maintainer, written when the piece was delivered and before anyone had listened, so on a public page it read as open doubts about a finished piece, and it named render folders the repository does not hold. The README no longer says how the four were picked.
- Questions and ideas go to the repository's [Discussions](https://github.com/owoevan-dev/music-skill/discussions): the issue form for them is replaced by links to Q&A and Ideas, and the READMEs and CONTRIBUTING.md say so.

### Fixed

Found by two reviews of this release and two audits of 0.3.0 (the main branch), each reproduced before it was fixed:

- **No command overwrites or writes through what is already there.** An output never takes an occupied path, a dangling link included. Every file is written to a new file of its own and swapped in whole, so a hard link at the path keeps its bytes and two runs never share a temporary name. A render taken up again with `--resume` refuses a folder holding links or files with other names.
- **A source build takes only the samples the lock pins.** Each bank built from a sample repository now has its files pinned in the lock (each path with its git blob hash and size, as `samples_sha256`), so a source listing that names other files, or a path that leaves the sample folder (a locked folder followed by `..`), is refused before anything is fetched, and each file is fetched no larger than its pinned size. Before, the listing's paths were trusted after a prefix match. Found by a GPT security scan of 0.3.0.
- **NumPy is installed only as published.** requirements.txt names every file PyPI publishes for the pinned NumPy (2.1.2, and 2.5.4 on Python 3.14 and newer) by SHA-256, which puts pip in its hash-checking mode; install other packages with a pip command of their own.
- The pedalboard wheel is fetched from its locked address, no larger than its locked size and hashed as it arrives, instead of by `pip download`, which fetched whatever the index served before the lock could be checked.
- `analyze` and `compare` refuse a render folder holding links, so no stem is read, or compared, from wherever a link points; no WAV longer than a render can be (31 minutes) is read, a reference included, and a FLAC sample source is decoded to at most ten minutes.
- A part that would need more memory at once than the machine has is refused before anything is synthesized, with the reason: the built-in synth makes each note whole, about 30 MB a second of it, so a pad held through a long song could take tens of gigabytes.
- `review`'s low-interval check indexes the notes by pitch, as the minor-ninth check does: the same findings (1,839 random projects), without scanning every note of the bars each note spans.
- **The installers keep what they did not put there.** `bootstrap.py` and `fetch_soundbanks.py` extract into a private folder and install file by file: a file at the target that the installer did not record there is set aside as `<name>.replaced-<time>`, never deleted, and so is a cached download that no longer matches the lock. A failed download leaves no partial file. `--delete-sources` deletes only files that are still the installer's own (an archive by its locked size and SHA-256, a sample by its git blob hash), never through a link, and names what it kept.
- `soundbank.py inspect` no longer overwrites its own input, and `selfcheck.py --work` takes only a new or empty folder.
- A render folder given to `compare` or `analyze` is validated as a project before it is read.
- A FLAC source must start with FLAC's `fLaC` marker (RFC 9639), and FFmpeg reads it with its FLAC demuxer from local files only, so a file named `.flac` cannot make it read anything else.
- Surge XT plays only when the binary checked is the plugin that loads, and pedalboard, which runs it, only when its files are those pip installed (each file's hash in the package's RECORD, the wheel format, and no code file it does not list).
- Delivered audio carries no tags from the files it was made from: every FFmpeg output is written with `-map_metadata -1 -map_chapters -1` (a tag added to a cached WAV reached master.wav, the MP3 and the FLAC).
- The records meant to be shared (round.json, the comparisons, audition.json) name files relative to their folders, and a Surge fallback's reason names files, not paths; the logs keep the full paths ([outputs.md](skills/music-production/references/outputs.md) says what stays local).
- A SoundFont whose preset or instrument tables do not hold together (indices that run back, a terminal record short of its table, a zone naming an instrument or sample that is not there) is refused, as the SoundFont 2.04 specification and FluidSynth refuse it; indices running back and forth made a 4 KB file expand into 8,192 zones.
- `review` of a dense score no longer takes quadratic time: the minor seconds and ninths are found through an index of the notes by pitch (2,000 dense notes: 0.05 s, from 0.27 s, the same counts on 370 random projects). A spreadsheet formula in a note's phrase or articulation is written to notes.csv with a leading apostrophe (OWASP, CSV Injection).
- `audition --solo` plays a part ducked to another part (the duck is left out, and said so) instead of failing every version. An audition records what stopped it, and its context parts may barely sound in the passage without stopping the render.
- A part's automation is applied in NumPy, sample by sample: an FFmpeg expression failed past 95 points and stepped the level every 4,096 samples (85 ms). Before its first point the part keeps the first point's level; FFmpeg extrapolated the first ramp backwards.
- A part's raw synthesis is found again only when no code it runs has changed: its cache key covers every module the synthesis imports (a vinyl noise part kept its old noise after `production.py` changed).
- Parts made at once share the memory free at the start, each holding what it was measured to need (its bank, its synthesis, its production tools, for the song's length), instead of 2 GB each whatever the song; the built-in synth's filter takes a long note a chunk at a time, which halves the memory of a held pad (two minutes: 3.7 GB, from 8.3 GB) with the same samples.
- The mix reads its filtergraph from a file: on one command line, 64 parts in a deep folder passed the 32,767 characters a Windows command line holds.
- The bootstrap, the selfcheck, `soundbank.py` and `make_example.py` print any character their output cannot encode, in whatever language a folder or piece is named, as an escape (`\u5586`), as `studio.py` and the bank fetcher already did, instead of stopping on it: on Windows, Python writes the output an agent reads through a pipe in the ANSI code page, which lacks characters some names hold (喆 and 堃 are not in code page 950, Traditional Chinese Windows'). Set up in a folder named with 喆, the bootstrap installed and checked everything, then stopped with an error printing where it saved the configuration. `clean` removes a round's `review/` once nothing else stays in it, its record with it, and tells its records by what they hold, not their names, so a file of the user's named `files.json`, or a `project.json` no record lists, stays. A render whose stages each took under a second no longer prints empty parentheses. Found by setting the skill up again in folders named with Chinese characters, spaces and 喆, and rendering a round in one named in Korean, Japanese, Turkish and accented Latin letters with an emoji; setup.md now says folder names may hold any characters and asks, on Windows, for runtime and piece folders under 120 characters, as FluidSynth 2.6.1 does not opt in to paths over 260 characters.

- From CI:
  - JSON is read to 64 levels of nesting and no deeper, on every Python. Python 3.14 parses a list nested 100,000 levels deep, which older Pythons refuse, and a free-form harmony 600 levels deep, which every Python parses, passed validate and stopped `excerpt` with RecursionError. The tools' own files nest 8 levels at most, measured over 183,131 of them.
  - `validate` refuses a project that is not a JSON object, and a clock, mix, track, section, tempo map, harmony entry, notes list or performance of another JSON type, naming the field; any other field of the wrong type is refused as that. They raised AttributeError or TypeError and stopped the command.
  - `bootstrap.py` tries a download again after a dropped connection, a timeout, a server error (5xx or 429) or a download cut short, after 3, 6 and 12 s, as `fetch_soundbanks.py` does, and names the download that failed. One HTTP 504 from GitHub's servers stopped a whole install, saying only "HTTP Error 504".
- A part whose synthesis is silent stops the render with its name and what to check (its notes against its bank's keys, its patch, its notes' length and loudness), not a bare "Silent stem"; as an audition's context part it keeps its own level. `clean` names what stays in a folder it removes only in part, the folders inside it included. Both found by setting the skill up from scratch and running a piece through it.
- A receipt's synthesis seconds no longer include the time spent waiting for another render.
- An excerpt, and so an audition, plays a passage as the piece does. A note is re-attacked at the cut only if it still sounds there: a long note struck again before the cut has stopped. A re-attack is left out where the key is struck again within 100 ms as played, counting both notes' timing, the part's offset and swing. Notes that a part's earlier offset plays before the cut all start the excerpt, and where two of one key would meet on its first tick only the later stays. Before, an excerpt could play a note the piece had already stopped, or strike a key twice at once and fail to render. Found by random projects and by two reviews.
  - It also keeps a linear CC ramp's value at the cut and the end, leaves out a note held over the cut that its gate or timing stopped before it, decides which notes are in the passage on the tick grid the piece plays them on (a note a float hair under the end, or with less than a tick left, is not; a note on a half tick lands where it does in the piece), and keeps the piece's ritardandi that reach into the passage, as `clock.rubato.spans`.
- Two renders of one piece running at once no longer write one cache entry together. A part holds its entry across processes while it is made (an operating-system file lock), and every temporary file is named for its process and thread.
- `compare` no longer gives a file that changed throughout an overall level (a rewritten harp showed −332 dB). Where one gain cannot match the two files, `level_db` is null and the summary says so. Reported by two of the agents that made the 0.4.0 test pieces.
- A phaser's `rate_hz` is checked against FFmpeg aphaser's range, 0.1 to 2 Hz. A rate outside it (validate allowed 0.05 to 5) passed and the render failed with FFmpeg's error. Found by random renders.
- A download redirect that the host check refuses now closes its connection.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-07

Music that keeps changing and moves. Seven pieces that agents made with 0.2.0 were heard as not wrong, but at times dull or stiff; measured on their scores, the causes were loops held for 24 to 48 bars, choruses thinner than their pre-choruses and chord parts struck as blocks, not missing micro-timing.

Guidance from professional sources only. - **[development-and-groove.md](skills/music-production/references/development-and-groove.md)**, from the measurements and the literature:
  - an energy plan per section, and how much the music should change;
  - variation that keeps identity: texture, register, rhythm, harmony, melody and transitions;
  - loops that move at 8- and 16-bar phrase edges;
  - syncopation in moderation, and pushes into strong beats;
  - accompaniment that moves, and chord voicings.
- **`musiclib.voicing.voice_lead`** voices a chord progression for a keyboard, pad, string or brass part, chosen across the whole progression so the voices move as little as possible (common tones held).
  - Spacing: adjacent voices within an octave, no close interval below its low interval limit, no voice crossing, and no minor second or minor ninth between two voices.
  - `against`: the pitches other parts sound with each chord (the melody, the bass, the left hand). The voicing keeps minor seconds and ninths away from them and, where it can, leaves their pitches to them.
  - `ceilings`: keeps the voicing under the melody, chord by chord.
  - Optionally no consecutive fifths or octaves, wherever the range leaves another voicing; where it leaves none, the fewest remain. `review` lists parallels between parts that each play one line, not inside one chord part.
  - Tones left out or doubled in the order arrangers use; under a major seventh the root goes first. A chord the range cannot hold doubles another tone, then leaves one more out. A tone that would rub against the other parts wherever the range puts it is left out: under a melody on the root of a major-seventh chord, the accompaniment plays the triad. `keep_root` keeps the root where no bass part plays it (a rootless A♭maj7 in three voices is heard as C minor).

  A ii-V-I moves three semitones per change; an I-V-vi-IV loop moves five to seven, against seven to 28 as root-position blocks.

  - phrases that breathe, mostly by step, with leaps for character (Perricone 2000);

  Linked from SKILL.md and the anime-opening guide.

Signal Fire, whose piano plays the pedal preset, was re-rendered on it (the knocks were 32-35 dB under the mix) and approved by ear; the other examples re-render bit for bit. The other sampled pianos (the Steinway, the Kawai and the uprights) have such knocks too, but most of them 3–20 s into a note, where only a long or pedalled note reaches them; their banks keep them as recorded, and `declick` can repair them in a local build. The recordings' own noise stays as recorded; the `noise.md` and `soundbanks.md` references name the pianos that carry the least of it under a ringing note (the Osiris, and the Yamaha upright) for quiet, exposed piano.
- The Kawai grand and the Yamaha, Knight and VSCO uprights played soft passages far too softly: their velocity layers were recorded 14–18 dB apart and the velocity curve came on top, so velocity 50 sounded 25–44 dB under 126 (16 dB on the Osiris and the Steinway) and a layer split jumped 5–20 dB. `soundbank.py` gains `match_notes: "across"`, which levels every dynamic and round robin of a note to one level on a register slope the dynamics share; `vcsl_keys` b2 and `vsco_colors` b3 use it: velocity 50 now sits about 16 dB under 126 on all six pianos, and on these four a layer split moves a note by 2.2 dB at most (the Steinway's by 2.4, the Osiris's by 10.6 dB on one note, E5). Late Set at the Corner (Kawai) and Rain on the Tram Line (Yamaha upright) were re-rendered on them and approved by ear; the other demos do not use those presets.
- NumPy 2.1.2 stays pinned for Python 3.10–3.13 (the banks' builds reproduce with it); Python 3.14 and newer take a current NumPy 2, and CI adds a Python 3.14 job and a Windows job.
- Documentation: install steps with a virtual environment and the disk space the downloads take, where each download comes from, the Surge host's licence, MS Basic's acknowledgement request, the listening rule, the default loudness target, nested synth fields, the ride pattern, the strum grid, the horn shelf (about 4 dB) and the tonal-balance thresholds as prompts rather than validated limits.

### Added

- **Projects**: a validated JSON format for score, performance and mix: up to 64 parts, 512 bars and 30 minutes, one meter with a tempo map, quarter-note time throughout. MIDI export of the performed and the notated timing, one MIDI per part, and a bounded MIDI reader.
- **Noise guide** (`references/noise.md`): where noise in a render comes from (the instruments, gain, voices adding up, the playback level), how to find the part, treatments from the arrangement and a high-pass to denoising the samples, with their limits, and every bank's exposed recording noise as measured (none calls for more reduction).
- **Score review**: sustained minor seconds and ninths between parts, foreground notes outside the written chords (anticipations excluded), phrase and velocity prompts.
- **Performance**: `musiclib.expression`, long-range correlated timing and dynamics per player, a feel per part, phrase arches, staggered chords with the top note leading, a cymbal accent cycle and CC11 swells. Pitch bends with an RPN range, render-time legato that slurs connected notes on one sample, controller ramps and part offsets for slow-speaking samples.
- **Rendering**: FluidSynth per part with any configured SoundFont bank, render-side patches and key maps, a raw synthesis cache keyed by notes, performance, dependency hashes and the renderer's own code, an additive synth voice and noise transitions. Parts render in parallel (`--jobs`, one per processor up to 8), and the master is the same for any number.
- **Mixing**: the bus chain: six buses, four generated spaces (room, plate, hall and church) with per-octave decay, a master chain and a delivery limiter checked for true peak and MP3 loudness, whose ceiling comes down when bright transients would overshoot −1 dBTP between samples. `mix.dynamics: "wide"` for orchestral and organ music leaves out the compressors and saturation; its master only sets the level and holds it down rather than limit, and the report gives the loudness range (EBU Tech 3342). The older single-room chain stays the default for existing projects.
- **Synthesizers and drum machines**: a `synth` engine with band-limited oscillators (PolyBLEP at four times the rate), supersaw unison, two-operator FM, resonant filters that may move every sample (exact direct-form recursions), envelopes, tempo-synced LFOs, glide, a pitch envelope and oversampled drive, and 25 presets from supersaw leads and future-bass chords to an 808 bass and NES-style chip voices (pulse duties, a 16-step triangle, clocked noise, chords as 60 Hz arpeggios); a `drum_synth` engine with 808- and 909-style voices, a choked open hat and a 1980s gated-reverb snare; a `surge` engine that plays fifty factory patches of the Surge XT synthesizer, chosen from its 505 lead, pad, key, pluck, brass and bass patches by measured pitch, attack, brightness, tail and width, through pedalboard in a separate process (both GPL-3.0, installed by default and optional), each with a built-in fallback preset where Surge XT is missing.
- **Production tools**: ducking keyed by another part's notes, a delay that follows the tempo map to the sample, filter sweeps, oversampled saturation, rhythmic gates, chorus, phaser and bit reduction, tape wow and flutter on a part or the whole mix, a record's surface noise, low frequencies made mono, and a loud master for dense electronic styles whose soft clipper runs at four times the rate; the report gives how much level clipper and limiter took from each section, and `analyze` warns past 3 dB.
- **Feel and phrasing**: `performance.swing` for eighths or sixteenths, with ratios by tempo from jazz recordings (Friberg and Sundström 2002); `clock.rubato` slows phrase ends and the close as performers do (Friberg and Sundberg 1999); drum parts that step through their kit's recorded round robins on repeated hits, and round robins from neighbouring keys for banks recorded without them; `musiclib.phrasing` for arched dynamics and swells.
- **Genre guides**: electronic music (the break routine measured by Solberg 2014 and Solberg and Dibben 2019, sidechain and perceived timing, synthwave, chiptune on the NES's channels), lo-fi and instrumental hip-hop, jazz and funk (swing ratios, walking bass, rootless voicings, the One), piano and chamber music (phrase timing after Repp 1992), and high points and melody (chills research, orchestral gestures, McAdams et al. 2022's grouping taxonomy, von Hippel and Huron 2000).
- **Game loops**: a project with `loop` also renders `loop.wav` (loop points in a `smpl` chunk, a cue point at every section), `loop.ogg` (`LOOPSTART` and `LOOPLENGTH`) and `loop.json` (with BPM, beats per bar and beat count for engines that switch on a beat), with the render's tail folded back from the loop start. `loop.layers` also renders each layer as its own loop; the layers line up with the full loop and add up to the mix before the master's dynamics. `review` and the render report describe the seam by meter, timbre, pitch, volume and abruptness, after Medina-Gray (2019). A game-music guide covers loop seams, repetition, mood cues and scenes, layers, stingers and endings, and delivery to Godot, Unity and RPG Maker.
- **Anime openings**: a guide to TV-size band cues: the form and the minor-third modulations measured on 100 popular openings (Li 2026), the build to the chorus (Summach 2011), harmony and power chords under distortion, an instrumental lead, drums and groove (Witek et al. 2014), arrangement by section, and where a band mix gets its brightness. `musiclib.expression` strums guitar chords down and up.
- **Scene music**: a guide to music under the text and voices of visual novels and RPG scenes: tempo and level for reading (Thompson et al. 2012; Kämpfe et al. 2011), the level under voiced lines (Torcoli et al. 2019; WCAG 1.4.7) and Ren'Py's ducking, the 1–4 kHz octaves that carry speech (ANSI S3.5), a set of cues built on leitmotifs (Summers 2014), scene starting points and delivery to Ren'Py.
- **Orchestra**: a guide to writing for the symphony orchestra on the banks: instruments and ranges, balance by Rimsky-Korsakov's equivalents of strength, chord spacing and doubling, soft solos on tracks of their own, seating, the hall (Beranek 2004) and the wide master.
- **Expression on sampled instruments**: `performance.dynamics` plays each note of a sustained part on the recorded dynamic nearest a curve, the curve moving its level and its tone through a filter learned from the bank's own layers (Fabiani 2009), or, with `dynamics_mode: "crossfade"`, crossfades renders of every recorded dynamic along it, with equal-power curves corrected for the renders' correlation (Fink, Holters and Zölzer 2016); one take per note is the default, since neighbouring dynamics of a note agree in phase only by chance and comb-filter when held together; crossfade legato joins a single line from presets that enter each take past its attack, as SFZ legato regions do; `mix.depth_m` stands a part on a modelled stage, with early reflections from image sources (Allen and Berkley 1979) and more hall with depth; `mix.range_lu` brings a wide master's loudness range down by lifting its quieter passages. The `vsco`, `vsco_extra` and `cello_bigcat` banks carry the dynamic-layer and legato presets.
- **Organ**: a guide to the pipe organ: registrations, manuals and pedal, phrasing through timing rather than dynamics (Gingras et al. 2010, 2013), and the church it sounds in (Mapp 2019; Girón et al. 2017, 2020).
- **Building banks**: `soundbank.py build` turns WAV, FLAC or AIFF sample sets into SF2 instruments from a JSON spec: pitch checked against file names, levels matched, round robins spread over neighbouring velocities. Kits can mix several microphones per hit, keep the recorded balance between pieces, choke an open hi-hat and replace the synthesizer's velocity curves. A pitch reading an octave off, from a tracker that locked onto a harmonic, can count as the named note. Sustained instruments keep each recording's own loop, moved back and crossfaded where a take sags at its end; takes recorded without one get a loop cut from their steady part, clear of noises. Uneven takes can be evened against their nearest neighbours, keeping the register's slope. A straight velocity line and a delayed vibrato are available for takes recorded without one. `soundbank.py split` cuts recordings of notes played one at a time (a chromatic run per file and dynamic) into one file per note, matching what it finds to the run the file is named for, keeping the better-tuned take of a note played twice, and records every cut with its hash so `soundbank.py cut` reproduces the notes exactly on install; a faint ring or hum far under the file's notes is not taken for a note, and a plucked note starts at its attack. A build can reduce the steady hiss and rumble of each take by a set amount, against the noise its own recording carries between notes (measured without the publisher's gated silence, breaths or last rings), leaving the notes' level and tone; told each note's pitch, it leaves the note's own low partials as recorded, where a soft low note is no louder than the room's rumble.
- **Measuring**: `studio.py analyze` for section levels, band shares, stereo correlation, stem levels and lead presence, in NumPy only, and for the whole master's tonal balance, width per band and peak-to-loudness ratio against published measurements of commercial recordings (Pestana et al. 2013; Elowsson and Friberg 2017) and, with `--reference`, against a measured demo of the genre, another render or a WAV file; `studio.py profile` describes the written score per part and section (density, register, intervals, rhythm, repetition) after Yang and Lerch (2020).
- **Setup**: pinned portable FluidSynth and FFmpeg on Windows x64; `--system` on Linux and macOS with FFmpeg 6 or newer; Surge XT (the plugin-only Windows release pinned by hash, or your own install elsewhere), pedalboard and the fifty patches set up unless `--no-surge`, about 55 MB on Windows; twenty-nine optional sound banks, all checked by hash, five downloaded from their publishers and twenty-four built here, downloaded as this project built them from its release (2.8 GB compressed; 3.4 GB for all twenty-nine) and installed by group (`--only orchestra`, `band`, `world`, `examples`) or by name, or, with `--from-source` or where the release cannot be reached, built from the samples, each build fetching only the samples its spec uses and retrying a dropped download, reusing a bank already built with its locked hash, and with `--delete-sources` removing the downloads once the banks are verified; a project that names a bank not yet installed stops with the command that installs it; all CC0 except the University of Iowa recordings, which are free for any use without restrictions, and a steel drum dedicated to the public domain under the Unlicense:
  - VSCO 2 CE: strings with tremolo and pizzicato, solo violin, brass, trumpet, woodwinds, glockenspiel, and, as banks of their own, the violin, viola and cello sections each over its whole range, trombone, tuba, solo double bass and piccolo with looped sustains, and muted brass, falls, growls and vibrato winds;
  - VCSL: grand piano, harp, marimba, vibraphone, xylophone, tubular bells, timpani with measured tuning, an orchestra kit with timed cymbal swells, and, as banks of their own, a pipe organ with looped sustains; harpsichords, a Kawai grand, upright pianos and Yamaha TX81Z FM voices; recorders, ocarina, harmonicas, tenor sax, saxello and a Renaissance organ; and folk harp, dan tranh, psaltery, kalimbas, balafon and world percussion;
  - Osiris Piano: a grand recorded with and without the sustain pedal;
  - Karoryfer x bigcat cello: a solo cello with looped sustains;
  - Big Rusty Drums;
  - Black And Blue Basses: a five-string bass with ghost notes;
  - more Karoryfer libraries: a pizzicato double bass with muted and ghost notes, an archtop guitar through its microphone and its pickup, a tuba and a baritone saxophone with looped sustains, and a sung "ah";

### Known issues

- Nobody but a person can say whether a piece works. The agent cannot listen; `review` and `analyze` only locate problems.
- Pitch bends move the whole sample, so a sampled guitar bend lacks a real string's change of tone, and fast runs cannot be slurred.
- The free banks have limits:
  - the YR basses have one velocity layer and sample only up to B♭2;
  - the VCSL piano has three velocity layers and no pedal-down samples (the Osiris piano has both pedal states, in two layers);
  - the cello's takes carry no vibrato of their own;
  - the double bass's vibrato takes repeat the player's bow changes about every 2.3 s, and the piccolo is sampled on five notes;
  - the section strings, the clarinet and the tremolo speak slowly;
  - marimba and xylophone are sampled sparsely;
  - the trumpet's vibrato samples last about 4.7 s.
- The erhu's sustained takes last 2–3 s, so a longer held note repeats its last second; the VCSL tenor saxophone's two channels are partly out of phase and want `mix.width` near 0.2; the steel drum's top notes keep their recorded tuning.
- The synthesizers run in NumPy, a few times slower than real time for dense parts.
- The timpani's tuning was measured from the recordings, not by ear; an independent by-ear mapping of some of the same takes sits about half a semitone lower.
- Layer loops carry no compressor or limiter, so a game playing them together needs a limiter on its music bus.
- Linux and macOS are tested in CI with the system tools only; samples can differ slightly from the pinned Windows builds.
- `declick` is for takes recorded one note per file: it reads clicks on the average of the channels, so a click recorded in opposite polarity on the two can be missed, and it cannot tell a second strike's hammer noise from a knock, which on a take holding more than one note would lower the new note's first frames.
- `match_notes: "across"` measures each dynamic against its own median, so a dynamic recorded over less of the range than the others can step slightly at the edge of its range (on the Kawai, 0.5 dB at most).
- Installing banks assumes a runtime folder that no other program changes during the install: archive members are checked against links in the destination before they are written, not while.
