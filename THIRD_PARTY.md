# Third-party software, sound banks and credits

Nothing in this list is bundled in the repository, apart from the statistics under [Data](#data), which are computed from a dataset that is not. Setup downloads each item from its publisher (the sound banks built from sample libraries come, unless `--from-source`, as this project built them, from the repository's release), checks it against the SHA-256 pinned in `skills/music-production/references/runtime-lock.json` or `soundbank-lock.json`, and keeps it outside the skill folder.

The recorded banks are CC0, or for the Iowa recordings free for any use without restrictions, or for the steel drum dedicated to the public domain under the Unlicense, so music rendered with them needs no credit. MS Basic's MIT notice and its sample-author notices go with copies of the bank, and its licence asks for its acknowledgements in any derivative work. We read that as copies and adaptations of the bank, not music played on it, but that is our reading, not a guarantee: if a piece you publish has parts on MS Basic (the General MIDI bank parts fall back to), credit it to be safe, for example "MuseScore MS Basic SoundFont (MIT): Fluid R3 by Frank Wen and the sample authors in its licence". A bank you add yourself keeps its own terms.

## Data

`skills/music-production/references/drum-statistics.json`, which the building blocks (`musiclib.blocks`) read, holds aggregate statistics computed from the MIDI files of the [Groove MIDI Dataset](https://magenta.tensorflow.org/datasets/groove) v1.0.0 (Jon Gillick, Adam Roberts, Jesse Engel, Douglas Eck and David Bamman; Google LLC), licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes made: the performances were reduced to per-style statistics (where each drum falls in the bar, velocity percentiles by step, notes per bar, how much the bars vary, and the fills); no performance and no MIDI file is included. The paper: Gillick, Roberts, Engel, Eck and Bamman (2019), *Learning to Groove with Inverse Sequence Transformations*, ICML.

Music written with the building blocks needs no credit to the dataset. It contains none of the dataset: its notes are drawn anew from averages over thousands of bars. The licence's attribution applies to copies of the statistics, not to music written with them. Copies of the statistics file, a fork of this repository among them, keep this notice and the file's `source` field, as they keep the repository's MIT notice. That is our reading of CC BY 4.0, not legal advice.

The style maps quote tempos and section lengths measured on the annotations of the [Harmonix Set](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset) (Copyright (c) 2019 Oriol Nieto), licensed under the [MIT License](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset/blob/main/LICENSE): medians and shares by the set's genre labels, a few numbers per genre. No annotation file is included. The paper: Nieto, McCallum, Davies, Robertson, Stark and Egozy (2019), *The Harmonix Set: Beats, Downbeats, and Functional Segment Annotations of Western Popular Music*, ISMIR.

## The examples

The examples' recordings (the release examples-2) and their compositions (*Fughetta on a Lantern Theme*, *Platform in the Snowmelt*, *Sodium Lights* and *Sprinkle Satellite*) are dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/): use them anywhere, no credit needed. Their sampled sounds are CC0 (VSCO 2 Community Edition, VCSL, Karoryfer Samples' banks, the Karoryfer x bigcat cello, FreePats) or, for the University of Iowa recordings, free for any use without restrictions; the other parts are played by the skill's own synthesizers and drum machines or by Surge XT with the patches that come with it, and what you render with Surge XT is yours, as [setup.md](skills/music-production/references/setup.md) says. The scripts and project files that produce them are code, under the repository's MIT licence; the music they describe is the CC0 work, so using a project file as the start of your own piece needs no notice beyond what MIT asks of a copy of the file. The earlier examples (*Above the Clouds*, the six genre demos and *Daybreak Signal*), whose recordings stay in the release examples-1, keep their CC0 dedication.

## Study material

The arrangement guidance draws on observations from CC0 OpenScore editions, public-domain Mutopia items and the [Groove MIDI Dataset](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/datasets/groove) (CC BY 4.0, Google LLC; Gillick et al., ICML 2019). None of that material is included here; [sources-and-rights.md](skills/music-production/references/sources-and-rights.md) lists the evidence. The orchestration guide summarizes Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles of Orchestration* (the 1922 English edition, public domain in the USA, read at [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33900)), and the organ guide summarizes published papers; neither copies more than a word of them.
