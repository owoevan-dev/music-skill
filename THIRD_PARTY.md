# Third-party software, sound banks and credits

Nothing in this list is bundled in the repository, apart from the statistics under [Data](#data), which are computed from a dataset that is not. Setup downloads each item from its publisher (the sound banks built from sample libraries come, unless `--from-source`, as this project built them, from the repository's release), checks it against the SHA-256 pinned in `skills/music-production/references/runtime-lock.json` or `soundbank-lock.json`, and keeps it outside the skill folder.

## Data

`skills/music-production/references/drum-statistics.json`, which the building blocks (`musiclib.blocks`) read, holds aggregate statistics computed from the MIDI files of the [Groove MIDI Dataset](https://magenta.tensorflow.org/datasets/groove) v1.0.0 (Jon Gillick, Adam Roberts, Jesse Engel, Douglas Eck and David Bamman; Google LLC), licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes made: the performances were reduced to per-style statistics (where each drum falls in the bar, velocity percentiles by step, notes per bar, how much the bars vary, and the fills); no performance and no MIDI file is included. The paper: Gillick, Roberts, Engel, Eck and Bamman (2019), *Learning to Groove with Inverse Sequence Transformations*, ICML.

Music written with the building blocks needs no credit to the dataset. It contains none of the dataset: its notes are drawn anew from averages over thousands of bars. The licence's attribution applies to copies of the statistics, not to music written with them. Copies of the statistics file, a fork of this repository among them, keep this notice and the file's `source` field, as they keep the repository's MIT notice. That is our reading of CC BY 4.0, not legal advice.

The style maps quote tempos and section lengths measured on the annotations of the [Harmonix Set](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset) (Copyright (c) 2019 Oriol Nieto), licensed under the [MIT License](https://github.com/urinieto/harmonixset/blob/main/LICENSE): medians and shares by the set's genre labels, a few numbers per genre. No annotation file is included. How they were taken, from the repository as it stood in November 2026: the tempo is the BPM in the set's metadata; a section's label is the annotation's with any trailing number dropped; its length is the number of annotated downbeats from its boundary to the next, a boundary matched to a downbeat within 50 ms; a genre is the set's own label; a percentile is interpolated between the two nearest songs. The paper: Nieto, McCallum, Davies, Robertson, Stark and Egozy (2019), *The Harmonix Set: Beats, Downbeats, and Functional Segment Annotations of Western Popular Music*, ISMIR.

## The examples

The examples' recordings (the release examples-4) and their compositions (*Stormglass Gambit*, *Cairnfire*, *Glass Towers, Low Gear*, *The Stone Vault Turns to Light*, *Paper Lantern Hours*, *Fughetta in D minor* and *Skybutton Speedway*) are dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/): use them anywhere, no credit needed. Their sampled sounds are CC0 (VSCO 2 Community Edition, VCSL, Karoryfer Samples' banks, the Karoryfer x bigcat cello, FreePats) or, for the University of Iowa recordings, free for any use without restrictions; the other parts are played by the skill's own synthesizers and drum machines or by Surge XT with the patches that come with it, and what you render with Surge XT is yours, as [setup.md](skills/music-production/references/setup.md) says. The scripts and project files that produce them are code, under the repository's MIT licence; the music they describe is the CC0 work, so using a project file as the start of your own piece needs no notice beyond what MIT asks of a copy of the file. The earlier examples (*Fughetta on a Lantern Theme*, *Platform in the Snowmelt*, *Sodium Lights* and *Sprinkle Satellite*; before them *Above the Clouds*, the six genre demos and *Daybreak Signal*) keep their CC0 dedication.

## Study material

The arrangement guidance draws on observations from CC0 OpenScore editions, public-domain Mutopia items and the [Groove MIDI Dataset](https://magenta.withgoogle.com/datasets/groove) (CC BY 4.0, Google LLC; Gillick et al., ICML 2019). None of that material is included here; [sources-and-rights.md](skills/music-production/references/sources-and-rights.md) lists the evidence. The orchestration guide summarizes Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles of Orchestration* (the 1922 English edition, public domain in the USA, read at [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33900)), and the organ guide summarizes published papers; neither copies more than a word of them.

The style maps and the genre guides state facts found in Wikipedia articles, in the open textbooks *Understanding Music: Past and Present* (Clark, Heflin, Kluball and Kramer) and *Open Music Theory* (Gotham et al.), all three under CC BY-SA 4.0, and in the books, papers and articles each guide lists. The facts are given in this skill's own words, each with its source. No passage, table or figure of those works is included; a quotation is short and marked. [sources-and-rights.md](skills/music-production/references/sources-and-rights.md) tells what is taken into the skill and what is only cited.
