# Electronic music: synth sounds and the curated Surge XT patches

Read this when choosing a synth sound for a part in any style: the sounds each role of electronic music uses, how to find them in the Surge XT catalog and on the built-in synth, the fifty curated Surge XT patches (valid names, not the menu), the controls each gives and how to hear candidates. Choosing from the whole catalog is in [sound-selection.md](sound-selection.md); the styles, drums and production in [electronic.md](electronic.md).

**Numbers here.** A number given with no source beside it is one of two things: a fact of the skill's own banks and tools, measured here or read from their files, or a setting that is this skill's starting value, to be judged by ear and with `analyze`. The sources named give the sound and the practice; a starting value is no norm of the style.

## Sound design

Where Surge XT is installed (the default setup), start a synth part from a Surge patch chosen from its catalog for the part's need: `studio.py sounds --role <role> --character <words> --seed <piece seed>`, then two to four of the shortlist heard on the part ([sound-selection.md](sound-selection.md)). The curated patches [below](#surge-xt-presets) remain valid names but are not the menu: led by a list of them, the 0.4.0 agent pieces took 71% of their Surge parts from those fifty, and five patches recurred across pieces. Each is a full synthesizer patch with its own oscillators, filters, modulation and effects. The built-in synth is the fallback where Surge is missing, and the tool for what the curated list lacks: chip waves, a supersaw stack set field by field, the 808's pitch punch and glide, the 303's resonant sweep, risers and downlifters. A built-in part is a preset from [synth-presets.json](synth-presets.json) with your own fields over it ([schema.md](schema.md)); a field you give replaces the preset's whole field, so to change one value of a nested object (`lfo`, `filter`, `amp`), copy the preset's object and change that value. Hear two to four candidates on the part's own notes, in the mix, before building on one: `studio.py audition` plays them in turn at one loudness. With Surge installed, `review --config` asks about a built-in preset that a Surge patch covers. With the full patch set it names three patches of that role from the whole catalog, picked by the piece's seed (with the curated patches only, those). For the 808, acid, reese, wobble, chip, riser and reverse-cymbal presets it names the catalog patches named for those sounds that the measurement does not flag (for the 808, `a_liv/basses/bonsai_808` and `vospi/basses/808_builder_a`); a name is the designer's label, so hear them on the part.

| Role | Surge XT: the query (the words are the catalog's measured character) | Built-in preset and settings |
|---|---|---|
| Trance or EDM lead, anthem chords | `sounds --role lead --character bright`; anthem chords `--role chords --character wide` | `supersaw_lead`: seven detuned sawtooths spread across the stereo field, like the Roland JP-8000's Super Saw (Szabo 2010 measured its detune and mix curves). More `detune_cents` thickens it; fewer `voices` thins and focuses it |
| A lead that sings | `sounds --role lead --character warm` | a square with `glide_ms` and a delayed vibrato |
| Plucks and arpeggios | `sounds --role pluck`; `--character short` for tight arpeggios, `long` for a ringing one | `pluck`, with a dotted-eighth delay (`mix.delay` `beats: 0.75`) |
| Future-bass chords | `sounds --role chords --character wide` | `future_bass_chord`, ducked hard by the kick ([electronic.md](electronic.md), Production); an `lfo` on pitch (10–30 cents, `rate_beats` 0.5–1) for the wobbling swell |
| Pads | `sounds --role pad`; `--character slow` for a swell, `dark` or `warm`; string machines `--name string`, choirs `--name choir` | `trance_pad`, `warm_pad`; `pwm_strings` for a string machine, with `effects` `chorus` |
| Bass | `sounds --role bass`; a sub with `--character dark` or `--name sub`, an edgier one with `--character mid` or `bright`; an 808 or an acid line on the patches measured to slide and take accents as written, with `glide_ms` and `velocity_to_cutoff` ([surge-roles.json](surge-roles.json); [beats.md](beats.md), [electronic.md](electronic.md)) | `sub_bass` under a kick-led groove; `acid_bass` with a filter swept by its envelope; `reese_bass`; `wobble_bass` (its `lfo`, copied whole, with `rate_beats` 0.5 or 0.25 for wobbles on eighths or sixteenths); `synthwave_bass`; `bass_808` (a long sine with a pitch punch and glide) |
| Stabs and brass | `sounds --role brass`, warm or bright; stabs `--role chords --character fast,short` | `brass_stab` |
| Keys | `sounds --role keys`; electric pianos `--name ep`, `rhodes`, `wurli` or `suitcase`, organs `--name organ` | `fm_epiano` |
| Bells | `sounds --name bell` | `fm_bell` (bright: an eighth-note melody from G4 to G5, played alone, measured a spectral centroid of 3.3 kHz and 65% of its power above 2 kHz) |
| Transitions | with `swell`, the patches measured to rise over their note and end on the beat ([surge-roles.json](surge-roles.json): a reverse crash, a dark noise riser; four other riser patches peaked early or grew darker) | `noise_riser`, `downlifter` after a drop, `reverse_cymbal` into a downbeat; `noise` parts with `rise` and `impact`. A recorded crash reversed: a sampled kit's crash (49) with `reverse: true`, the note ending on the downbeat ([schema.md](schema.md)) |

Give every query the piece's `--seed` (`musiclib.blocks.seed(title)`): the shortlist is then a spread of its own, not the patches another piece saw first.

Write bass lines for the key-tracked filter: a bass whose cutoff follows its envelope gets duller the lower and shorter its notes. A synth part plays exactly what is written, so shape it with velocity (most presets map velocity to level and some to the filter) and with note lengths.

### Surge XT presets

Surge XT comes with 3,008 patches, 637 from its team and 2,371 from 32 sound designers. [surge-catalog.json](surge-catalog.json) measures every one on a note in its role's register: level, brightness, attack, tail, width, whether it sounds at the written pitch, and what its mod wheel and macros move. `studio.py sounds` searches it by role, character, name and designer (`--id` prints one patch in full), and a part plays any catalog patch by its id (`surge: {"preset": "factory/pads/bright"}`). The bootstrap installs the full set unless `--no-surge-catalog`; without it a catalog patch plays its fallback. Choose by the part's job and hear the shortlist on the part, as [sound-selection.md](sound-selection.md) describes. The fifty patches below were picked on 3 November 2026 for being usable (in pitch, with attack, brightness, tail and width in range), not for any style or role. They remain valid names, kept for projects that use them, but they are not where a part starts.

Where Surge XT is installed (bootstrap.py installs it unless `--no-surge`), a part can play one of fifty curated factory patches of the Surge XT synthesizer instead of a built-in preset: `engine: "surge"` with `surge: {"preset": ...}` ([schema.md](schema.md)). They were chosen from Surge XT 1.3.4's 505 lead, pad, polysynth, key, pluck, brass, wind and bass patches by measurement: each plays at the written pitch (the bass patches, built to sound an octave below their key, are played an octave up for that) and speaks as its role needs; [surge-presets.json](surge-presets.json) holds each one's measurements and built-in fallback. The words below are those measurements: dark, warm, mid or bright by spectral centroid (under 400, 800 and 1500 Hz, then above); fast attack under 30 ms to half level, soft under 250 ms, slow swell beyond; dry, short tail (under 1.2 s) or long tail after the release, the patch's own effects included; mono, narrow or wide.

Choose by the part's job ([sound-selection.md](sound-selection.md)), then hear the candidates on the part's notes in the mix (`studio.py audition`, below). A patch's own chorus, delay or reverb adds to the mix's, and effect tails can make chord changes sound sluggish (Senior 2010, in [sound-selection.md](sound-selection.md)); weigh the mix's `reverb_db` with the patch's tail, and give the project a `tail_seconds` that lets the tail ring out: the patch plays on through it, and a tail still sounding at its end fails the render's quiet-tail check. A Surge part's take differs from one synthesis to the next (random oscillator phases); the render cache keeps it until the part changes. Where Surge XT is missing the part plays its fallback preset, and `analyze` warns.

**The controls a patch gives you.** [surge-controls.json](surge-controls.json) lists, for every curated patch, what its mod wheel and its macros move, read from the patch files, with the depth.
- Most curated patches route the mod wheel (CC1, which reaches the patch) to something. In many leads and pads it opens the filter: `smooth_lead` by 17 semitones at full wheel, `butter` 39, `dreamy_flute` 25, `juno_60_strings`, `sawteeth` and `prophet_vs` 42. In `sharpish`, `slow_poly`, `mg_poly` and `rubber_bass` it closes it. Elsewhere it moves vibrato (`moogy_saw`), FM depth (`fm_rock`) or the patch's effects.
- A part with no CC1 plays the wheel where the patch was saved: at zero, except `synth_choir`, `organ_1` and `diamonds`, saved at full. A CC1 value at beat 0 (`performance.cc` `[[0, 1, 64]]`) sets the part's brightness. A curve, with `performance.cc_mode` `"linear"`, opens or closes it over a build, the filter sweep of dance music played on the patch's own filter.
- A few patches have labelled macros, set once for the part with `surge.macros` (`{"1": 0.3}`): `bright_pad` (1 Cutoff, 2 Resonance, 3 Uni Spread, 4 Attack), `slow_poly` (1 Cutoff, 2 Sync), `uni_saw` (1 Sync, 2 Uni Spread, 3 Decay), `ambient_guitar` (1 Drive, 2 Brightness, 7 FX1 Send, among eight), `bell_pad` (1 Cutoff, among eight). Others route unlabelled macros to the filter, an oscillator or an effect; the file says which.

**Hearing candidates.** `studio.py audition <project>/song.json --track lead --sounds surge:smooth_lead,surge:butter,synth:supersaw_lead --bars 21-24 --output <folder> --config <config>` renders the part's passage once as written and once per sound, with the other parts, and writes `audition.mp3` (the versions in turn at one loudness, 0.8 s apart) and `audition.json`. That file holds the part's level and brightness per version (spectral centroid, the share above 2 and 5 kHz) and, for a Surge patch, its controls. `--solo` plays the part alone; `--jobs` sets the most parts rendered at once, as for `render`; a Surge patch takes the part's Surge settings after it (`surge:a_liv/basses/bonsai_808+glide_ms=80`, `+velocity_to_cutoff=24`, `+swell`), as `review` names them, and `audition.json` gives each version's `played_as`; `soundfont:<bank>:<bank number>:<program>` auditions a sampled preset the same way.
