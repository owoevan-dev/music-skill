# Project schema: `music-production/v1`

All `on`, `duration`, CC times, tempo positions and mix automation positions use **quarter-note beats**, including compound meter. Bars in `sections.start_bar` are zero-based. Pitch and program are zero-based MIDI integers. Every count and MIDI value (bars, pitch, velocity, program, bank, CC values) is a JSON integer: `100`, not `100.0`, which validation refuses. Section ids are text. `validate` also lists, as warnings, any field it does not know with the nearest known name: a misspelt optional field would otherwise do nothing. The export uses 960 ticks per quarter.

## The project

```json
{
  "schema": "music-production/v1",
  "title": "Original piece",
  "version": "1.0.r1",
  "brief": "Musical direction and intended foreground",
  "clock": {
    "bars": 8,
    "meter": [4, 4],
    "tempo": [[0, 108]],
    "tail_seconds": 5
  },
  "sections": [{"id": "theme", "start_bar": 0, "bars": 8, "intent": "Question and answer"}],
  "tracks": [],
  "mix": {"chain": "bus", "rules": 2, "target_lufs": -14}
}
```

`clock.tempo` is a step map of [beat, quarter-note BPM] points starting at beat 0. Optional `clock.rubato` shapes it as played: `{"final_ratio": 0.6, "final_beats": 4, "phrase_ratio": 0.94, "phrase_beats": 1, "q": 2}`. The last `final_beats` slow to `final_ratio` of the written tempo along Friberg and Sundberg's (1999) final-ritardando curve (q = 2 a steady braking force, 3 softer), and the last `phrase_beats` before each section boundary ease to `phrase_ratio` and come back at its downbeat. Optional `spans`, `[[from_beat, to_beat, ratio], ...]`, lay further slowings along the same curve, each easing to `ratio` at `to_beat` and back at it; `excerpt.py` writes them so that a passage keeps the piece's ritardandi, which may begin before its first beat or end after its last. MIDI export, audio and every measurement share the played map; the project keeps the written one.

`tracks` must contain 1–64 entries. Sections must cover the entire form without gaps. The fixed meter denominator may be 2, 4, 8 or 16. Tempo must begin at beat zero, be strictly increasing in time and stay within 30–300 quarter-note BPM. For 6/8 with dotted-quarter pulse 66, use quarter-note BPM 99. The bounds are 512 bars, 500,000 notes and 30 minutes; these are resource limits, not compositional targets.

## The mix

The project `mix`:
- Bus chain only: `loudness: "loud"` for dense electronic genres: a soft clipper computed at four times the rate rounds peaks before the delivery limiter and `target_lufs` may go up to −8 (not with `dynamics: "wide"`). The report's `mastering.limiting_db` gives, per section, how much level clipper and limiter took, and `analyze` warns past 3 dB. `mono_below_hz` (40–250) makes the premix mono below that frequency (a zero-phase high-pass on the side channel, 48 dB per octave). `wow_flutter` (below) wobbles the whole premix as a worn tape would, before `mono_below_hz`.
- `chain`: `"bus"` mixes through buses, four generated spaces, a master chain and a delivery limiter; `"room"` (the default, kept for older projects) sends every part dry into one shared room and limits once. See [performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md).
- `target_lufs` (−30 to −10, default −16): integrated loudness of the master. The bus chain reaches it within 0.3 LU with the true peak at or below −1 dBTP (−2 dBTP for a target louder than −14).
- Bus chain only: `dynamics`, `"glue"` (the default) or `"wide"` for orchestral and organ music. With `"wide"` no bus compresses or saturates, the master keeps only its EQ, and one gain brings the mix to `target_lufs`, unless the true peak would then pass −1.2 dBTP (−2.2 above −14 LUFS): the master then stays quieter instead of being limited, by `mastering.peak_held_db` in the report. The report also gives the loudness range (`lra_lu`) of the mix and of the master.
- Bus chain only: `rules`, 1 or 2. Give a new piece 2; a project without the key keeps 1, so it renders as it did before 0.6. Version 2 sets from the mix itself what the mixing literature gives a rule for ([performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md#the-rules-of-version-2-mixrules-2)): each bus's compressor takes its attack and release from the bus's own crest factor, every bus comes back at the loudness its parts had together (so the parts' `level_dbfs` are the balance the mix has), the reverb returns move together toward the amount measured in engineers' mixes, and the master has one true-peak limiter, with no delay. The report's `buses.rules` and the render's summary say what was measured and set.
- With `rules: 2`, optional `reverb_range_lu`: `[lowest, highest]` (each −40 to −9, in LU), the range the reverb's loudness against the dry mix is brought toward, in place of the default `[-17, -11]`; or `null`, which leaves every send exactly as written. The step is one gain on all the spaces' returns, never more than 6 dB, so a piece written dry stays drier than the range and `analyze` says by how much. A piece that is dry on purpose, its few sends written small by design, names a lower range or `null`; a piece with no reverb send at all has nothing to move.
- With `rules: 2`, optional `c80_db` (−5 to 25), in place of `reverb_range_lu`: how near the players are heard, as the clarity a room would have (C80: the sound within 80 ms of the direct sound over the sound after it, in dB). The spaces' returns move together by the one gain that gives the mix that clarity, however far from where the sends were written. A seat in a concert hall is −2 to +2 dB; this skill's orchestral examples, written with the guide's starting sends, measured +17 dB, which is the players at arm's length. Which to ask for depends on the piece and where it will be heard: [orchestration.md](orchestration.md#how-near-the-orchestra-is-heard). `analyze` reports the mix's C80 whether or not the project asks one.
- With `dynamics: "wide"`, optional `range_lu` (3–30) brings the master's loudness range (EBU Tech 3342) down to that many LU by lifting the quieter passages, slowly and in proportion; passages at the 95th percentile of short-term loudness keep their level. `mastering.range` reports the range before and after and the largest lift. Not with `loop`, whose layers must add up to the master.
- Room chain only: `peak_ceiling_db` (−8 to −1, default −1.6, or −2.6 for a `target_lufs` louder than −14, under the true-peak ceiling of [performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md)), `max_limit_db` (0–4, default 1.5: how far the limiter may reduce peaks; the master may finish below the target instead) and `room_seconds` (0.2–4, default 1.1).

## Game loops

The optional project `loop`, for music that repeats in a game: `{"start_bar": 0}` loops from the start of that bar (zero-based, like sections) to the end of the last bar. Bars before it play once as an intro. The render then also writes:
- `loop.wav`: 24-bit, with the loop points in a `smpl` chunk;
- `loop.ogg`: Vorbis, with `LOOPSTART` and `LOOPLENGTH` comments in samples;
- `loop.json`: the loop points in samples, seconds and bars.

In all three, everything that sounds after the last bar (note releases and reverb, up to `tail_seconds`) is folded back from the loop start. The WAV carries a cue point at every section start, labelled with the section id; `loop.json` lists them too, and with a constant tempo gives `bpm`, `bar_beats` and `beat_count`. `report.json` gains a `loop` entry with its checks and a description of the seam, and `review` asks about the seam. A loop from bar 1 gets no fade-in at the start of the file, because those frames replay on every repeat. See [game-music.md](game-music.md).

Optional `loop.layers`, bus chain only, splits the loop into 2–8 layers for vertical layering: `{"start_bar": 0, "layers": {"base": ["strings", "harp", "bass"], "drive": ["drums", "timpani"], "lead": ["horn"]}}`. Every track belongs to exactly one layer. Each layer is also written as `layers/<id>.wav` and `.ogg`, mixed alone through the same buses and spaces with the master's gain and EQ but not its dynamics, and folded like the loop; together the layers add up to the mix before the master's compressor and limiters.

## Tracks

A track:

```json
{
  "id": "piano",
  "name": "Piano foreground",
  "role": "melody",
  "engine": "soundfont",
  "program": 0,
  "bank": 0,
  "drums": false,
  "range": [48, 90],
  "notes": [
    {"id": "piano_b001_n000", "on": 0, "duration": 1.5, "pitch": 74,
     "phrase": "question", "articulation": "connected_line"}
  ],
  "performance": {
    "notes": {"piano_b001_n000": {"velocity": 82, "timing_ms": 0, "gate": 1.02}},
    "cc": [[0, 64, 0], [0.07, 64, 100], [3.97, 64, 0]]
  },
  "mix": {
    "level_dbfs": -25,
    "level_mode": "active_rms",
    "pan": -0.1,
    "highpass": 100,
    "lowpass": 15000,
    "eq": [[280, -1.5]],
    "reverb_db": -17,
    "delay_db": null,
    "automation": [[0, 0], [32, 0]]
  }
}
```

The example track illustrates fields, not a complete eight-bar composition. `scripts/make_example.py` creates a short working fixture; the music-skill repository's `examples/` holds a complete piece and the script that wrote it.

- `id` is a safe filename token, lower-case letter followed by letters, digits or underscores, maximum 48 characters. Notes have unique stable IDs within a track. Performance keys must exactly match note IDs. Labels are metadata, not executable commands.
- `notes` is the score. `duration` is the intended notation duration. `performance.notes[id]` contains velocity 1–127, onset offset ±100 ms and key-release multiplier `gate` 0.05–1.6. The renderer shares the MIDI-rounded tempo clock; it reports same-key retrigger trimming. It does not alter the score to conceal that trimming.
- `role` is a musical label such as `melody`, `countermelody`, `harmony`, `bass`, `rhythm`, `drums`, `texture`, `accent`, `effect`. Use it for review; no role automatically composes notes.
- `engine` is `soundfont`, `synth`, `surge`, `drum_synth`, `additive` or `noise`. SoundFont programs are MIDI 0–127. Drums use channel 10 (index 9), each drum part on its own port, so two kits keep their programs and controllers apart. Combined MIDI allocates additional ports after 15 melodic parts; DAW support for port metadata varies. Individual part MIDIs avoid that import ambiguity.
- `range` constrains note pitches for this authored part. It does not certify acoustic playability.
- `cc` holds `[beat, controller, value]`. All three engines share the clock; the additive synth supports CC11 expression, CC1 delayed vibrato depth and CC74 brightness. SoundFont CC response depends on the preset. The compiler releases sustain at the end.
- `level_dbfs` is the chosen pre-effect reference level, not a target loudness for each part. `level_mode` is `active_rms` or `peak`. Pan is −1 left to +1 right. `eq` pairs are Hz/dB. `highpass` (default 30 Hz) and `lowpass` (default 18 kHz) are FFmpeg's two-pole Butterworth filters, 12 dB per octave; `review` names a part whose high-pass takes more than 1.5 dB from the fundamental of its lowest note (an octave under that note keeps it within 0.3 dB; under B0, 15 Hz, the lowest `highpass` accepted, keeps A0 within 0.4 dB; [noise.md](noise.md)). Sends are dB relative to the processed part, or `null` to disable.
- Bus chain only: optional `bus` (`drums`, `bass`, `rhythm`, `ensemble`, `lead`, `fx`) overrides the default from the role (drums parts → drums, `bass` → bass, `rhythm` → rhythm, `melody` → lead, `effect` and noise parts → fx, every other role → ensemble). Optional `space` (`room`, `plate`, `hall`, `church`) overrides where `reverb_db` sends: by default the room for drums, bass and rhythm, the plate for the lead and the hall for ensemble and fx. Optional `depth_m` (0–10) stands the part that many metres behind the first desks of a modelled stage: its pan and early reflections from that position replace the plain pan, its energy stays the same, and its reverb send rises by half the distance loss (`stage` in the stem receipt; see [orchestration.md](orchestration.md)). Production tools on a part's processed stem (musiclib/production.py), in this order: `wow_flutter` `{"wow_cents": 0–50, "wow_hz": 0.1–4, "flutter_cents": 0–20, "flutter_hz": 4–25}` (defaults 8, 0.5, 2, 8), tape speed variation that bends the pitch by those peak amounts with no net shift in time (a few cents is a worn machine, 15–25 an obvious warble); `filter_sweep` `{"type": lowpass|highpass, "points": [[beat, Hz], ...], "resonance"}`; `saturate` (0–1, tanh at 4× oversampling, loudness kept); `gate` `{"pattern": "x.x.xx.x", "step_beats": 0.25, "depth_db": 60}` (an excerpt adds `phase_beats`, where the pattern stands at its first beat, so the passage gates as it did in the piece); `delay` `{"beats", "feedback", "send_db", "ping_pong", "highpass", "lowpass"}`, each echo landing that many beats later on the tempo map, to the sample; `duck` `{"by": part id, "keys": [36], "depth_db", "attack_ms", "hold_ms", "release_ms"}`, the part pulled down on each of that part's notes, as a sidechain compressor keyed by the kick does. `effects`: a list of `{"type": "chorus", "depth"}`, `{"type": "phaser", "rate_hz", "depth"}` and `{"type": "bitcrush", "bits", "downsample", "mix"}` (after the EQ: the chorus mixes two voices whose delays sweep 0 to 2 × `depth` and 0 to 2.5 × `depth` ms on sines of 0.25 and 0.35 Hz, read between samples; the phaser and bitcrush are FFmpeg's aphaser, whose `rate_hz` runs from 0.1 to 2 Hz, and acrusher). The receipt reports them under `production`. With `facing: "back"` (needs `depth_m`), for horns, whose bells point away from the hall: the direct sound and the side reflections lose 8 dB above 2 kHz and the stage shell's reflections come back 6 dB stronger, at the same energy.
- Optional `width` (0–1, default 1) scales the side signal before the pan: 0 is mono. Spaced-microphone samples can measure near-zero (in the treble even negative) left/right correlation, so a lead or a high doubling built on them sits diffuse and off-centre and partly cancels in mono; about 0.3–0.5 puts it in front.
- Optional compressor: `{"threshold_db": -24, "ratio": 2.5, "attack_ms": 20, "release_ms": 100}`. It acts after the gain that `level_dbfs` sets, at a fixed threshold, so raising `level_dbfs` on a compressed part mostly deepens its gain reduction: in a boom-bap mix, 1 dB more on drums compressed at 4:1 came out 0.4 dB louder, and on a bass at 3:1 0.3 dB. Raise the threshold with the level, or move the other parts. Optional `delay_quarters` defaults to 0.75, using the initial tempo for this simple effect. Variable-tempo delay retiming is not implemented.
- Automation pairs are beat/dB, strictly increasing, any number of them. The level runs linearly in dB and in time from point to point, applied sample by sample after the pan; before the first point the part keeps the first point's level, after the last the last one's. To hold a level then change it, include a point near the end of the hold; sparse points otherwise create long ramps.

### Render-side sound selection

These fields choose recordings for synthesis only. `composition.mid`, `score.mid` and the part MIDIs keep the authored `program`, `bank`, channel and pitches, so the export stays General-MIDI readable.

- `soundfont`: name of a bank in the configuration's `soundfonts` map (default: the configuration's `soundfont`). SoundFont engine only.
- `patch`: `{"bank": b, "program": p}` used inside that bank instead of `program`/`bank`. A drum part whose patch is not bank 128 renders on a melodic channel (many single-kit banks put the kit at bank 0).
- `key_map`: `{"36": 48, ...}` render-time pitch map (for kits or sample sets that are not GM-mapped). Notes that land on one key keep the louder attack; overlaps are trimmed.
- `reverse: true` (sampled parts) plays each note's recording backwards: the note is rendered alone, held for its written length, and the first `duration` of its sound, attack and ring, is reversed into the span the note sits in, so it swells and stops on the note's end, as a crash cymbal reversed on tape leads into a downbeat. End the note on the beat it leads to; a longer note takes more of the ring. Swing, timing deviation, gate, the part's offset, bends and controllers do not apply; each note renders on its own, at most 64, and the part takes neither `performance.dynamics` nor crossfade legato.
- Banks are addressed as CC0 = bank // 128, CC32 = bank % 128, and FluidSynth runs in MMA bank-select mode, so `bank: 17` really selects bank 17.

### Performance controls

- `performance.cc_mode`: `step` holds each controller value until the next point (default for SoundFont/noise tracks); `linear` ramps continuous controllers between points at 1/32-beat resolution (default for additive). Switch controllers 64–69 always step. Write ramps as breakpoints, not dense lists.
- `performance.offset_ms`: −300…300, moves the whole performed part (MIDI and audio), e.g. −90 for sampled section strings whose recorded attack takes a few hundred milliseconds. Per-note `timing_ms` still applies on top.
- `performance.swing`: `{"ratio": 0.5–0.8, "unit": 0.5}` swings the part: in each pair of `unit` notes (0.5 beat swings eighths, the default; 0.25 sixteenths; 1 quarters) the first takes `ratio` of the pair, so 0.5 is straight, 0.667 a triplet feel and 0.75 dotted. Notes on the start of a pair stay put, and anything inside the pair moves in proportion, ends as well as onsets. Pairs are counted from each bar line, as a drum machine delays the even-numbered steps of the beat (Linn 2013), so every bar swings alike; a bar holding an odd number of units (eighths in 5/8 or 7/8, quarters in 3/4) plays its last one straight. It changes the performance (MIDI and audio), not score exports; `timing_ms` applies on top. Drum machines write the same number as a percentage (66% for a triplet feel). In recorded jazz the ride cymbal's ratio falls as the tempo rises, from about 3.5:1 (0.78) at slow tempi to nearly even eighths around 300 bpm, and soloists swing less than the drummer (Friberg and Sundström 2002), so the parts of one band need not share a ratio.
- `performance.bend`: `[[beat, semitones], ...]` in time order, ramped linearly (two points on one beat make a jump); `performance.bend_range` (1–24, default 2) is the deflection at full wheel, sent as RPN 0 before the part. Bends act on the whole channel, so they suit monophonic parts. SoundFont tracks get pitch-wheel events; the additive engine adds the same curve to its pitch. Score exports (`expressive=False`) leave bends out.
- `performance.legato` (SoundFont tracks): `{"max_interval": 5, "glide_ms": 25, "gap_ms": 40, "max_notes": 4}`. At render time only, notes that follow within `gap_ms`, change pitch and stay within `max_interval` of the group's first note sound as one sample (up to `max_notes` per attack) and the wheel glides to each later pitch over `glide_ms`; authored `bend` adds on top. `bend_range` must cover `max_interval` plus the largest authored bend. Exported MIDI keeps every written note.
- `performance.legato` with `"mode": "crossfade"` (and `crossfade_ms` 20–400, default 100; `gap_ms`, default 40; `max_interval` 1–24, default 12): a single line on a bank with legato presets. A note that follows its neighbour within `gap_ms` and `max_interval`, with no other note sounding, starts from the bank's legato preset (bank 32 + k), past the recording's attack, and the two notes cross over with equal-power curves in `crossfade_ms`; the entering note leaves out `offset_ms`. Chords and repeated notes attack as written. Needs the part's dynamic layers too (below).
- `performance.legato` crossfade options: `portamento_ms` (0–300, default 0) slides a legato note at least three semitones from the one before into its pitch, the wheel starting `portamento_from` (0.2–1, default 0.6) of the way back toward the previous note and easing in over that many milliseconds; a part with its own `bend` keeps those instead.
- `performance.bow_change_s` (2–12, crossfade legato only): a note longer than one and a half times this is cut into even stretches of about that length, each re-entered from the legato preset over `bow_ms` (20–200, default 60), as a string player changes bow on a long note.
- `performance.round_robin` (true or false, pitched SoundFont parts): a note that repeats a pitch within 1.2 s plays the nearest key, up to two semitones away, whose zone holds another recording, with the wheel bringing it back to pitch, so a bank without round robins does not repeat one sample (musiclib/variation.py). Not with pitch bends of the part's own. A drum part steps through the round robins its bank records on neighbouring velocities: each repeated hit takes its layer's next take, a velocity step or two away inside the layer (three where the layer ends; a fraction of a dB), so a groove written at one velocity per piece no longer repeats one sample; set `round_robin` to false on a drum part to keep the written velocities.
- `performance.dynamics`: `[[beat, 0–127], ...]` in time order, linear between points, played on the recorded dynamics of the part's bank (the presets at banks 16 + k on its program; the part's own patch must be bank 0). `review --config` names a part whose preset has none, with the programs that do, and a render stops on it before anything is synthesized. By default (`dynamics_mode` "take", below) each note plays one of them; with `dynamics_mode` "crossfade" the part is rendered once per recorded dynamic and the renders are crossfaded along the curve, equal-power between neighbours in the middle half of the span between them (one take alone near each recorded dynamic) and corrected for their correlation. Either way the level follows the velocity curve of a plain preset (64 sounds about 12 dB under 127). Velocity then changes nothing, and a part with crossfade legato but no curve takes one from its notes' velocities. The stem receipt reports the layers used under `expressive`. Exported MIDI carries the curve as CC1, so the part may not write CC1 itself.
- `performance.dynamics_mode`: `"take"` (the default) or `"crossfade"` (above). In take mode each note plays one recorded dynamic, the one nearest the curve's mean over the note, so two takes of a note never sound together and nothing comb-filters; the curve still moves within the note as level (the layers' own offsets interpolated, so the level depends on the curve alone) and as tone, a blend toward the neighbouring dynamic's spectrum learned from the bank's own layers (1/3-octave envelopes of the same notes at two dynamics, as a linear-phase filter). Crossfades suit a section's long swell, where two takes sounding together read as more players; on a solo they comb-filter. Neighbouring dynamics of one note agree in phase only by chance: over a steady 0.2 s their correlation is near 0 at zero lag in every bank built here, and no shift within 1.5 ms brings the string layers (VSCO sections, the Iowa solos) much past 0.5, though it would bring VSCO's winds and brass to 0.75–0.94. The receipt's `expressive` gives the takes used and the morph's reach.
- MuseScore "Expr." presets (banks 17/18 of MS Basic) are driven by CC2 and are near-silent until CC2 is written; `review` reports a missing CC2.

### Harmony for review

`harmony` may be a list of `{"bar": 1-based, "beat": 0-based quarter offset (optional), "symbol": "F#m7/A", "transpose_semitones": 0}`. It is still documentation for rendering, but `review` uses it to name the notes outside the written chord as embellishing tones and to question those not resolved by step; a chord restated with the same notes continues. Unknown symbols are listed, not guessed. Older free-form harmony objects remain accepted and are not checked.

For `synth` (musiclib/synth.py), `synth` names a preset from [synth-presets.json](synth-presets.json) (`{"preset": "pluck"}`) and/or gives a patch; fields beside a preset replace its own, whole: an `lfo`, `filter` or `amp` you give replaces the preset's object, so copy it and change the value you want. A mono patch joins notes that overlap or touch into one line; after 30 s of unbroken legato the next note starts afresh, which bounds the memory a line takes. A patch: `oscillators` (1–8 of `{"wave": saw|supersaw|square|pulse|triangle|sine|fm|noise, "level", "octave", "semitones", "cents", "voices", "detune_cents", "spread", "width", "pwm": {"rate_hz", "depth"}, "ratio", "index", "index_env"}`), `filter` (`{"type": lowpass|highpass|bandpass, "slope": 12|24, "cutoff_hz", "resonance" 0–1, "env_amount", "key_track", "velocity"}` in octaves), `amp` and `filter_env` (`attack`, `decay`, `sustain`, `release`), `lfo` (`{"rate_hz"` or `"rate_beats", "shape", "to_pitch_cents", "to_cutoff_octaves", "to_amp", "delay", "fade"}`), `mono`, `glide_ms`, `drive` (0–1, at 4× oversampling), `velocity_curve`, `gain_db`. For chip music: a triangle's `steps` (16 gives the NES's 4-bit stepped triangle), a noise oscillator's `clock: true` (noise held for a sixteenth of the note's period, so the note sets its colour: dull around C3, nearly white from C7), and `arpeggio` (`{"rate_hz": 4–120}` or `{"rate_beats"}`, `"order"` up, down or updown): notes starting within 30 ms of each other become one voice stepping through the pitches still held, once per step, the envelope starting once per chord. Oscillators are band-limited (PolyBLEP at four times the sample rate: a sawtooth at C7 keeps its aliases more than 70 dB down), and the filter follows its envelope every sample; a render repeats exactly. Performance `bend` bends the pitch.

For `surge` (musiclib/surge.py), `surge` names a Surge XT patch: a curated one from [surge-presets.json](surge-presets.json) (`{"preset": "juno_60_strings"}`) or any patch of [surge-catalog.json](surge-catalog.json) by its id (`{"preset": "factory/pads/bright"}`; `studio.py sounds` searches the catalog, and a catalog patch falls back by its role), with optional `macros` (`{"1": 0.6}`: the patch's eight macro knobs, 0–1, set before the part plays; what each patch's macros and mod wheel move is in [surge-controls.json](surge-controls.json)) and `fallback` (a built-in synth preset to use where Surge XT is missing; each curated patch names one). Two settings make the patch play what the score controls: `glide_ms` (4–4000) makes it one voice that glides between overlapping notes only, over that many milliseconds, its envelopes not restarting there (Surge's mono single-trigger fingered portamento: the 808's and the 303's slides), and the fallback keeps the slide; `velocity_to_cutoff` (−60 to 60) moves filter 1's cutoff by that many semitones at velocity 127, in proportion below, in place of a velocity routing the patch had there. With both, a note that glides in keeps the velocity of the note the slide starts from (Surge XT's legato does not take the new one), so only a note that attacks is accented: end every note still sounding before an accented one (a long note held under shorter ones counts), and `review` names the slid notes it cannot accent. `swell: true` sets the attacks of the amplitude and filter envelopes to the length of the part's notes, which must share one length (a part per length), so a riser or a reverse cymbal rises over its note and ends on the next beat; Surge's attack stops at 32 s, and the receipt's `played_as` gives the length the patch got (`swell_asked_seconds` beside it where the range cut it). Patches measured to play them are in [surge-roles.json](surge-roles.json); on another patch, hear the result, as a patch may route its filter or pitch elsewhere. A patch's tempo-synced Delay times and LFO rates follow one tempo per part, the written tempo most of its notes start under, before rubato, so a pickup or a ritardando does not set it (the plugin gets no tempo of its own here, so the skill sets them for it); a tempo change inside the part does not move them. Surge XT and pedalboard are separate GPL-3.0 programs that bootstrap.py installs unless `--no-surge`; they play the part in their own process. Notes, sustain (CC64), mod wheel (CC1) and pitch bend reach the patch; a patch built to sound an octave below the key it is played on is played an octave up, so the written pitch sounds (`transpose` in the preset list). Surge starts its oscillators at random phases, so a part's take differs from one synthesis to the next while it sounds the same: the render cache keeps a take until the part changes. Patches carry their own effects, some with tails past a second (`tail_s` in the preset list): give the project a `tail_seconds` long enough for them. Without Surge XT, pedalboard or the patch file, the part plays its fallback on the built-in synth, and its stem receipt (`surge`) and `analyze` say so.

For `drum_synth` (musiclib/drums.py), drum-machine voices on General MIDI keys: 35/36 kick, 37 rim, 38/40 snare, 39 clap, 42 closed, 44 pedal and 46 open hi-hat (a closed or pedal hat chokes the open one), 41/43/45/47/48/50 toms, 49 crash, 51 ride, 56 cowbell, 70 shaker. Optional `kit`: `{"style": "808"|"909", "tune": semitones, "decay": factor, "gated_ms": 100–600, "gated_db": −18–0}`; `gated_ms` puts the 1980s gated reverb behind snares and claps, a dense stereo room held that long and cut in 15 ms, `gated_db` (default −6) below the hit. Set `"drums": true` so MIDI exports use channel 10.

For `additive`, optional `instrument` parameters: `harmonics` (default 12), `spectral_tilt` (1.25), `detune_cents` ([-5,0,5]), `attack_ms` (22), `release_ms` (120), `glide_ms` (28), `cutoff_hz` (1900), `odd_only` (false), and optionally `vibrato_depth_cents` (depth at CC1 = 127; without it about 14 cents at full CC1), `vibrato_rate_hz` (5.1) and `vibrato_delay_ms` (180). A single detune voice is centred. Keep monophonic lead roles; polyphonic pad emulation is outside this instrument's design. For `noise`, a note's `articulation` selects `rise`, `impact`, `vinyl` or a decaying noise transition; pitch is retained for MIDI editing but is not acoustic pitch in the noise engine. A `vinyl` note plays a worn record's surface noise for its whole length (hiss, a crackle of clicks, occasional pops, a scratch once a revolution); its clicks stand far above its hiss, so give the part `level_mode: "peak"` and set `level_dbfs` for the loudest click, around −30 to −22.

Optional `harmony`, `decisions`, `authorship` and `revision_history` retain compositional intent. The renderer never infers or enforces harmony; `review` only questions notes against it. You must author the notes.

Optional `style` places the piece on the style maps ([style-maps.md](style-maps.md)): an entry, `"style": "family/sub-style"`, or a primary entry with entries borrowed for some axes, `"style": {"primary": "family/sub-style", "borrow": {"rhythm": "family/other"}}`. The axes are `tempo`, `harmony`, `melody`, `rhythm`, `timbre`, `form` and `mix`; each is governed by the entry borrowed for it, otherwise by the primary one. `validate` refuses an entry the maps do not have. `review` takes each check's limits from the entry governing the check's axis, and its record `limits` gives every limit with its origin: a source, or `ours` where the project set the number itself ([style-maps.json](style-maps.json)). The renderer does not read `style`.

## Revision and reproducibility

Change the score, performance or mix in a new JSON revision. Keep note IDs when the musical event survives. `revise` offers a narrow pitch/velocity/duration edit; complex edits can be made directly in JSON and validated. A render snapshot plus the exact runtime hashes identifies a result. Cross-platform or different binary versions may not produce identical samples. Raw synthesis cache keys include clock, note/performance/instrument settings, SoundFont/FluidSynth hashes and synthesis code. Mix changes intentionally reuse those raw stems.
