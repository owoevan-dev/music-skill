# Project schema: `music-production/v1`

All `on`, `duration`, CC times, tempo positions and mix automation positions use **quarter-note beats**, including compound meter. Bars in `sections.start_bar` are zero-based. Pitch and program are zero-based MIDI integers. Every count and MIDI value (bars, pitch, velocity, program, bank, CC values) is a JSON integer: `100`, not `100.0`, which validation refuses. Section ids are text of 1-64 characters and note ids of 1-120; neither may hold a control character (a tab too), a line or paragraph separator or a bidirectional formatting character, since both are printed in tables and messages. `validate` also lists, as warnings, any field it does not know with the nearest known name: a misspelt optional field would otherwise do nothing. The export uses 960 ticks per quarter.

## The project

```json
{
  "schema": "music-production/v1",
  "title": "Original piece",
  "version": "1.0.r1",
  "brief": "Musical direction and intended foreground",
  "clock": {
    "bars": 8,
    "meter": [4, 4],
    "tempo": [[0, 108]],
    "tail_seconds": 5
  },
  "sections": [{"id": "theme", "start_bar": 0, "bars": 8, "intent": "Question and answer"}],
  "tracks": [],
  "mix": {"chain": "bus", "rules": 2, "target_lufs": -14}
}
```

`clock.tempo` is a step map of [beat, quarter-note BPM] points starting at beat 0. Optional `clock.rubato` shapes it as played: `{"final_ratio": 0.6, "final_beats": 4, "phrase_ratio": 0.94, "phrase_beats": 1, "q": 2}`. The last `final_beats` slow to `final_ratio` of the written tempo along Friberg and Sundberg's (1999) final-ritardando curve (q = 2 a steady braking force, 3 softer), and the last `phrase_beats` before each section boundary ease to `phrase_ratio` and come back at its downbeat. A tempo point written inside either changes the tempo that is slowed, and the slowing goes on. Optional `spans`, `[[from_beat, to_beat, ratio], ...]`, lay further slowings along the same curve, each easing to `ratio` at `to_beat` and back at it; `excerpt.py` writes them so that a passage keeps the piece's ritardandi, which may begin before its first beat or end after its last. MIDI export, audio and every measurement share the played map; the project keeps the written one.

`clock.tail_seconds` is the time kept after the last bar for note releases and reverb (1 to 15; a master that still sounds at its end fails a full render's `tail_quiet`, and a draft says how much longer the tail would have to be: [outputs.md](outputs.md)). The render's files and `validate`'s `seconds` include it, so a piece asked for at a length is that long in its bars, and its files run the tail longer; a game loop folds the tail back onto its start.

`tracks` must contain 1–64 entries. Sections must cover the entire form without gaps. The fixed meter denominator may be 2, 4, 8 or 16. Tempo must begin at beat zero, be strictly increasing in time and stay within 30–300 quarter-note BPM. For 6/8 with dotted-quarter pulse 66, use quarter-note BPM 99. The bounds are 512 bars, 500,000 notes and 30 minutes; these are resource limits, not compositional targets.

## The mix

The project `mix`:
- Bus chain only: `headphones: true` also writes `headphones.wav` and `headphones.mp3`: the same piece with every part at a direction around the listener's head, for headphones ([performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md#a-headphone-version-mixheadphones)). The master and the stems are the files they are without it (the report gains `headphones`). `{"reflections": false}` in its place leaves out the room's first reflections; the reverb is the parts' own sends, as in the master, so a part without `reverb_db` has the reflections and no more. A part stands where its `pan` puts it (a pan of 1 is 70 degrees to the right) and follows its `move`; a part's `mix.around` places it anywhere around the head. Not for a looped piece. The buses' saturation and `mono_below_hz` are left out of the headphone version, which the report's `headphones.left_out` names; a loud master's soft clip gives both ears one gain.
- Bus chain only: `loudness: "loud"` for dense electronic genres: a soft clipper computed at four times the rate rounds peaks before the delivery limiter and `target_lufs` may go up to −8 (not with `dynamics: "wide"`). The report's `mastering.limiting_db` gives, per section, how much level clipper and limiter took, and `analyze` warns past 3 dB. `mono_below_hz` (40–250) makes the premix mono below that frequency (a zero-phase high-pass on the side channel, 48 dB per octave). `wow_flutter` (below) wobbles the whole premix as a worn tape would, before `mono_below_hz`.
- `chain`: `"bus"` mixes through buses, four generated spaces, a master chain and a delivery limiter; `"room"` (the default, kept for older projects) sends every part dry into one shared room and limits once. See [performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md).
- `target_lufs` (−30 to −10, default −16): integrated loudness of the master. The bus chain reaches it within 0.3 LU with the true peak at or below −1 dBTP (−2 dBTP for a target louder than −14).
- Bus chain only: `dynamics`, `"glue"` (the default) or `"wide"` for orchestral and organ music. With `"wide"` no bus compresses or saturates, the master keeps only its EQ, and one gain brings the mix to `target_lufs`, unless the true peak would then pass −1.2 dBTP (−2.2 above −14 LUFS): the master then stays quieter instead of being limited, by `mastering.peak_held_db` in the report. The report also gives the loudness range (`lra_lu`) of the mix and of the master.
- Bus chain only: `rules`, 1 or 2. Give a new piece 2; a project without the key keeps 1, so it is mixed as it was before 0.6 (its drum parts take their recorded takes another way: `performance.round_robin`, below). Version 2 changes four things, none from a measurement of the piece's audio ([performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md#the-rules-of-version-2-mixrules-2)): the lead bus's compressor takes its threshold from the lead's written level, so a quiet lead is compressed as deep as a loud one and the bus stays near the sum of its parts (within 0.4 dB on 20 of this skill's 21 test projects that compress it); the drum, bass and rhythm buses saturate at four times the sample rate; the master has one true-peak limiter, with no delay (a `"wide"` master has none: its one gain holds the peak); and the piece may ask how near it is heard (`c80_db`, next). The report's `buses.rules` and the render's summary say what was set.
- With `rules: 2`, optional `c80_db` (−5 to 25): the clarity of the mix (C80: in the response the mix gives its parts, the energy within 80 ms of the direct sound over the energy after it, in dB, at mid frequencies). The spaces' returns move together by the one gain that gives the mix that clarity, however far from where the sends were written (30 dB at most). It is worked out from the project file and the spaces' impulse responses: each part counts by its `level_dbfs`, the time its notes sound and its send. That time is the time the part's renderer holds a note: a slur counts as the one note it sounds as, a mono synth line to the end of the note that owns the voice, a drum hit dropped by `key_map` not at all, and a part played backwards for its written spans. Some part must send to a reverb (`reverb_db`), or the project is refused. Barron (2013) recommends −2 to +2 dB for concert halls; a fughetta for harpsichord and strings written with the orchestral guide's starting sends has +16 dB, far on the clear side. The number compares the balance of early and late sound with a hall's and says nothing else of a hall. Which to ask for depends on the piece and where it will be heard: [orchestration.md](orchestration.md#how-near-the-orchestra-is-heard). `analyze` reports the mix's C80 whether or not the project asks one.
- With `dynamics: "wide"`, optional `range_lu` (3–30) brings the master's loudness range (EBU Tech 3342) down to that many LU by lifting the quieter passages, slowly and in proportion; passages at the 95th percentile of short-term loudness keep their level. `mastering.range` reports the range before and after and the largest lift. Not with `loop`, whose layers must add up to the master.
- Room chain only: `peak_ceiling_db` (−8 to −1, default −1.6, or −2.6 for a `target_lufs` louder than −14, under the true-peak ceiling of [performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md)), `max_limit_db` (0–4, default 1.5: how far the limiter may reduce peaks; the master may finish below the target instead) and `room_seconds` (0.2–4, default 1.1).

## Game loops

The optional project `loop`, for music that repeats in a game: `{"start_bar": 0}` loops from the start of that bar (zero-based, like sections) to the end of the last bar. Bars before it play once as an intro. The render then also writes:
- `loop.wav`: 24-bit, with the loop points in a `smpl` chunk;
- `loop.ogg`: Vorbis, with `LOOPSTART` and `LOOPLENGTH` comments in samples;
- `loop.json`: the loop points in samples, seconds and bars.

In all three, everything that sounds after the last bar (note releases and reverb, up to `tail_seconds`) is folded back from the loop start. The WAV carries a cue point at every section start, labelled with the section id; `loop.json` lists them too, and with a constant tempo gives `bpm`, `bar_beats` and `beat_count`. `report.json` gains a `loop` entry with its checks and a description of the seam, and `review` asks about the seam. A loop from bar 1 gets no fade-in at the start of the file, because those frames replay on every repeat. See [game-music.md](game-music.md).

Optional `loop.layers`, bus chain only, splits the loop into 2–8 layers for vertical layering: `{"start_bar": 0, "layers": {"base": ["strings", "harp", "bass"], "drive": ["drums", "timpani"], "lead": ["horn"]}}`. Every track belongs to exactly one layer. Each layer is also written as `layers/<id>.wav` and `.ogg`, mixed alone through the same buses and spaces with the master's gain and EQ but not its dynamics, and folded like the loop; together the layers add up to the mix before the master's compressor and limiters.

## Tracks

A track:

```json
{
  "id": "piano",
  "name": "Piano foreground",
  "role": "melody",
  "engine": "soundfont",
  "program": 0,
  "bank": 0,
  "drums": false,
  "range": [48, 90],
  "notes": [
    {"id": "piano_b001_n000", "on": 0, "duration": 1.5, "pitch": 74,
     "phrase": "question", "articulation": "connected_line"}
  ],
  "performance": {
    "notes": {"piano_b001_n000": {"velocity": 82, "timing_ms": 0, "gate": 1.02}},
    "cc": [[0, 64, 0], [0.07, 64, 100], [3.97, 64, 0]]
  },
  "mix": {
    "level_dbfs": -25,
    "level_mode": "active_rms",
    "pan": -0.1,
    "highpass": 100,
    "lowpass": 15000,
    "eq": [[280, -1.5]],
    "reverb_db": -17,
    "delay_db": null,
    "automation": [[0, 0], [32, 0]]
  }
}
```

The example track illustrates fields, not a complete eight-bar composition. `studio.py start` writes into a piece's folder (made if missing; its brief and words may be there): a `compose.py` that builds the project with `musiclib.builder` (notes by bar and beat, ids and performance entries made with them) and the valid `song.json` it writes; the music-skill repository's `examples/` holds a complete piece and the script that wrote it.

- `id` is a safe filename token, lower-case letter followed by letters, digits or underscores, maximum 48 characters. Notes have unique stable IDs within a track. Performance keys must exactly match note IDs. Labels are metadata, not executable commands.
- `notes` is the score. `duration` is the intended notation duration. `performance.notes[id]` contains velocity 1–127, onset offset ±100 ms and key-release multiplier `gate` 0.05–1.6. The renderer shares the MIDI-rounded tempo clock; it reports same-key retrigger trimming. It does not alter the score to conceal that trimming.
- `role` is a musical label such as `melody`, `countermelody`, `harmony`, `bass`, `rhythm`, `drums`, `texture`, `accent`, `effect`. Use it for review; no role automatically composes notes.
- `engine` is `soundfont`, `synth`, `surge`, `drum_synth`, `additive` or `noise`. SoundFont programs are MIDI 0–127. Drums use channel 10 (index 9), each drum part on its own port, so two kits keep their programs and controllers apart. Combined MIDI allocates additional ports after 15 melodic parts; DAW support for port metadata varies. Individual part MIDIs avoid that import ambiguity.
- `range` constrains note pitches for this authored part. It does not certify acoustic playability.
- Optional `strings` plays a sampled part by string, as a guitar or a bass is played: the open pitches of its four to eight strings as MIDI numbers, from low to high (a guitar `[40, 45, 50, 55, 59, 64]`, drop D `[38, 45, 50, 55, 59, 64]`, a bass `[28, 33, 38, 43]`). Every note of the part then names its `string`, counted as players count: 1 is the highest. A note must lie on its string, from the open string to 24 frets above it, and a string cannot be struck twice at once; a note without its string is refused with the strings that have its pitch. A note is written no longer than to its string's next note (a longer one is refused with the length to write; the builder's `fret` and `shape` shorten it), so the written lengths are what sounds, while the other strings ring on. As played, a note that its gate or the next stroke's timing carries past that stroke ends at it; the exported MIDI, the piano roll, `review` and an excerpt show it ending there too. Two notes cannot be played on one string on the same tick. One pitch may be held on two strings when the two notes are written two ticks or more apart (a strum's timing alone does not do it); written closer they are one key twice, which is refused. Held on two strings a pitch rings on both; the part's MIDI file, which holds a key once at a time, ends the first where the second begins. A pedal (controllers 64, 66, 69) and a `key_map`, an empty one too, are refused on such a part. In a loop a note is also ended by the loop's notes on its string a loop later. A note may be `"mute": "palm"` (damped at the bridge: shorter and duller) or `"mute": "dead"` (stopped by the fretting hand: a click of little pitch). `musiclib.expression`'s `strum` then goes by string, and the builder writes frets: `part.fret(bar, beat, string, fret, beats)` and `part.shape(bar, beat, [(string, fret), ...], beats)` on a part made with `strings=[...]`. Not with `performance.legato`, `round_robin` or `dynamics`, nor on a reversed part. The part's entry in `stem_receipts.json` records `strung`: the passes synthesized, the strings used, how many notes were open, palm-muted or dead, `ended_by_next_stroke` (how many were still sounding, above −80 dBFS, when their string's next note ended them), and `tail_seconds`, the time after a note's key is released for which its pass is kept to itself: the preset's longest release as the SoundFont gives it plus 0.05 s. `tail_assumed` is there where that is not known: 3 s where the release and its 0.05 s come to more, 1 s where the SoundFont makes the release depend on a controller; a voice may then sound on into the next note of its pass. A quick line on one string takes more passes, and so more time, the longer the tail is ([rock-and-metal.md](rock-and-metal.md#the-direct-recording-through-an-amplifier-mixamp)).
- `cc` holds `[beat, controller, value]`. All three engines share the clock; the additive synth supports CC11 expression, CC1 delayed vibrato depth and CC74 brightness. SoundFont CC response depends on the preset. The compiler releases sustain at the end.
- `level_dbfs` is the chosen pre-effect reference level, not a target loudness for each part. It is reached from how the part plays, so softening a part's loudest notes raises all the others (cellos came up about 5 dB in one piece): to change one passage, use `automation`. `level_mode` is `active_rms` or `peak`. Pan is −1 left to +1 right. `eq` is a list of bands, applied in order: a pair `[Hz, dB]` is a peak of width Q 1 (20–20,000 Hz, ±12 dB), FFmpeg's `equalizer`; an object (0.11) is `{"type": "peak"|"low_shelf"|"high_shelf", "hz", "db", "q"}` (20–20,000 Hz, −24 to +12 dB, Q 0.1–20, default 1; FFmpeg's `equalizer`, `lowshelf` and `highshelf`) or `{"type": "notch", "hz", "q"}` (FFmpeg's `bandreject`, which removes its band and takes no gain: a partial cut is a peak with a negative `db`). `highpass` (default 30 Hz) and `lowpass` (default 18 kHz) are FFmpeg's two-pole Butterworth filters, 12 dB per octave; `review` names a part whose high-pass takes more than 1.5 dB from the fundamental of its lowest note (an octave under that note keeps it within 0.3 dB; under B0, 15 Hz, the lowest `highpass` accepted, keeps A0 within 0.4 dB; [noise.md](noise.md)). Sends are dB relative to the processed part, or `null` to disable.
- Bus chain only: optional `bus` (`drums`, `bass`, `rhythm`, `ensemble`, `lead`, `fx`) overrides the default from the role (drums parts → drums, `bass` → bass, `rhythm` → rhythm, `melody` → lead, `effect` and noise parts → fx, every other role → ensemble). Optional `space` (`room`, `plate`, `hall`, `church`) overrides where `reverb_db` sends: by default the room for drums, bass and rhythm, the plate for the lead and the hall for ensemble and fx. Optional `depth_m` (0–10) stands the part that many metres behind the first desks of a modelled stage: its pan and early reflections from that position replace the plain pan, its energy stays the same, and its reverb send rises by half the distance loss (`stage` in the stem receipt; see [orchestration.md](orchestration.md)). Production tools on a part's processed stem (musiclib/production.py), in this order: `wow_flutter` `{"wow_cents": 0–50, "wow_hz": 0.1–4, "flutter_cents": 0–20, "flutter_hz": 4–25}` (defaults 8, 0.5, 2, 8), tape speed variation that bends the pitch by those peak amounts with no net shift in time (a few cents is a worn machine, 15–25 an obvious warble); `filter_sweep` `{"type": lowpass|highpass, "points": [[beat, Hz], ...], "resonance"}`; `saturate` (0–1, tanh at 4× oversampling, loudness kept; from 0.11 also `{"drive": 0–1, "curve": "atan"|"alg"|"tanh"|"erf"}`, on the same path, drive 0.5 and tanh by default, so `{}` saturates: four rounded curves from the softest knee, `atan`, through `alg` and `tanh` to the firmest, `erf`; a drive of 0 is no saturation in either form); `gate` `{"pattern": "x.x.xx.x", "step_beats": 0.25, "depth_db": 60}` (an excerpt adds `phase_beats`, where the pattern stands at its first beat, so the passage gates as it did in the piece); `delay` `{"beats", "feedback", "send_db", "ping_pong", "highpass", "lowpass"}`, each echo landing that many beats later on the tempo map, to the sample; `duck` `{"by": part id, "keys": [36], "depth_db", "attack_ms", "hold_ms", "release_ms"}`, the part pulled down on each of that part's notes, as a sidechain compressor keyed by the kick does. `effects`: a list of at most 8, applied in the order listed, of `{"type": "chorus", "depth"}`, `{"type": "phaser", "rate_hz", "depth"}` (`depth` 0–1), `{"type": "bitcrush", "bits", "downsample", "mix"}`, `{"type": "tremolo", "rate_hz", "depth"}` (0.1–20 Hz, depth 0–1; defaults 5 and 0.5) and `{"type": "flanger", "delay_ms", "depth_ms", "rate_hz", "feedback", "mix"}` (0–30 ms, 0–10 ms, 0.1–10 Hz, −0.95 to 0.95, 0–1; defaults 0, 2, 0.5, 0 and 0.71), each type with its own limits (after the EQ: the chorus mixes two voices whose delays sweep 0 to 2 × `depth` and 0 to 2.5 × `depth` ms on sines of 0.25 and 0.35 Hz, read between samples; the phaser, bitcrush, tremolo and flanger are FFmpeg's aphaser, whose `rate_hz` runs from 0.1 to 2 Hz, acrusher, tremolo, and flanger with quadratic interpolation, whose `mix` is the share of the delayed signal: at 1 the dry half stays). The receipt reports the production tools under `production`; the EQ, the effects, the compressor and the gate are in its `filter_chain` as they run (the chorus as `chorus(...)`, the skill's own, between FFmpeg's passes). `noise_gate` (0.11) `{"threshold_db": −80–0, "range_db": −80–0, "attack_ms": 0.01–1000, "release_ms": 1–5000}` (defaults −50, −40, 2, 120) is FFmpeg's agate at a ratio of 10, first among the part's processors (after its level and its high- and low-pass, before the EQ, the effects and the compressor, where gates go: White 1997, 2002, in [processing.md](processing.md)): measured on held tones by their RMS level, as the gate reads them, one 2 dB under the threshold loses about 5 dB, one 8 dB or more under it falls to `range_db`, the floor; `{}` is the gate with these defaults. `surge_fx` (0.11): a chain of one to four of Surge XT's own effects after the compressor, before the width and the static pan, `[{"type": "chorus", "params": {"modulation_rate": 0.6, "output_mix": 35}}, ...]`, each `type` one of the nineteen in [surge-fx.json](surge-fx.json) with its parameters' units, limits and defaults (every parameter is set on each render, what the project gives or the default), or `airwindows` with an `algorithm`, one of the 75 by its Airwindows name, and that algorithm's knobs: `{"type": "airwindows", "algorithm": "ToTape6", "params": {"input_gain": 3}}` (`studio.py effects --describe airwindows` lists them). After the chain a 5 Hz first-order high-pass removes any DC offset, and the part is brought back to the level it entered the chain at, so `level_dbfs` stays its level; the stem receipt's `surge_fx` gives each parameter as the plugin showed it, `level_change_db`, what the chain had done to the level, and `repeat`. A setting the table marks (`varies_when`: Tape's degrade, the combulator's extra noise, the spring reverb's chaos and knock, the flanger's and phaser's noise waveforms) renders otherwise each time the part is processed anew; its output is kept with the part, so the headphone version and later renders play the same take until the part's audio into the chain or the chain changes. Surge XT Effects is a separate GPL-3.0 program that bootstrap.py installs with Surge XT; without it the part plays without its chain, and the receipt, `report.json` (`surge_fx`) and the render's summary say so. How to choose and set them: [processing.md](processing.md). With `facing: "back"` (needs `depth_m`), for horns, whose bells point away from the hall: the direct sound and the side reflections lose 8 dB above 2 kHz and the stage shell's reflections come back 6 dB stronger, at the same energy.
- Optional `width` (0–1, default 1) scales the side signal before the pan: 0 is mono. Spaced-microphone samples can measure near-zero (in the treble even negative) left/right correlation, so a lead or a high doubling built on them sits diffuse and off-centre and partly cancels in mono; about 0.3–0.5 puts it in front.
- Optional compressor: `{"threshold_db": -24, "ratio": 2.5, "attack_ms": 20, "release_ms": 100}` (−50 to −3 dB, 1–12, 0.1–200 ms, 5–3000 ms), FFmpeg's acompressor, with, from 0.11, optional `knee_db` (0–18: the knee's width; FFmpeg's default is about 9 dB), `makeup_db` (0–36), `mix` (0–1: below 1 the dry signal is mixed back, parallel compression) and `detection` (`"rms"`, the default, or `"peak"`). It acts after the gain that `level_dbfs` sets, at a fixed threshold, so raising `level_dbfs` on a compressed part mostly deepens its gain reduction: in a boom-bap mix, 1 dB more on drums compressed at 4:1 came out 0.4 dB louder, and on a bass at 3:1 0.3 dB. Raise the threshold with the level, or move the other parts. Optional `delay_quarters` defaults to 0.75, using the initial tempo for this simple effect. Variable-tempo delay retiming is not implemented.
- Automation pairs are beat/dB, strictly increasing, any number of them. The level runs linearly in dB and in time from point to point, applied sample by sample after the pan (before the movement of a part with `move`); before the first point the part keeps the first point's level, after the last the last one's. To hold a level then change it, include a point near the end of the hold; sparse points otherwise create long ramps.
- Optional `around` places the part in the headphone version (the project's `mix.headphones`; refused without it). Directions are degrees clockwise seen from above: 0 ahead, 90 to the right, 180 behind, -90 or 270 to the left. A number (-360 to 360) is a part that stands there. `{"type": "path", "points": [[beat, degrees], ...]}` moves it straight in time from point to point, two or more points on rising beats, standing at the first point before it and at the last after it; degrees run on past 360, so `[[0, 0], [16, 360]]` is once round to the right in 16 beats and `[[0, 0], [16, -720]]` twice round to the left. `{"type": "turn", "beats": 16}` turns it once every 16 beats for the whole piece, from where its pan puts it (or `"from": degrees`), to the right unless `"direction": "left"`; `phase_beats` starts it that far into a turn. The ordinary master does not read `around`: the part keeps its `pan` there. The report's `headphones.parts` gives each part's `points` (1 for a part whose two channels are the same signal, 3 for any other: what they share at its place, what differs spread either side, with `side_db`, the difference's level against the shared part, null where nothing is shared), `by` (`pan`, `pan and move`, `still`, `path` or `turn`) and the range of its directions, which run on past 360 where a part turns.
- Optional `move` moves the part across the stereo image; a place is a pan value, −1 to +1. `{"type": "path", "points": [[beat, pan], ...]}` gives absolute places, two or more points on strictly increasing beats: the beats run from 0 to the end of the last bar; the place runs straight in time from point to point, the first point's place before it and the last one's after it (so a part comes to rest at its last point), and the part's `pan` is not also written (a jump is two points close together). `{"type": "swing", "beats": 4, "width": 0.5}` goes to and fro around the part's `pan`: `width` (0.01–1) is the farthest to each side and `pan` plus or minus it must stay within −1 and +1; `beats` (0.25–1024) is the period under the piece's tempo, counted from beat 0; optional `shape` is `"sine"` (the default) or `"triangle"`; optional `phase_beats` (0 to `beats`) shifts it: at 0 the part stands at `pan` at beat 0 and moves right. A moving part is one point: its two channels are folded to one, then the two gains of its place are applied at every sample (level only, no delay between the sides). So `move` is not written with `width` or `depth_m`. In a loop, a part with a note reaching the loop's end and a note starting at its start as played (`loop.start_bar`, after an intro; a note's timing, gate and the part's offset counted) must stand in the same place at both, within 0.05 (a swing whose period divides the loop, or a path that returns to the place it has at the loop's start); otherwise the project is refused. The part's entry in `stem_receipts.json` records `move`: `fold_loss_db` (−120 where the fold lost all of the part) and, where the part sounds as it reaches the movement (before its gate, duck and other production tools), `lowest`, `highest`, `fastest_pan_per_second` and `mono_dip_db`; for a swing, sounding or not, also `period_beats` and `period_seconds`, the seconds of its first period and of its last under the tempo as played ([performance-and-mix.md](performance-and-mix.md#a-part-that-moves-mixmove)).
- Optional `amp` plays the part through a guitar or bass amplifier and loudspeaker: `{"voice": "crunch", "drive": 0.5}`. `voice` is `clean`, `edge`, `crunch`, `lead` or `high_gain` for a guitar and `bass` for a bass guitar (the guitar voices lower a bass's low notes by tens of decibels); `drive` (0–1, default 0.5) moves the first stage's gain over 24 dB. It runs first, on the part's whole dry synthesis folded to one channel, so the notes sounding together pass one set of curves; its output is then brought to `level_dbfs` and goes through the rest of the part's mix. It is made for a bank recorded from a pickup (`fsbs_direct`), whose level into the amplifier is measured, so that a louder velocity drives it harder; the levels of three bass banks are measured too (`bass_pick` 0:0, `bass_finger` 0:0, `bass_darkblack` 0:33). A measured level belongs to that preset of the bank, known by its file where a renderer configuration is given (the same file under another name has it) and by its name in `review` without one; the receipt's `amp.bank_measured` says whether the render used it. On another bank write `input_db` (−40 to 40) once the receipt's `amp.input_peak` is known: near 1 for the hardest stroke (a guitar's full chord, a bass's single note) is the working level; `review` asks about an `amp` without a measured level or an `input_db`. The part's entry in `stem_receipts.json` records `amp`: `voice`, `drive`, `input_gain_db`, `bank_measured`, `input_peak` and `fold_loss_db` ([rock-and-metal.md](rock-and-metal.md#the-direct-recording-through-an-amplifier-mixamp)).

### Render-side sound selection

These fields choose recordings for synthesis only. `composition.mid`, `score.mid` and the part MIDIs keep the authored `program`, `bank`, channel and pitches, so the export stays General-MIDI readable.

- `soundfont`: name of a bank in the configuration's `soundfonts` map (default: the configuration's `soundfont`). SoundFont engine only.
- `patch`: `{"bank": b, "program": p}` used inside that bank instead of `program`/`bank`. A drum part whose patch is not bank 128 renders on a melodic channel (many single-kit banks put the kit at bank 0).
- `key_map`: `{"36": 48, ...}` render-time pitch map (for kits or sample sets that are not GM-mapped), each key a MIDI pitch written without leading zeros. Notes that land on one key keep the louder attack; overlaps are trimmed.
- `reverse: true` (sampled parts) plays each note's recording backwards: the note is rendered alone, held for its written length, and the first `duration` of its sound, attack and ring, is reversed into the span the note sits in, so it swells and stops on the note's end, as a crash cymbal reversed on tape leads into a downbeat. End the note on the beat it leads to; a longer note takes more of the ring. A sound that does not decay (an organ, a held pad) has no ring to reverse and stays level: write its swell with `mix.automation`. Swing, timing deviation, gate, the part's offset, bends and controllers do not apply; each note renders on its own, at most 64, and the part takes neither `performance.dynamics` nor crossfade legato.
- Banks are addressed as CC0 = bank // 128, CC32 = bank % 128, and FluidSynth runs in MMA bank-select mode, so `bank: 17` really selects bank 17.

### Performance controls

- `performance.cc_mode`: `step` holds each controller value until the next point (default for SoundFont/noise tracks); `linear` ramps continuous controllers between points at 1/32-beat resolution (default for additive). Switch controllers 64–69 always step. Write ramps as breakpoints, not dense lists.
- `performance.offset_ms`: −300…300, moves the whole performed part (MIDI and audio), e.g. −90 for sampled section strings whose recorded attack takes a few hundred milliseconds. Per-note `timing_ms` still applies on top.
- `performance.swing`: `{"ratio": 0.5–0.8, "unit": 0.5}` swings the part: in each pair of `unit` notes (0.5 beat swings eighths, the default; 0.25 sixteenths; 1 quarters) the first takes `ratio` of the pair, so 0.5 is straight, 0.667 a triplet feel and 0.75 dotted. Notes on the start of a pair stay put, and anything inside the pair moves in proportion, ends as well as onsets. Pairs are counted from each bar line, as a drum machine delays the even-numbered steps of the beat (Linn 2013), so every bar swings alike; a bar holding an odd number of units (eighths in 5/8 or 7/8, quarters in 3/4) plays its last one straight. It changes the performance (MIDI and audio), not score exports; `timing_ms` applies on top. Drum machines write the same number as a percentage (66% for a triplet feel). In recorded jazz the ride cymbal's ratio falls as the tempo rises, from about 3.5:1 (0.78) at slow tempi to nearly even eighths around 300 bpm, and soloists swing less than the drummer (Friberg and Sundström 2002), so the parts of one band need not share a ratio.
- `performance.bend`: `[[beat, semitones], ...]` in time order, ramped linearly (two points on one beat make a jump); `performance.bend_range` (1–24, default 2) is the deflection at full wheel, sent as RPN 0 before the part. Bends act on the whole channel, so they suit monophonic parts. SoundFont tracks get pitch-wheel events; the additive engine adds the same curve to its pitch. Score exports (`expressive=False`) leave bends out.
- `performance.legato` (SoundFont tracks): `{"max_interval": 5, "glide_ms": 25, "gap_ms": 40, "max_notes": 4}`, with a `performance.bend_range` of at least `max_interval` plus the largest written bend (the default of 2 is refused here: write `"bend_range": 5`). At render time only, notes that follow within `gap_ms`, change pitch and stay within `max_interval` of the group's first note sound as one sample (up to `max_notes` per attack) and the wheel glides to each later pitch over `glide_ms`; authored `bend` adds on top (the two curves are summed; the glide arrives at a joined note's pitch at its onset, so a bend written from there is measured from that note: a bend from two semitones below into an E4 slurred from C4 starts on D4, and one written earlier, while the glide is still on its way, lies lower by what the glide has left to go). `bend_range` must cover `max_interval` plus the largest authored bend. Exported MIDI keeps every written note.
- `performance.legato` with `"mode": "crossfade"` (and `crossfade_ms` 20–400, default 100; `gap_ms`, default 40; `max_interval` 1–24, default 12, at most 12 with `portamento_ms`): a single line on a bank with legato presets. A note that follows its neighbour within `gap_ms` and `max_interval`, with no other note sounding, starts from the bank's legato preset (bank 32 + k), past the recording's attack, and the two notes cross over with equal-power curves in `crossfade_ms`; the entering note leaves out `offset_ms`. Chords and repeated notes attack as written. Needs the part's dynamic layers too (below).
- `performance.legato` crossfade options: `portamento_ms` (0–300, default 0) slides a legato note at least three semitones from the one before into its pitch, the wheel starting `portamento_from` (0.2–1, default 0.6) of the way back toward the previous note and easing in over that many milliseconds; a part with its own `bend` keeps those instead.
- `performance.bow_change_s` (2–12, crossfade legato only): a note longer than one and a half times this is cut into even stretches of about that length, each re-entered from the legato preset over `bow_ms` (20–200, default 60), as a string player changes bow on a long note.
- `performance.round_robin` (true or false, pitched SoundFont parts): a note that repeats a pitch within 1.2 s plays the nearest key, up to two semitones away, whose zone holds another recording, with the wheel bringing it back to pitch, so a bank without round robins does not repeat one sample (musiclib/variation.py). Not with pitch bends of the part's own. A drum part varies the takes its bank records on neighbouring velocities: each hit takes one of its layer's takes by a draw of its own (from the hit's id, the key it plays and where it falls in the piece, together: the same on every render, and another draw when the hit is moved or its key changed), at the nearest velocity inside the layer that holds that take (a step or two away where a layer has up to four takes, at most one fewer than its takes), so a groove written at one velocity per piece does not play one sample throughout. A hit has the take of the hit before it on that drum and layer about one time in N, where the layer has N takes (a chance, not a count). Until 0.6 a drum stepped through its takes in order, so a drum part of an older project now plays other takes of the same hits. The velocities written in the project and in the exported MIDI stay as they are; only the MIDI given to the synthesizer carries the moved ones. Set `round_robin` to false on a drum part to play the written velocities.
- `performance.dynamics`: `[[beat, 0–127], ...]` in time order, linear between points, played on the recorded dynamics of the part's bank (the presets at banks 16 + k on its program; the part's own patch must be bank 0). The recordings are spaced evenly from 0 to 127, so a preset of two plays its loud take from 64 up. `review --config` names a part whose preset has none, with the programs that do, and a render stops on it before anything is synthesized. By default (`dynamics_mode` "take", below) each note plays one of them; with `dynamics_mode` "crossfade" the part is rendered once per recorded dynamic and the renders are crossfaded along the curve, equal-power between neighbours in the middle half of the span between them (one take alone near each recorded dynamic) and corrected for their correlation. Either way the level follows the velocity curve of a plain preset (64 sounds about 12 dB under 127). Velocity then changes nothing, and a part with crossfade legato but no curve takes one from its notes' velocities. The stem receipt reports the layers used under `expressive`. Exported MIDI carries the curve as CC1, so the part may not write CC1 itself.
- `performance.dynamics_mode`: `"take"` (the default) or `"crossfade"` (above). In take mode each note plays one recorded dynamic, the one nearest the curve's mean over the note, so two takes of a note never sound together and nothing comb-filters; the curve still moves within the note as level (the layers' own offsets interpolated, so the level depends on the curve alone) and as tone, a blend toward the neighbouring dynamic's spectrum learned from the bank's own layers (1/3-octave envelopes of the same notes at two dynamics, as a linear-phase filter). Crossfades suit a section's long swell, where two takes sounding together read as more players; on a solo they comb-filter. Neighbouring dynamics of one note agree in phase only by chance: over a steady 0.2 s their correlation is near 0 at zero lag in every bank built here, and no shift within 1.5 ms brings the string layers (VSCO sections, the Iowa solos) much past 0.5, though it would bring VSCO's winds and brass to 0.75–0.94. The receipt's `expressive` gives the takes used and the morph's reach.
- MuseScore "Expr." presets (banks 17/18 of MS Basic) are driven by CC2 and are near-silent until CC2 is written; `review` reports a missing CC2.

### Harmony for review

`harmony` may be a list of `{"bar": 1-based, "beat": 0-based quarter offset (optional), "symbol": "F#m7/A", "transpose_semitones": 0}` (the builder's `song.harmony(bar, beat, symbol)` writes it with beats counted from 1, as notes are). It is still documentation for rendering, but `review` uses it to name the notes outside the written chord as embellishing tones and to question those not resolved by step; a chord restated with the same notes continues. Unknown symbols are listed, not guessed. Older free-form harmony objects remain accepted and are not checked.

For `synth` (musiclib/synth.py), `synth` names a preset from [synth-presets.json](synth-presets.json) (`{"preset": "pluck"}`) and/or gives a patch; fields beside a preset replace its own, whole: an `lfo`, `filter` or `amp` you give replaces the preset's object, so copy it and change the value you want. A mono patch joins notes that overlap or touch into one line; after 30 s of unbroken legato the next note starts afresh, which bounds the memory a line takes. A patch: `oscillators` (1–8 of `{"wave": saw|supersaw|square|pulse|triangle|sine|fm|noise, "level", "octave", "semitones", "cents", "voices", "detune_cents", "spread", "width", "pwm": {"rate_hz", "depth"}, "ratio", "index", "index_env"}`), `filter` (`{"type": lowpass|highpass|bandpass, "slope": 12|24, "cutoff_hz", "resonance" 0–1, "env_amount", "key_track", "velocity"}` in octaves), `amp` and `filter_env` (`attack`, `decay`, `sustain`, `release`), `lfo` (`{"rate_hz"` or `"rate_beats", "shape", "to_pitch_cents", "to_cutoff_octaves", "to_amp", "delay", "fade"}`), `mono`, `glide_ms`, `drive` (0–1, at 4× oversampling), `velocity_curve`, `gain_db`. For chip music: a triangle's `steps` (16 gives the NES's 4-bit stepped triangle), a noise oscillator's `clock: true` (noise held for a sixteenth of the note's period, so the note sets its colour: dull around C3, nearly white from C7), and `arpeggio` (`{"rate_hz": 4–120}` or `{"rate_beats"}`, `"order"` up, down or updown): notes starting within 30 ms of each other become one voice stepping through the pitches still held, once per step, the envelope starting once per chord. Oscillators are band-limited (PolyBLEP at four times the sample rate: a sawtooth at C7 keeps its aliases more than 70 dB down), and the filter follows its envelope every sample; a render repeats exactly. Performance `bend` bends the pitch.

For `surge` (musiclib/surge.py), `surge` names a Surge XT patch: a curated one from [surge-presets.json](surge-presets.json) (`{"preset": "juno_60_strings"}`) or any patch of [surge-catalog.json](surge-catalog.json) by its id (`{"preset": "factory/pads/bright"}`; `studio.py sounds` searches the catalog, and a catalog patch falls back by its role; `review` reads the part as the patch it names, so one the catalog measured without a clear pitch stays out of its pitch checks also where the fallback plays), with optional `macros` (`{"1": 0.6}`: the patch's eight macro knobs, 0–1, set before the part plays; what each patch's macros and mod wheel move is in [surge-controls.json](surge-controls.json)) and `fallback` (a built-in synth preset to use where Surge XT is missing; each curated patch names one). Two settings make the patch play what the score controls: `glide_ms` (4–4000) makes it one voice that glides between overlapping notes only, over that many milliseconds, its envelopes not restarting there (Surge's mono single-trigger fingered portamento: the 808's and the 303's slides), and the fallback keeps the slide; `velocity_to_cutoff` (−60 to 60) moves filter 1's cutoff by that many semitones at velocity 127, in proportion below, in place of a velocity routing the patch had there. With both, a note that glides in keeps the velocity of the note the slide starts from (Surge XT's legato does not take the new one), so only a note that attacks is accented: end every note still sounding before an accented one (a long note held under shorter ones counts), and `review` names the slid notes it cannot accent. `swell: true` sets the attacks of the amplitude and filter envelopes to the length of the part's notes, which must share one length (a part per length), so a riser or a reverse cymbal rises over its note and ends on the next beat; Surge's attack stops at 32 s, and the receipt's `played_as` gives the length the patch got (`swell_asked_seconds` beside it where the range cut it). Patches measured to play them are in [surge-roles.json](surge-roles.json); on another patch, hear the result, as a patch may route its filter or pitch elsewhere. A patch's tempo-synced Delay times and LFO rates follow one tempo per part, the written tempo most of its notes start under, before rubato, so a pickup or a ritardando does not set it (the plugin gets no tempo of its own here, so the skill sets them for it); a tempo change inside the part does not move them. Surge XT and pedalboard are separate GPL-3.0 programs that bootstrap.py installs unless `--no-surge`; they play the part in their own process. Notes, sustain (CC64), mod wheel (CC1) and pitch bend reach the patch; a patch built to sound an octave below the key it is played on is played an octave up, so the written pitch sounds (`transpose` in the preset list). Surge starts its oscillators at random phases, so a part's take differs from one synthesis to the next while it sounds the same: the render cache keeps a take until the part changes. Patches carry their own effects, some with tails past a second (`tail_s` in the preset list): give the project a `tail_seconds` long enough for them. Without Surge XT, pedalboard or the patch file, the part plays its fallback on the built-in synth, and its stem receipt (`surge`) and `analyze` say so.

For `drum_synth` (musiclib/drums.py), drum-machine voices on General MIDI keys: 35/36 kick, 37 rim, 38/40 snare, 39 clap, 42 closed, 44 pedal and 46 open hi-hat (a closed or pedal hat chokes the open one), 41/43/45/47/48/50 toms, 49 crash, 51 ride, 56 cowbell, 70 shaker. Optional `kit`: `{"style": "808"|"909", "tune": semitones, "decay": factor, "gated_ms": 100–600, "gated_db": −18–0}`; `gated_ms` puts the 1980s gated reverb behind snares and claps, a dense stereo room held that long and cut in 15 ms, `gated_db` (default −6) below the hit. Set `"drums": true` so MIDI exports use channel 10.

For `additive`, optional `instrument` parameters: `harmonics` (default 12), `spectral_tilt` (1.25), `detune_cents` ([-5,0,5]), `attack_ms` (22), `release_ms` (120), `glide_ms` (28), `cutoff_hz` (1900), `odd_only` (false), and optionally `vibrato_depth_cents` (depth at CC1 = 127; without it about 14 cents at full CC1), `vibrato_rate_hz` (5.1) and `vibrato_delay_ms` (180). A single detune voice is centred. Keep monophonic lead roles; polyphonic pad emulation is outside this instrument's design. For `noise`, a note's `articulation` selects `rise`, `impact`, `vinyl` or a decaying noise transition; pitch is retained for MIDI editing but is not acoustic pitch in the noise engine. A `vinyl` note plays a worn record's surface noise for its whole length (hiss, a crackle of clicks, occasional pops, a scratch once a revolution); its clicks stand far above its hiss, so give the part `level_mode: "peak"` and set `level_dbfs` for the loudest click, around −30 to −22.

Optional `harmony`, `decisions`, `authorship` and `revision_history` retain compositional intent. The renderer never infers or enforces harmony; `review` only questions notes against it. You must author the notes. A decision that begins with a review question's identifier and a colon (`"stiffness:keys: a sequenced arpeggio, meant"`; the identifiers are in the review's summary and in `questions` of score_review.json) answers that question: `review` lists it under `answered` with the reason and does not ask it again for as long as the question keeps that identifier.

Optional `association` keeps what the piece started from beside the request (SKILL.md, step 2):

```json
"association": {
  "source": "draw", "seed": 548970676,
  "words": ["scarlet", "stapler", "sewer", "sauna", "screwdriver", "motel", "mesa", "whittle", "leek", "crimp", "lemur", "tram"],
  "whole": "A night tram crosses the mesa to a motel whose scarlet sign has gone out ...",
  "gives": [
    {"level": "form", "what": "seven scenes in the order of the journey", "where": "all sections"},
    {"level": "rhythm", "what": "the tram's wheels, 3+3+2", "where": "tram, bars 9-28"},
    {"level": "harmony", "what": "the scarlet chord, B-flat over D", "where": "bars 5-6, 29-44, 95"}
  ]
}
```

`source`, `seed` and `words` are copied from the `association.json` that `studio.py words` wrote in the project folder (`source` is `draw`, or `user` for words the user gave). `whole` is the one scene, story or chain that holds the request and every word. `gives` says what the whole decided, level by level, and where it is heard. A piece whose user asked for no association records `"association": {"none": "<why>"}`. `validate` checks the frame only. `review` asks once for a project without the field, for words without their whole, and for a whole that gave the music fewer than three levels (`association.missing`, `association.whole`, `association.gives`); nothing refuses a project for it, and nothing checks that the whole is a good one: that is for its reader.

Optional `plan` is the piece's plan as data, written before the notes and kept:

```json
"plan": {
  "identity": "what makes this piece itself, in your words",
  "high_point": {"section": "top", "means": "by what it is the high point"},
  "sections": [
    {"section": "verse", "sounds": ["drums", "bass", "keys", "lead"], "leads": "lead"},
    {"section": "chorus", "sounds": ["drums", "bass", "keys", "lead", "brass"], "leads": "lead", "answers": ["brass"],
     "register": {"lead": "higher"}, "harmony": "faster", "dynamics": "louder",
     "differs": "what sets it apart from the section before, in your words"}
  ]
}
```

Every field is optional and a section may be left out: the plan holds what you commit to and nothing else, and it has no form to fill. `sounds` is all the parts that sound in the section, attacked there or held into it: a list of some of them reads as a difference, so leave it out if you commit to only a few. Parts the plan names and the project does not have yet are asked about once, in one question for the whole plan (`plan.unwritten:<the parts>`), so a score reviewed before its other parts are written shows its real differences; a decision on it covers those parts and no other. `leads` and each part of `answers` attack in it. `register` gives a part `higher`, `lower` or `same` against the last section it played in (its median pitch there and here; 2 semitones count). `harmony` is `faster`, `slower` or `same` against the section before (chord changes a bar, as the review's glance counts them). `dynamics` is `louder`, `softer` or `same` against the section before (the mean written velocity of every note begun in the section, all its parts together; 3 counts; levels and automation of the mix are not read). A passage cut from the piece with `excerpt.py` keeps this plan whole and adds `passage` (`first_bar` and `bars`, in the piece's bars): the plan is read against the piece, and the review of a passage asks nothing of it. `identity` and `differs` are your words and are compared with nothing; the high point's `means` are shown and judged by nothing, since a high point need not be more parts or more notes. `validate` checks the plan's frame only: it may name a part or a section the score does not have yet. `review` reads the score against each commitment and asks where they differ (`plan:<section>`), with the figures; the score or the plan may be the one to change. `round` keeps the plan as first written (`plan_first` in round.json) and says where a later plan no longer says the same, so a plan rewritten to fit the score is seen as that. A project without a plan is asked for one (`plan.missing`); a decision can answer that for a piece that needs none.

Optional `style` places the piece on the style maps ([style-maps.md](style-maps.md)): an entry, `"style": "family/sub-style"`, or a primary entry with entries borrowed for some axes, `"style": {"primary": "family/sub-style", "borrow": {"rhythm": "family/other"}}`. The axes are `tempo`, `harmony`, `melody`, `rhythm`, `timbre`, `form` and `mix`; each is governed by the entry borrowed for it, otherwise by the primary one. `validate` refuses an entry the maps do not have. `review` takes each check's limits from the entry governing the check's axis, and its record `limits` gives every limit with its origin: a source, or `ours` where the project set the number itself ([style-maps.json](style-maps.json)). The renderer does not read `style`.

## Revision and reproducibility

Change the score, performance or mix in a new JSON revision. Keep note IDs when the musical event survives. `revise` offers a narrow pitch/velocity/duration edit; complex edits can be made directly in JSON and validated. A render snapshot plus the exact runtime hashes identifies a result. Cross-platform or different binary versions may not produce identical samples. Raw synthesis cache keys include clock, note/performance/instrument settings, SoundFont/FluidSynth hashes and synthesis code. Mix changes intentionally reuse those raw stems.
