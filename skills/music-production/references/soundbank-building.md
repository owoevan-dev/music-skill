# Sound banks: building a bank from a sample set

Read this to build an SF2 bank from recordings with `soundbank.py`: the spec, cutting notes, loops, levels, dynamic layers and legato, noise reduction and click repair. The banks already built are in [soundbanks.md](soundbanks.md).

`soundbank.py build` reads a JSON spec (the ones in `references/soundbanks/` are worked examples) and writes an SF2 plus a receipt with every source file's hash and every decision. A spec has `name`, `comment`, `copyright`, `target_rms` (0.08) and `presets`; a preset has `name`, `program`, optional `bank`, and `layers`, each layer becoming one instrument.

| Layer field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `dir`, `pattern` | Folder and a regex over file names with named groups `note` (C4, F#2), `dyn` and `rr`; `.wav`, `.flac` (through `--ffmpeg`) and uncompressed `.aif`/`.aiff` files are read. |
| `mics` | Instead of `dir`: microphones of the same takes, `[{"dir", "gain", "pan", "name"}]`, the same file name in each folder or, with `name` (`["MicA", "MicB"]`), one that differs by the microphone's tag. They are summed sample-aligned; a mono one is placed with a constant-power `pan` (−1 to 1). |
| `dynamics`, `velocity` | The `dyn` values used, loudest last, and the velocity range each serves. A note missing a dynamic borrows the nearest one. |
| `keys`, `octave_offset`, `root` | Playable key range; a shift for libraries that number octaves differently; a fixed root key for unpitched layers with no `note` in the name. |
| `pitches` | For takes named by position rather than pitch: a map from the regex group `id` to a measured MIDI pitch with cents (`{"1C": 39.95}`). The root is the nearest key and the rest becomes fine tune; positions left out are not used. |
| `pitch_check`, `fmin`, `fmax`, `yin_threshold`, `max_correction_cents` | Pitch measured against the name: a whole-octave median error is fixed, smaller ones become fineTune. Turn it off for drums. |
| `fold_octaves` | A single take read an octave or two from its name, give or take a few cents (the tracker locked onto a harmonic or a subharmonic), counts as the named note that many cents out. |
| `denoise` | `{"reduce_db": 12, "keep_partials_below": 300}`: the steady hiss and rumble of the recordings reduced by up to that much, by a short-time Wiener gain with a decision-directed SNR estimate (Ephraim and Malah 1984; Scalart and Filho 1996); the gain is shared by both channels and never goes under the limit, so a note keeps its level and tone and the residual noise does not flicker. With `keep_partials_below` (Hz) each take's own partials under that frequency stay as recorded (the bins within the window's main lobe or 3 % of each harmonic of its measured pitch): a soft low note can be no louder than a recording's rumble, where the gain alone would thin it. The noise each take is measured against: for notes `split` cut, their recording's own, stored in the manifest (frames away from every note, the margins narrowing for recordings with short pauses, skipping a publisher's gated digital silence, leaving out frames louder than the quiet ones such as a breath or a last ring, each band the median of its bins so a hum line or a partial does not count); a recording with no usable pause borrows the median of its layer's recordings at the same dynamic; other takes use their quiet frames pooled per dynamic. The receipt names each take's source (`noise_measured_from`). For takes with real noise between their notes; not for breath or bow noise that belongs to the sound, nor for cymbals and tam-tams, whose sound is itself noise-like. Takes recorded one note per file have no pause to measure: their quiet frames are the takes' own last ring wherever a take was cut before its note died away, and a gain measured on them takes from soft attacks (on a soft VSCO violin take, 2.9 dB under 300 Hz and 4.2 dB over 4 kHz in the first 80 ms); find plain noise in such takes first. [noise.md](noise.md) gives every bank's measured noise: none calls for more reduction. |
| `soft_from` | `{"2": "4"}`: each take of the soft dynamic (here `2`) is made from the take of the louder one (`4`) on the same key: both takes' partials are read over their first 0.3 s, the louder take is given the soft blow's level partial by partial with one zero-phase gain, the trend continued above the last partial that stands 12 dB over the soft take's own noise, and the result is brought to the soft take's level. After the hammer leaves the string, string and soundboard ring as a linear system, and a harder blow gives the upper partials more (Hall and Askenfelt 1988; Askenfelt and Jansson 1993), so the soft note is, nearly, the loud note of that key filtered; it then carries the loud take's noise, lowered, in place of its own. For a soft layer recorded little over the room and the microphones. A key with fewer than four clear partials borrows the curve of the nearest key that has them; with none, or no louder take, it stays as recorded. The receipt gives each take's `soft_from`: the take it was made from, the partials read, the gain at the first and the last. What it cannot carry over: the sound of the blow itself, whose thump against the tone is stronger in a soft blow, and the soft take's own decay. Not for looped layers. The build stores samples only: no filter and no impulse response is kept or applied at playback |
| `close_hiss` | `{}` (or `{"from_hz": 1000, "settled_db": 6, "not_before_s": 0.06}`): each half-octave band of a take from `from_hz` up is taken out from the moment it has come down to the recording's hiss, never sooner than 0.06 s after the onset (`not_before_s`: the top of a blow or a pluck lasts less than the 0.1 s the levels are read over, and a band read as settled from the first frame would be closed through it), with a fade of 0.03 to 0.3 s. A struck string's upper partials die first, so a take's bands reach the hiss from the top down, and a soft take never sounds above a few kHz; what those bands hold afterwards is hiss, which a held pedal adds up over every ringing note. A band's gain moves once and never back, so nothing rises and falls with the playing, as it did under the gain of `denoise`, which follows the signal. A band is read against the top band, which holds hiss alone, so a publisher's slow fade does not pass for a decay; a band that does not hold still against it holds partials and stays as recorded, as does a take under 2 s, and a take whose peak or level the closing would lower by more than 0.5 dB (a take that begins with a knock and sounds its note later reads as settled in between). Two things pass those tests that are partials: one that dies slowly, under a decibel a second, holds as still against the top band as hiss does; and where the top band holds partials of its own that fall as the others do (an FM voice), a band read against it is settled from the first frame. So the take is also read for narrow components, over its later part as a whole and before it in windows of a quarter second: a bin that stands 12 dB over the hiss floor of its half octave (the floor of the take's later part) and whose power is within 10 dB of all that half octave's hiss is a tone heard in that hiss. A band is held by every such tone it would take more than 3% of the amplitude of, which may be a tone of the band above or below, since a band's filter reaches into its neighbours. A tone heard in the later part keeps the band as recorded; one heard only earlier moves the closing to after the last window that holds it (`after_a_partial` in the receipt). A steady tone of the recording itself (a whine of the equipment) keeps its band as recorded in the same way: left alone, with the hiss around it taken out, it would be heard more than before. What is left: the hiss of a band while its partials still sound, of a band that holds such a tone, and the first moments of a note. A tone more than 10 dB under its half octave's hiss goes with the hiss. The receipt gives each take's `closed_bands` |
| `full_scale` | `true`: each take is stored as loud as 16 bits hold and attenuated as much in its zone, so the level heard is the same and the dither's floor, a fixed 93 dB under full scale, lies that much lower under the note (`stored_lift_db` in the receipt). It moves the floor by what the take's own noise leaves room for: on the Osiris piano, takes stored 14.5 dB louder gave a soft pedalled passage 2.5 dB less floor, since the recording's hiss, not the dither, is most of it. The file holds 57.6 dB of attenuation as heard: a build whose zone would need more (a take raised 36 dB under an `attenuation_db` of 40) stops and says so. A take that a `full_scale` layer and a plain one share is stored once for each |
| `declick` | `{}` (or the options of `find_clicks`): mechanical clicks in a take that rings on, such as the knock of a key let go under the sustain pedal, found and repaired after the onset trim, before the ring cut is measured. A click is broadband and stands alone: in its frames (1024 samples every 128) at least 40 % of the bins from 2 to 12 kHz rise more than 6 dB over their level 50–120 ms either side, its 3–12 kHz band stands 6 dB over the frames 20–110 ms either side, nothing else within 0.25 s rises within 4 dB of it, and it lasts at most 0.15 s. A low string's own pulse, once a period, and a soft take's flickering hammer noise rise again and again, so they stay as recorded. In the frames that reach into a click, each bin is held to its level interpolated between the four frames just before and just after it, plus 1.5 dB, with its phase kept: a ringing partial holds its level across the click and passes, the click's added noise does not, and samples outside those frames stay bit for bit. The receipt lists each repair (`declicked_ms`: start, end and the 3–12 kHz rise in dB, from the onset). It is for takes recorded one note per file: a second strike's hammer noise can read as a click too, and its new note's first frames would be lowered, so on recordings holding several notes check the receipt's spans first. Clicks are read on the average of the channels, so one recorded in opposite polarity on the two can be missed. |
| `keys: "auto"`, `key_margin` | The key range from the notes found, after the octave check, plus `key_margin` (2) semitones each side. |
| `pitch_source` | `"split"`: take each note's pitch from the `split.json` beside notes cut by `soundbank.py split`, measured over the note's whole steady part, instead of a 0.6 s excerpt. |
| `borrowed_level` | `"served"`: a note missing a dynamic borrows the nearest louder one at the level `layer_levels_db` gives the dynamic it serves (a zone attenuation), not at its own. |
| `level`, `match_notes`, `match_limit_db`, `layer_levels_db`, `gain` | `"peak"` matches percussive layers by peak instead of early RMS; notes are pulled toward their layer's median unless `match_notes` is false, or with `"local"` toward the median of the note and its two nearest sampled neighbours each side, which evens out uneven takes but keeps the register's slope, or with `"across"` every dynamic and round robin of a note to one level on a register slope the dynamics share (the median of each one's local level against its own median), so a velocity split changes the tone and not the level, which the velocity curve sets; `match_limit_db` caps the pull (default 6 dB; more also raises a soft take's noise); `layer_levels_db` sets recorded dynamics relative to the loudest. |
| `loop`, `loop_window`, `loop_repair` | `"recorded"` plays each take's own sustain loop from its WAV `smpl` chunk, so a held note lasts as long as the key. `"found"` cuts a loop from a take recorded without one, inside `loop_window` (seconds after the onset, default `[0.8, 600]`): the longest stretch clear of noises (frames more than `loop_repair.noise_db`, default 4 dB, over the median of their own half second; raise it for tremolo), at least 0.8 s. `loop_repair` (`{"sag_db": 1.5, "crossfade": 0.12}`) moves a loop end that sags back to where the level is steady, matches it in phase to the loop start and crossfades into it; found loops always get it. `period_search: true` compares at least one period of the note (low brass) and searches the end over the last 0.3 s of the steady part; `match_seconds` lengthens the comparison (0.12 lines up a vibrato), `min_loop` lowers the 0.8 s minimum, and a take too short for its window loops from earlier, never inside its first 0.15 s. |
| `dynamic_layers`, `legato` (preset fields) | `{"bank": 16}` adds one velocity-deaf preset per recorded dynamic at that bank and up; `{"bank": 32, "attack": 0.01}` adds legato versions of those, each zone starting past its take's attack (`startAddrsOffset`) and fading in over `attack` seconds. The takes are shared with the main preset, so the bank grows only by zones. |
| `vibrato` | `{"rate_hz", "depth_cents", "delay_seconds"}` for takes recorded straight: the synthesizer's vibrato LFO, starting after the delay so short notes stay straight. The mod wheel (CC1) adds depth. |
| `rr_block`, `round_robins` | Round robins alternate in blocks of this many neighbouring velocities (default 4; 1 for kits). |
| `onset_db`, `max_seconds`, `ring_db`, `envelope`, `attenuation_db` | Silence trim threshold, a cut with a 50 ms fade, a per-take cut where the ring last reaches `ring_db` under its own peak (smoothed over 50 ms; `max_seconds` still caps it), `attack`/`hold`/`decay`/`release` in seconds, `sustain_db`, `filter_hz`. Kits use a long release (100 s) so a short note does not cut a cymbal. |
| `exclusive_class` | Zones that cut each other, such as closed, pedal and open hi-hat. |
| `velocity_attenuation_cb`, `velocity_curve`, `velocity_filter_cents` | Replace the synthesizer's velocity curves when recorded layers already carry the dynamics. The SoundFont 2.04 defaults are 960 cB of attenuation on a concave curve and −2400 cents of filter cutoff. FluidSynth, which renders these banks, computes its own form of the filter default as zero, so velocity closes no filter in a render here; `velocity_filter_cents: 0` keeps a bank the same in players that apply the 2.04 default, and a negative amount closes the filter on soft notes, on a straight line over the whole velocity range (measured with FluidSynth 2.6.1: −2400 took 13 dB above 12 kHz at velocity 48 and nothing at 127). With `velocity_curve: "linear"` the attenuation falls on a straight line in dB, the same step per velocity value; 0 cB makes velocity change nothing, as on an organ. |

A preset with `"level_reference": "preset"` measures one reference over all its layers, so a kit keeps the recorded balance between kick, snare and cymbals; if the loudest hit would clip, the whole kit comes down together.

Recordings of notes played one at a time, one dynamic per file, are cut into notes first: a spec's `split` section lists each source file with its dynamic and the run it holds (`"notes": ["C4", "B4"]`), and optionally its `string` with the group's preferred `strings` ranges, `partial` where a file may lack notes, `whole` for a file that is one note (`pitch: "spectrum"` checks struck metal by its strongest partial), `out` to name an unpitched one. A group's `name`, a file's `dyn` and its `out` become part of each note file's name, so each is one name of letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-` (POSIX's portable filename characters; no path), and a layer's `dir` stays under `--root`. `soundbank.py split <spec> --root <sources> --output <notes>` writes one WAV per note and `split.json`, which records every cut, its pitch and its hash; `soundbank.py cut <split.json> --root <sources> --output <notes>` cuts the same notes again and checks each hash, so an install reproduces the intake exactly. Then build from the notes folder.

Before writing a part around a new bank, render a short probe (velocity sweep, the figure you intend, the register you need), measure attack, range and level, and get a human audition of a dry reel. A bank that renders without errors is not yet shown to be good.

FluidSynth applies a zone's attenuation (SF2 generator 48) at 0.4 of its centibels, as the EMU8000 did, so `soundbank.py` writes 2.5 times the centibels meant to be heard (a dynamic borrowed from a louder take 12 dB down is written as 300), and `musiclib/layered.py` reads it the same way; the loop search sizes its window from each recording's own pitch. Seven banks were rebuilt for these on 3 November: `tuba_war` b2, `vsco_colors` b2, `vcsl_winds` b2, `iowa_brass` b2, `iowa_winds` b4, `iowa_strings` b4 and `iowa_perc` b3.
